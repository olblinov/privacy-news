# Summary

The President of the Office for Personal Data Protection (Prezes UODO) in Poland imposed administrative fines on A. (Panek SA) and X. (ITCenter) following a data breach that exposed the personal data of over 21,000 individuals during a website migration. The breach occurred because old website files containing a historical database were mistakenly copied to an unhidden folder and indexed by a search robot. Prezes UODO found that A., as the data administrator, failed to implement adequate technical and organizational security measures, conduct proper risk analysis, or sufficiently oversee its data processor, X.. X., the data processor, was also found to have neglected its security obligations, primarily due to a lack of awareness about the personal data contained on the website and insufficient communication with A.. Consequently, A. was fined 1,527,855.00 PLN, and X. was fined 20,037.00 PLN.

# Details

## Background

The data breach occurred on April 17, 2020, and was reported to Prezes UODO by A. (Panek SA) on April 24, 2020. The incident involved copying files from an old website to a new folder that should have been hidden but was inadvertently made accessible, leading to its indexing by a robot (Z.). This exposed a historical database containing personal data of approximately **21,453 clients and employees** of A.. The compromised data included **names, surnames, residential addresses, PESEL numbers, dates of birth, email addresses, phone numbers, and encrypted website login passwords**. #fine 

A. had contracted X. (ITCenter) for IT services, including website migration and ongoing IT infrastructure support, under an agreement dated July 31, 2019, which also included a separate data processing agreement. A. attributed the incident to a configuration error by X.'s employee, stating that A. was unaware of this specific action. Conversely, X. argued that it was not provided with crucial information from A., such as the website's functionality (e.g., handling reservations) or that its database contained personal data, and therefore, it treated the website as informational. X. also stated that the data processing agreement did not specifically list the website as a system for personal data processing. X. claimed it informed A. that the old website's software was outdated and needed updates, which were not part of their service agreement.

Prezes UODO initiated administrative proceedings on April 27, 2020, to investigate potential violations by A. and later included X. as a party, expanding the scope of the inquiry multiple times. Both A. and X. submitted explanations, often contradicting each other regarding responsibility for the lack of security measures.

## Legal Analysis

Prezes UODO, as the supervisory authority for data protection in Poland, based its decision on the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) (Regulation 2016/679) and the Polish Personal Data Protection Act.

**For A. (Administrator), the following GDPR violations were identified:**

- **Article 5(1)(f) (Confidentiality and Integrity)** and **Article 5(2) (Accountability)**: A. failed to process data in a manner that ensured appropriate security, leading to unauthorized access and a breach of confidentiality [3b, 4, 61, 64, 112]. A. also failed to demonstrate compliance with these principles [3b, 61, 112].
- **Article 24(1) (Responsibility of the Controller)**: A. did not implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and demonstrate that processing was performed in accordance with GDPR [2, 3a, 62, 112].
- **Article 25(1) (Data Protection by Design and by Default)**: A. failed to implement adequate technical and organizational measures to effectively implement data protection principles and safeguard data subjects' rights, both when determining the means of processing and during processing itself [2, 3a, 62, 97, 112].
- **Article 28(1) (Processor Selection)**: A. did not ensure that its chosen processor, X., provided sufficient guarantees for implementing appropriate technical and organizational measures to comply with GDPR requirements and protect data subjects' rights [2, 3c, 87, 97]. A. also failed in its ongoing duty to verify these guarantees.
- **Article 32(1) and (2) (Security of Processing)**: A. failed to implement security measures appropriate to the risks, particularly concerning pseudonymsation, encryption, ensuring confidentiality, integrity, availability, and resilience of systems, and regularly testing, measuring, and evaluating the effectiveness of security measures [2, 3a, 3b, 4, 63, 111].

**Key findings regarding A.'s failures:**

- **Lack of Risk Analysis:** A. admitted to not conducting a risk analysis for the website migration, claiming it was X.'s obligation as per their agreement. Prezes UODO clarified that while a processor might assist, the administrator remains primarily responsible for conducting risk assessments.
- **Insufficient Oversight:** A. did not supervise X.'s implementation of changes or verify if security standards and the data processing agreement were being followed, despite having the contractual right to conduct audits.
- **Poor Communication:** A. did not sufficiently inform X. that the website handled personal data or that its database stored sensitive client information, leading to X. treating it as an informational site.
- **Inadequate Contracts:** The data processing agreement was too general and did not precisely define the scope of data entrusted or X.'s specific responsibilities regarding the website's data processing, making it difficult to establish clear areas of responsibility.
- **Failure to Regularly Test:** A. outsourced this entirely to X. and did not independently verify the effectiveness of security measures, which is a fundamental obligation of the administrator.

**For X. (Processor), the following GDPR violations were identified:**

- **Article 32(1) and (2) (Security of Processing)**: X. failed to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure the security and confidentiality of personal data.
- **Article 32(1) and (2) in conjunction with Article 28(3)(c) and (f)**: X. did not adopt all required security measures (Article 32) and did not adequately assist A. in meeting its Article 32 obligations, partly due to its lack of awareness of the data's content and failure to encrypt the database.

**Key findings regarding X.'s failures:**

- **Lack of Due Diligence:** Despite its professional status and long-term cooperation with A., X. failed to clarify the content of the website's database (which was not encrypted) and assumed it was only an informational site, not undertaking sufficient security measures for personal data.
- **Inadequate Communication with Administrator:** X. did not effectively communicate its need for information about the website's data processing nature or explicitly refuse the task without such information, which is expected of a professional entity.
- **Failure to Ensure Confidentiality:** X. did not encrypt the database, believing it was unnecessary for an "informational" website, leading to a breach of confidentiality.

Prezes UODO emphasized that data protection by design and default, including risk analysis and continuous security monitoring, is a fundamental and ongoing responsibility of the administrator, which cannot be entirely delegated to a processor.

## Sanction

Prezes UODO imposed administrative fines on both A. and X. based on Article 58(2)(i) and Article 83 of the GDPR, taking into account the specific circumstances of the case. The maximum fines for the violated articles range up to €10,000,000 or 2% of annual worldwide turnover, and up to €20,000,000 or 4% of annual worldwide turnover, whichever is higher. The total fine for multiple violations within the same or related processing operations does not exceed the amount for the most serious violation. The fines were converted from EUR to PLN using the average exchange rate from January 29, 2024 (1 EUR = 4.3653 PLN).

**For A. (Administrator), a fine of 1,527,855.00 PLN (equivalent to 350,000 EUR) was imposed.**

- **Aggravating Factors:**
    - **Character, Gravity, and Duration:** The violation was significant, affecting over 21,000 individuals, with a wide range of personal data including PESEL numbers, which are highly sensitive and can lead to substantial financial or non-material damage (e.g., identity theft, fear of loss of control over data). The breach lasted from July 31, 2019, until security measures were implemented after June 3, 2020.
    - **Intent/Negligence:** The violation was deemed **unintentional** but resulted from **gross negligence** and disregard for administrator duties, despite A.'s knowledge of relevant security requirements.
    - **Previous Infringements:** A. had received two prior reprimands for GDPR violations (July 9, 2021, and September 14, 2023), indicating recurring issues, though not systemic or directly related to the current breach's subject matter.
- **Mitigating Factors:**
    - **Cooperation with Supervisory Authority:** A. demonstrated good cooperation by properly fulfilling procedural obligations and taking actions post-breach to enhance security.
- **Neutral Factors:** Actions to minimize harm (reporting the breach is mandatory), the degree of responsibility (part of the core violation), how UODO learned of the breach (A.'s mandatory notification), no prior remedies applied in this specific case, no use of approved codes of conduct/certifications (not mandatory), and no financial gain from the breach.

The fine was calculated using the EDPB Guidelines 04/2022. Starting from a high severity assessment (50% of the €20,000,000 static maximum for Art. 83(5) violations, resulting in €10,000,000), it was adjusted downwards to €500,000 and then further reduced to **€350,000** (1,527,855.00 PLN) to ensure proportionality and effectiveness, considering A.'s financial situation. The fine represents approximately (...)% of A.'s 2023 annual turnover.

**For X. (Processor), a fine of 20,037.00 PLN (equivalent to 4,590 EUR) was imposed.**

- **Aggravating Factors:**
    - **Character, Gravity, and Duration:** Similar to A., the breach was severe, affecting over 21,000 individuals with sensitive data (including PESEL), carrying a high risk of harm. The duration of the violation was also long.
    - **Intent/Negligence:** The violation was deemed **unintentional** but resulted from X.'s lack of due diligence and proper communication with A., despite its professional obligations to ensure data security.
- **Mitigating Factors:**
    - **Cooperation with Supervisory Authority:** X. provided comprehensive explanations and cooperated with the investigation.
- **Neutral Factors:** No actions taken to minimize harm directly, the degree of responsibility (part of the core violation), no prior GDPR violations identified for X., how UODO learned (A.'s notification), no prior remedies applied, no use of approved codes of conduct/certifications (not mandatory), and no financial gain from the breach.

The fine was also calculated using the EDPB Guidelines 04/2022. Starting from a high severity assessment (40% of the €10,000,000 static maximum for Art. 83(4) violations, resulting in €4,000,000), it was adjusted downwards to €12,000, then to €10,200, and further reduced to **€4,590** (20,037.00 PLN) to ensure proportionality, effectiveness, and consider X.'s financial situation. The fine represents approximately (...)% of X.'s 2023 net sales revenue.

Prezes UODO concluded that administrative fines were necessary to be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive, ensuring both companies take their data protection obligations seriously and to prevent similar future negligence.
