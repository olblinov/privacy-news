# Summary

The Italian Data Protection Authority, Garante per la Protezione dei Dati Personali, issued a **fine of €678,897** against Illumia S.p.A. on November 13, 2024, for multiple violations of the GDPR and the Italian Data Protection Code. The decision stemmed from an investigation initiated after complaints regarding **unlawful telemarketing practices**, specifically promotional calls made without a proper legal basis and using numbers not registered with the Communication Operators Register (ROC). The Authority found Illumia responsible for failing to demonstrate accountability, conducting marketing without valid consent, inadequately selecting and monitoring its commercial partners, and implementing insufficient security measures to prevent illegal contacts from entering its systems. As a result, in addition to the fine, Illumia was issued an **admonishment**, prohibited from further processing the complainants' data, and enjoined to adopt appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure compliance.

# Details

## Background

The investigation by the Italian Data Protection Authority (Garante per la Protezione dei Dati Personali) originated from **two separate complaints** filed against Illumia S.p.A. concerning the processing of personal data for telemarketing purposes.

- **Complaint 345203**: An individual claimed to have been contacted for promotional purposes on their mobile phone on behalf of Illumia, without a suitable legal basis, despite being registered with the Public Register of Oppositions (RPO).
- **Complaint 352673**: An individual reported receiving a call on their son's mobile phone, which they habitually used as a contact for managing their home utilities. The caller, identifying as an Illumia commercial representative, already knew about the complainant's request to switch providers to another company (XX) and falsely claimed that the switch would not go through, urging the complainant to sign a new contract with Illumia. In both cases, the calling numbers were **not registered with the Communication Operators Register (ROC)**.

Following these complaints, the Authority requested information from Illumia regarding the incidents and the measures adopted to ensure compliance with data protection laws in its telemarketing and teleselling activities. Illumia initially responded by stating that its agencies were contractually obligated to verify numbers with the Fondazione Ugo Bordoni (FUB) before campaigns. The company claimed the complainants' names were not on their systems, nor were the calling numbers associated with their partners, and that they had filed a **police report against unknown parties**. Illumia also described its agency selection process, which included a "Technical Disciplinary" document, a six-month trial period, and mapping agencies on internal systems, along with a "Check list GDPR" implemented from January 1, 2024, to verify data protection compliance. Illumia also mentioned regular training sessions, contractual clauses for data processors (ex art. 28 GDPR), and various control procedures for contracts and agency operations, including bimonthly monitoring of disavowals and on-site visits.

## Legal Analysis

The Authority's Office found Illumia's initial responses to be generic and contradictory, lacking sufficient documented proof, which led to the **contestation of several violations**. The Authority highlighted the following issues:

- **Lack of Accountability (Art. 5(2), 24 GDPR)**: Illumia failed to sufficiently demonstrate that its data processing operations complied with current data protection regulations. The documentation provided was deemed insufficient to provide a complete and coherent picture of the company's governance in data protection.
- **Unlawful Processing for Promotional Calls (Art. 5, 6, 7 GDPR, Art. 130 Codice)**: The Authority confirmed that promotional calls were made on Illumia's behalf without a proper legal basis and using numbers not registered with the ROC, as alleged in the complaints. Illumia's defense regarding the RPO registration timing for one complainant was rejected because the complainant had stated multiple RPO registrations, including one prior to the call. For the second complaint, the Authority affirmed the call was directed at the complainant, as the operator possessed specific identifying information related to her utilities, making the telephone number a personal data point linked to her, regardless of the number's owner.
- **Issues with Selection and Monitoring of Agencies (Art. 5(2), 24, 28 GDPR)**:
    - **_Culpa in Eligendo_ (Fault in Choosing)**: The Authority criticized Illumia for its agency selection process. While a "Check list GDPR" was implemented, it was only started in January 2024, significantly delayed compared to the GDPR's effective date, meaning many existing agencies were contracted without prior, careful privacy assessments. The six-month trial period clause was found to primarily focus on economic convenience rather than ensuring data protection compliance from the outset, which is a requirement of Art. 28 GDPR. #pbd #transfers #vendor_management 
    - **_Culpa in Vigilando_ (Fault in Monitoring)**: Illumia's oversight on its data processors was deemed insufficient. While Illumia detailed its monitoring activities (e.g., sample audits, reporting, quality committee), the Authority found that relying solely on formal instructions or reacting only to a high threshold of anomalies was not enough. The criteria for selecting agencies for scrutiny (e.g., number of finalized contracts, disavowals) were considered ineffective because they might only identify a small fraction of issues, and the obligation to act applies even to single anomalous episodes. #pbd #transfers #vendor_management 
    - **Inconsistent RPO Consultation Instructions**: The instructions given to agencies regarding RPO consultation were found to be contradictory (stating monthly consultation but acknowledging a 15-day validity period) and did not fully align with legal requirements, failing to distinguish between different marketing channels (phone, email, postal mail) and not contemplating data from public registers.
- **Insufficient Security Measures (Art. 5, 25, 32 GDPR)**: Illumia was found to have violated principles of integrity, security, and confidentiality. Despite having personalized accounts with passwords for contract loading, Illumia admitted that it was still evaluating the feasibility of implementing a system to track portal access, indicating a fundamental lack of preventative measures to block contracts originating from illicit contacts and to ensure adequate security against unauthorized access or data loss.

## Sanction

Based on the ascertained violations, the Garante issued the following measures against Illumia S.p.A. on November 13, 2024:

- **Ammonishment**: Illumia received an admonishment for not fully fulfilling its obligation to demonstrate compliance with data protection regulations during the preliminary investigation [24, 25a].
- **Prohibition of Further Processing**: Illumia was prohibited from any further processing of the complainants' data [24, 25b].
- **Injunction to Adopt Measures**:
    - Illumia was ordered to adopt technical and organizational measures to ensure full compliance with the obligations on data controllers under **Article 28 of the GDPR** (regarding the selection and monitoring of data processors) [24, 25c].
    - Illumia was ordered to adopt technical and organizational measures to prevent the activation of contracts originating from **illicit contacts** and to prevent **unauthorized access to corporate systems** [24, 25d].
    - Illumia must communicate to the Authority, within **thirty days** of notification, the initiatives undertaken to implement these imposed measures [25e]. Failure to comply may result in additional administrative monetary sanctions [25e].
- **Monetary Administrative Sanction**: A fine of **€678,897.00** was imposed.
    - This amount was determined considering Illumia's previous year's turnover, which set the maximum possible fine at €27,155,872.00 (4% of global annual turnover).
    - The Authority considered **aggravating factors** such as the gravity of the violations, the complex nature of telemarketing, and the Authority's extensive past guidance on the matter.
    - **Mitigating factors** included some technical and organizational measures already implemented by Illumia since January 2024, such as the privacy checklist for agency selection, sample controls on lists and contracts, and personal accounts for system access.
    - The fine of €678,897.00 represents **2.5% of the maximum editable sanction**, balancing the rights of data subjects with business freedom. #fine 
- **Ancillary Sanction of Publication**: The decision will be **published on the Garante's website**. This accessory sanction was deemed reasonable and proportionate due to the nature of the treatments, Illumia's conduct, and the risks to data subjects' rights and freedoms, emphasizing the culpability in delaying compliance with the GDPR.

Illumia has the option to settle the dispute by complying with the prescriptions and paying **half of the imposed sanction** within thirty days. Otherwise, the full amount is due within 30 days of notification. The company can also appeal the decision to the ordinary judicial authority within thirty days.
