# Summary

This document details a decision by the **Dutch** Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (AP) regarding an objection filed by **Coolblue B.V.**. The case concerns Coolblue's **violation of Article 5, first paragraph, read in conjunction with Article 6, first paragraph, of the General Data Protection Regulation (AVG)**. This violation specifically involved Coolblue processing personal data through cookies **without properly obtaining consent** from website visitors, thus lacking a valid legal basis for data processing. The AP initially imposed a fine, against which Coolblue objected. Following a re-evaluation of the case, the AP declared Coolblue's objection well-founded and decided to **reduce the fine to €40,000.00 (forty thousand euros)**.

# Details

## Factual Background

In October and November 2019, supervisors from the AP investigated websites, including Coolblue's (www.coolblue.nl), to assess compliance with AVG requirements for the use of (tracking) cookies. The investigation specifically examined whether visitors' consent for processing personal data via (tracking) cookies was requested appropriately.

The investigation revealed that Coolblue's website at the time displayed a cookie banner stating: "To help you better and more personally, we use cookies and similar techniques. With the cookies, we and third parties track your internet behavior within our site. This allows us to show advertisements based on your interest and you can share information via social media. If you continue on our website, we assume you agree. Want to know more? View our cookie page.". This banner operated on the basis of **implied consent**, and the "personal cookie package" was automatically active (pre-checked).

On November 29, 2019, the AP sent Coolblue a letter indicating that its website likely did not comply with AVG requirements and requested that Coolblue review its consent process for (tracking) cookies. However, on April 28, 2020, and May 6, 2020, AP supervisors found that Coolblue's approach had **not been adjusted**, leading the AP to initiate an official investigation.

Coolblue subsequently introduced a new, compliant cookie banner on **June 17, 2020**. The violation period, during which Coolblue processed personal data without a valid legal basis, was determined to be from **April 28, 2020, to June 17, 2020**.

## Violation

Coolblue was found to have violated **Article 5, first paragraph, section a, read in conjunction with Article 6, first paragraph, section a, of the AVG**. The core of the violation was the **processing of personal data through cookies without properly requesting consent from the visitor**, meaning Coolblue lacked a legally valid basis for this processing.

Specifically, Coolblue relied on **implied consent** from data subjects for processing their personal data via (tracking) cookies, and its "personal cookie package" was **automatically active (pre-checked)** #consent . The AP stated that neither implied consent nor the use of pre-checked boxes can lead to legally valid consent for personal data processing. This aligns with the European Court of Justice's ruling on October 1, 2019 (Planet49), which stated that valid consent under Article 4 of the AVG requires an **unambiguous active action** demonstrating a free, specific, informed, and unambiguous expression of will by the data subject to accept the processing of their personal data.

Coolblue acknowledged the violation, confirmed its responsibility as a data controller for the processing of personal data via (tracking) cookies on its website, and admitted that the infringement could be attributed and blamed on them. The AP deemed the infringement to be **reproachable (verwijtbaar)** because Coolblue could not have been unaware that its conduct constituted a breach, especially after receiving a non-compliance letter from the AP on November 29, 2019.

The violation primarily concerned the **principle of lawfulness**, a fundamental requirement for personal data protection that safeguards data subjects' control over their personal data. Coolblue's actions undermined this control by assuming silent consent and using pre-checked boxes.

The **severity of the infringement** was characterized as "low" due to several factors: #enforcement_pattern 

- The processing was not "very large scale" but also "certainly not small-scale," as it targeted online shoppers throughout the Netherlands, though limited to Coolblue's own website.
- While the website had a substantial number of visitors, Coolblue argued that the number of _actual affected data subjects_ was lower due to recurring visitors and extensive A/B testing of cookie banner designs during the violation period, which gradually reduced the number of affected individuals.
- Although data subjects lost control over their personal data, there was **no evidence of other damage** suffered by them.
- The **duration of the infringement** was relatively short, lasting seven weeks and one day (from April 28, 2020, to June 17, 2020), which the AP did not consider long-term. #change 
- The personal data involved were **not sensitive** or subject to additional protection under AVG Articles 9 and 10. Coolblue explained that the data were non-sensitive, low-risk, and used for internal analysis, displaying relevant products and advertisements, and sending personalized newsletters.

## Sanction

The AP initially imposed a fine on Coolblue. In its re-evaluation following Coolblue's objection, the AP applied both the **Dutch Fine Policy Rules 2019 (Boetebeleidsregels 2019)** and the **EDPB Guidelines 04/2022** for calculating administrative fines under the AVG. In this case, both sets of guidelines led to the same fine amount.

The initial base fine calculation:

1. Violations of AVG Articles 5 and 6 typically fall into **Category III** of the Fine Policy Rules. This category has a bandwidth of €300,000.00 to €750,000.00, with a base fine of **€525,000.00**. #fine 
2. However, considering mitigating circumstances, the AP determined that Category III would not result in an appropriate penalty and therefore applied the **next lower category, Category II**.
3. Category II has a bandwidth of €120,000.00 to €500,000.00, with a base fine of **€310,000.00**. This €310,000.00 served as the neutral starting point for the fine. #fine 

**Mitigating circumstances** that led to a significant reduction of the fine:

- **Low severity of the infringement**: As detailed in the "Violation" section, the nature, seriousness, and duration of the breach were considered low. The data involved were non-sensitive and low-risk.
- **Circumstances surrounding the termination of the infringement**: Although Coolblue did not terminate the violation entirely on its own initiative (it acted after the AP's letter), it was actively working to rectify the issue when the AP's investigation began and indeed ended the violation before the AP formally announced the investigation.
- **Ambiguity regarding the remediation deadline**: Coolblue argued that the non-compliance letter did not specify a hard deadline for rectification. The AP agreed that while the letter mentioned "within a few months," it lacked a concrete end date, contributing to some uncertainty.
- **Length of the period between the start of the procedure and the primary decision**: The AP acknowledged that the significant time elapsed (over three and a half years between the violation's end and the primary decision) was disproportionate to the complexity of the case and should have a mitigating effect on the fine.

Considering these mitigating circumstances, the AP concluded that a fine of **€40,000.00** was appropriate and necessary. This amount falls below the bandwidth of Category II, prompting a moderation of the fine based on the principle of proportionality. The AP also confirmed that this fine amount is below the statutory maximum fine under Article 83, fifth paragraph, of the AVG.

The AP determined that the imposed fine is **effective, proportionate, and deterrent**. It is deemed effective in punishing unlawful conduct and promoting compliance. Despite the violation of a fundamental AVG principle, the significant moderation due to specific circumstances ensures proportionality. The AP also believes the fine will deter Coolblue from future AVG infringements.

The AP therefore declared Coolblue's objection well-founded, revoked its original decision, and imposed a final administrative fine of **€40,000.00**. Coolblue has the right to appeal this decision within six weeks.
