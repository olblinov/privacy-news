# Summary

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) has finalized an order against Mobilewalla Inc., a data broker, **banning it from selling sensitive location data** and from collecting consumer data from online real-time bidding advertising exchanges for non-auction purposes. This settlement addresses FTC allegations that Mobilewalla unlawfully tracked and sold sensitive consumer location information, including visits to healthcare facilities and places of worship, without adequately verifying consumer consent. The order imposes **strict requirements** on Mobilewalla, including implementing programs to identify and prevent the use of sensitive location data, verify supplier consent, and establish a comprehensive privacy program, along with a mandate to delete historical sensitive data. No monetary fine was specified in the order.

# Details

## Background

Mobilewalla, Inc. is a Delaware corporation operating as a data broker, collecting and aggregating vast quantities of consumer information, particularly **precise location data tied to individual consumers**. The company aimed to create a "comprehensive, cross channel view of the customer, understanding online and offline behavior". Mobilewalla did not collect data directly from consumers; instead, it obtained consumer location data and other personal information from various data suppliers. Consumers generally had no direct interactions with Mobilewalla and were often unaware that the company possessed their data.

Mobilewalla's business practices involved:

- **Licensing "audience segments"**: The company categorized mobile advertising identifiers (MAIDs) into "audience segments" based on locations and events visited by consumers' mobile devices, such as "Music Lovers" or custom segments like "pregnant women and young mothers". These segments were used for advertising, political campaigning, and government purposes, including attempts to track union organizers. #adtech 
- **Collecting data from real-time bidding (RTB) exchanges**: Mobilewalla primarily sourced consumer information from RTB exchanges. Even when it did not win a bid to place an advertisement, Mobilewalla collected and retained information from bid requests, including a consumer’s device MAID and precise geolocation, despite RTB exchange terms disallowing such collection for non-advertising purposes. Between January 2018 and June 2020, approximately **60% of Mobilewalla's consumer data came from RTB exchanges**. Over this period, it collected hundreds of millions of unique advertising identifiers paired with location information, and more than 2 billion unique advertising identifiers in total. #adtech 
- **Purchasing data from other data brokers**: Mobilewalla also acquired consumer information from data brokers and aggregators, including MAIDs paired with precise geolocation, app names, phone numbers (in clear text or hashed), and hashed email addresses.
- **Selling raw location data**: Mobilewalla licensed raw, non-anonymized location data tied to MAIDs to third parties, which could be used to identify individual consumers and their movements. The geolocation data was highly precise, with Mobilewalla asserting the ability to target within a **25-meter radius**. Mobilewalla lacked policies to remove sensitive locations from the raw data sets it sold, allowing the data to track consumers to sensitive locations such as medical facilities, places of religious worship, LGBTQ+-related locations, and domestic abuse shelters, inferring sensitive information about them.
- **Targeting consumers based on sensitive characteristics**: The company created custom audience segments based on sensitive information, such as pregnant women, Hispanic churchgoers, and members of the LGBTQ+ community. Mobilewalla used geo-fences around locations like pregnancy centers, political rallies, polling places, and places of worship to build these segments and even infer racial demographics. #sensitive 
- **Non-advertising uses**: Mobilewalla used collected information for purposes beyond advertising, including attempting to "poach nurses" by geo-fencing their home addresses and certain healthcare centers, and experimenting with tracking union organizers. It also transferred consumer information, including precise geolocation, to government entities.
- **Indefinite data retention**: Mobilewalla indefinitely retained sensitive precise location data, timestamps, and MAIDs, amassing a vast repository that allowed tracking consumer movements and inferring sensitive information over years. This extensive retention was touted as allowing more accurate identification of home addresses. #retention 

The FTC alleged that Mobilewalla failed to take reasonable steps to verify consumer consent for its data collection and use. The company relied on vague contractual assurances from suppliers and conducted only minimal, one-time checks of app disclosures, often finding deficiencies but failing to act. Consumers were typically unaware of Mobilewalla's collection and how their data was used to classify them based on sensitive characteristics and disclosed to unknown third parties. The FTC highlighted that these practices caused or were likely to cause substantial injury to consumers, including loss of privacy, stigma, discrimination, physical violence, and emotional distress. Examples cited included the use of location data to reveal religious beliefs, sexual preferences (leading to a priest's resignation), and visits to reproductive health clinics, resulting in targeted and harmful advertisements. The indefinite retention of data also increased risks of cybersecurity intrusions, stalking, targeted scams, and reputational harm.

## Legal Analysis

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) enforces federal competition and consumer protection laws that prohibit anticompetitive, deceptive, and unfair business practices. The legal action against Mobilewalla was initiated under **Section 5(a) of the FTC Act, 15 U.S.C. §45(a)**, which prohibits "unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce". An act or practice is considered unfair under Section 5 if it causes or is likely to cause substantial injury to consumers that consumers cannot reasonably avoid themselves, and that is not outweighed by countervailing benefits to consumers or competition.

The FTC's complaint alleged five specific counts of unfair acts or practices against Mobilewalla:

- **Count I: Unfair Sale of Sensitive Location Information**. The FTC alleged that Mobilewalla's sale, licensing, or transfer of precise location information associated with MAIDs, which revealed consumers' visits to sensitive locations (e.g., medical facilities, places of worship, LGBTQ+ community services), caused substantial injury to consumers through loss of privacy, chilling of First Amendment rights, and increased risk of harmful disclosure.
- **Count II: Unfair Targeting Based on Sensitive Characteristics**. Mobilewalla's practice of categorizing consumers into audience segments based on sensitive characteristics (e.g., medical conditions, religious beliefs) derived from location data, and selling these segments for marketing and other purposes, was deemed to cause substantial injury due to loss of privacy and increased risk of sensitive information disclosure.
- **Count III: Unfair Collection of Consumer Information from RTB Exchanges**. The complaint stated that Mobilewalla's collection and retention of consumers’ personal and location information from RTB exchanges without having a winning bid and for non-advertising purposes caused substantial injury through privacy loss, chilling of First Amendment rights, and risk of harmful disclosure.
- **Count IV: Unfair Collection and Use of Consumer Location Information Without Consent Verification**. The FTC alleged that Mobilewalla's failure to take reasonable steps to verify consumer consent for the collection and use of their location information resulted in substantial injury, including loss of privacy, chilling of First Amendment rights, and risk of harmful disclosure.
- **Count V: Unfair Retention of Consumer Location Information**. Mobilewalla's indefinite retention of detailed, sensitive information about consumers’ movements was cited as causing substantial injury through privacy loss and increased risk of disclosure and use, including through retroactive geofences.

The Commission determined that it had reason to believe Mobilewalla had violated the Federal Trade Commission Act, leading to the issuance of the complaint and the subsequent Consent Agreement.

## Sanction

The Federal Trade Commission finalized an order banning data broker Mobilewalla Inc. from various practices and imposing specific compliance requirements. Commissioner Melissa Holyoak dissented from the 4-1 vote approving the final order.

The key provisions of the order include:

- **Prohibition Against Misrepresentations**: Mobilewalla is prohibited from misrepresenting the extent to which it collects, uses, maintains, discloses, or deletes Covered Information, or the extent to which Location Data is Deidentified.
- **Prohibition on Collection and Retention of Covered Information from Advertising Auctions**: Mobilewalla must not collect, purchase, acquire, or retain Covered Information accessed during online advertising auctions for any purpose other than participating in those auctions.
- **Prohibition on the Use, Sale, or Disclosure of Sensitive Location Data**: Mobilewalla is banned from selling, licensing, sharing, disclosing, transferring, or otherwise using Sensitive Location Data in any products or services. This prohibition applies to data associated with Sensitive Locations identified by a new program, unless such data is converted into non-sensitive location data or non-location data.
- **Prohibition on the Sale, Licensing, or Disclosure of Private Residence Data**: Mobilewalla must not sell, license, or disclose Location Data that can determine the identity or location of an individual’s private residence (e.g., homes, apartments).

The order also mandates several programs and actions for Mobilewalla:

- **Sensitive Location Data Program**: Within 180 days, Mobilewalla must establish and maintain a program to develop a comprehensive list of Sensitive Locations and prevent the use, sale, licensing, transfer, or disclosure of Sensitive Location Data. This program requires documented components, a senior officer responsible for it, at least semi-annual assessments of the list's accuracy and completeness, implementation of procedures to identify Sensitive Locations, and monitoring of technical measures to prevent prohibited data use. Data associated with locations added to the sensitive list must be deleted or rendered non-sensitive within 7 days.
- **Supplier Assessment Program**: Within 90 days, Mobilewalla must implement a program to ensure consumers have provided consent for collected Location Data. This includes initial and annual assessments to confirm **Affirmative Express Consent** (defined as freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous agreement with clear and conspicuous disclosures, excluding inferred consent or consent subverting user autonomy). Mobilewalla must cease using data for which consent was not provided.
- **Disclosures to Consumers**: Mobilewalla must provide a **Clear and Conspicuous** means for consumers to request the identity of any entity to whom their Location Data was disclosed in the preceding year. "Clear and Conspicuous" has specific requirements for visibility, audibility, and understandability across different mediums.
- **Withholding and Withdrawing Consent**: Mobilewalla must provide a simple, easily-located means for consumers to withdraw consent that is no more burdensome than providing it. The company cannot unreasonably limit a consumer’s ability to withhold or withdraw consent, such as by degrading product or service quality, unless technically necessary.
- **Obligations When Consent is Withdrawn**: Upon receiving notice of consent withdrawal, Mobilewalla must delete all associated Location Data within 30 days and immediately cease further collection or use, with limited exceptions for security, archives, or legal requirements.
- **Location Data Deletion Requests**: Mobilewalla must provide a **Clear and Conspicuous** means for consumers to request deletion of their device’s Location Data, with similar exceptions for security, archives, or legal requirements.
- **Data Retention Limits**: Within 60 days, Mobilewalla must document, adhere to, and publicly post a retention schedule for Covered Information. This schedule must specify purposes, business needs for retention, and established timeframes for deletion, **prohibiting indefinite retention**.
- **Deletion**: Within 90 days, Mobilewalla must delete or destroy all **Historic Location Data** (data lacking demonstrable consent) and consumers’ unhashed/hashed phone numbers. Within 120 days, all **Data Products** (models, algorithms, derived data, audience segments) must be deleted or destroyed. Mobilewalla must also inform customers who received Historic Location Data within the past three years of these deletion requirements. Exceptions to deletion apply if consent is obtained, data is deidentified or non-sensitive, or retention is for security or legal reasons.
- **Mandated Privacy Program**: Within 60 days, Mobilewalla must establish and maintain a comprehensive privacy program protecting Covered Information. This program requires written documentation, annual review by senior leadership, designation of qualified employees, annual assessment of privacy risks, design and implementation of safeguards, annual privacy training for employees, and annual testing/monitoring of safeguards.
- **Compliance and Recordkeeping**: Mobilewalla must obtain acknowledgments of the order from relevant personnel, submit compliance reports and notices to the FTC, and maintain specific records for 5 to 10 years to demonstrate compliance with the order's provisions. The order is effective upon its publication on the FTC's website and will terminate 20 years from its issuance date or from the most recent complaint alleging a violation.
