# IAB Europe's Feedback Paper on "Consent or Pay" Models

IAB Europe, the European-level association for the digital marketing and advertising ecosystem, along with several other trade associations, submitted a feedback paper to the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) on January 16, 2025. This paper outlined key remarks and concerns following the EDPB’s stakeholder event on the forthcoming draft Guidelines concerning “Consent or Pay” (CorP) models, which took place on November 18, 2024. The associations emphasized the need for a **unified European approach consistent with existing legal frameworks**, such as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and rulings by the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU). They also expressed gratitude for the opportunity to participate and for the EDPB inviting companies to explain the economic and practical implications of further restrictions on CorP models.

# Legality and "Freely Given Consent" in CorP Models

The undersigned trade associations stress that CorP models have already been recognized as legal in various jurisdictions, demonstrating their **legality and alignment with GDPR principles**. A major concern is the assessment of "freely given consent" in the context of CorP. CorP models are seen as providing users with **autonomy by offering clear options**: either paying a fee for access to an online service or accepting the processing of personal data for personalized advertising to gain free access. Users also retain the **freedom to choose neither option and seek alternative services**.

The paper pushes back against the premise that individuals suffer a detriment by being offered these options, comparing it to routinely paying for services in the physical world. It asserts there is **no fundamental right to access digital content and services for free**, and consent to personalized advertising should be viewed as consideration for benefiting from a service, much like monetary payment. Publishers are naturally incentivized to apply reasonable pricing to avoid driving users to competitors.

Furthermore, the paper argues that **CorP models do not coerce or limit consumer ability to seek alternatives**, noting that 10% to 50% of users leave services without selecting an option, which demonstrates user autonomy. It also clarifies that **end-users who consent to personalized advertising do not forfeit their fundamental data protection rights**, which are guaranteed under GDPR regardless of the legal basis for processing. A key element for assessing freely given consent should be ensuring users are **well-informed about data processing purposes, how to refuse/withdraw consent, and the available options**.

Regarding the **imbalance of power**, the associations argue that it does not inherently negate voluntariness unless there's clear coercion, intimidation, deception, or disproportionate disadvantage. They cite a CJEU judgment that recognizes the legality for companies, even with established market power, to offer users a choice between consenting and paying a fee. The GDPR's legislative history also supports this, as the originally proposed exclusion of valid consent in cases of "significant imbalance" was deliberately removed. The paper states that **Data Protection Authorities (DPAs) lack the mandate and expertise to assess market power or lock-in network effects**, which are the remit of competition authorities. The GDPR is technology- and business-model-neutral and applies a risk-based approach, and the EDPB's inconsistent opinions, first targeting "large online platforms" (a notion without GDPR basis), are criticized for creating legal uncertainties and potentially burdening smaller businesses disproportionately.

# The "Free Alternative Without Behavioural Advertising"

The sources assert that **neither the GDPR nor the ePrivacy Directive intend to interfere with business models** or mandate a third, free option without personalized advertising. They argue that **contextual advertising is not a realistic alternative** for most market players because it generates significantly less revenue and is less effective in filling available ad slots compared to personalized advertising. Studies indicate that 90% of publishers sell ads at a lower price without cookies or identifiers, with a relative price difference of -50.9% for news & information publishers. Contextual advertising also fills far fewer available ad slots (20% on average) compared to personalized advertising (80-90%).

It is also highlighted that **contextual advertising still requires the use of personal data and/or information from users’ devices**, likely triggering a consent requirement under strict regulatory interpretations. A requirement for a free alternative funded by contextual advertising would **reduce companies' revenue** due to lower prices and volume, potentially making it unfeasible to maintain free or lower-priced ad-funded options, ultimately leading to less diverse or more obtrusive content for users. The paper encourages the EDPB to adopt a **neutral approach regarding technology and business models**.

# The Concept of an "Equivalent Alternative"

The concept of an "equivalent alternative" cannot apply uniformly across all types of online services and **depends intrinsically on the nature of the offering(s)**. CorP models are used by various services, not just traditional content publishers. If users refuse consent for personalized advertising and choose a paid option, this should not prevent controllers from establishing legal bases for other data processing purposes that improve user experience, such as audience measurement or content personalization. The inclusion of premium features in a paid alternative **does not undermine the freely given nature of consent** as long as users are adequately informed. Finally, "equivalence" should not be seen as requiring "identical" experiences, as services with or without ads, or with different types of advertising, will inevitably differ.

# Assessing an "Appropriate Fee"

The notion of an "appropriate fee" in CorP models is complex and involves **implications across data protection, consumer law, and competition law**. The primary criterion under data protection law for assessing whether a fee undermines voluntary consent is whether it imposes "significant economic pressure" or “substantial extra costs” on users. Any evaluation of appropriateness should consider the **overall revenue and operating costs on a case-by-case basis** to account for the diversity and competitiveness of digital services.

The feedback paper strongly refutes the civil society organization NOYB's allegation of an average annual ad revenue of €1.41 per user, stating that this calculation is **inaccurate, oversimplified, and disregards significant market differences** and the varying revenue potential of different publishers. It argues that **average consent rates should not be considered a relevant criterion** for assessing fee appropriateness, as affordability and willingness to pay are distinct concepts. The freedom to conduct a business includes the right to choose remuneration models, and the CJEU judgment supports this discretion. The paper asserts that **"protection of personal data" and "freedom to conduct a business" are both fundamental freedoms** under the EU Charter and should be balanced, not subjected to a hierarchy.

# Other Guidance Beyond the Scope of the Opinion

The paper urges the EDPB to refrain from making over-generalized assumptions that portray personalized advertising as unlawful, emphasizing that this is **inconsistent with the broader EU legal framework** and conflicts with the GDPR's case-by-case assessment. Existing laws, such as the Digital Content Directive 2019/770 and the EU Consumer Rights Omnibus Directive 2019/2161, already **recognize the lawfulness of providing digital content or services in exchange for personal data** instead of payment.

Finally, the associations recommend that **guidance concerning Article 9 GDPR (special categories of personal data) and the protection of minors should be addressed separately** from the upcoming CorP guidelines. This is due to stricter consent requirements for special categories of data and specific rules under the Digital Services Act (DSA) for minors, necessitating a comprehensive approach that considers the interplay between GDPR and DSA.

# Conclusion and Recommendations

In conclusion, IAB Europe and the co-signing associations argue that CorP models do not coerce users but rather provide a **fair and transparent framework that upholds user autonomy, respects GDPR principles, and balances the interests of businesses and end-users**. They reiterate that "protection of personal data" is not an absolute right and must be balanced with other fundamental rights and freedoms, such as the freedom to conduct business and freedom of expression and information. Given the significant economic implications, any EDPB guidance on CorP models should be **adequately based on empirical research and appropriate consultation with competition and consumer protection authorities**.

# Organizations that Signed the Feedback Paper

The feedback paper was submitted by IAB Europe along with the following organizations:

- **Alliance Digitale**: Represents professions related to data and print/digital marketing in France, aiming to promote their development and defend their interests, and is a privileged interlocutor for public authorities and regulators.
- **BVDW (German Association for the Digital Economy)**: Advocacy group for companies with digital business models or value creation based on digital technologies in Germany, shaping the future through creative solutions and advocating for innovation-friendly framework conditions.
- **IAB Finland**: A community of professionals dedicated to driving the growth of member companies' businesses through digital advertising and marketing in Finland, part of the international IAB network.
- **IAB Ireland**: The trade organization for digital advertising in Ireland, focused on proving, promoting, and protecting the Irish digital advertising industry through standards development, research, knowledge sharing, and engagement with policymakers.
- **IAB Italia**: The Italian chapter of the Interactive Advertising Bureau, contributing to the diffusion of digital culture and market growth in Italy through ethical and sustainable communication, events, and training.
- **IAB Polska**: A Polish advertising industry organization that unites and represents entities of the interactive industry, including web portals, media groups, agencies, and technology providers.
- **IAB Spain**: A forum for meeting and representing the digital advertising industry in Spain, playing a crucial role in promoting and developing digital advertising through regulation, standards, and best practices.
- **SPIR (Association for Internet Progress)**: Represents key players in the Czech Internet economy, including media publishers, media agencies, and technology companies, operating official measurements of Czech Internet traffic and advertising.
