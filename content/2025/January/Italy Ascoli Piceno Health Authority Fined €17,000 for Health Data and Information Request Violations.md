# Summary

The Italian Data Protection Authority, Garante per la protezione dei dati personali, imposed a fine of **€17,000.00** on the Azienda Sanitaria Territoriale di Ascoli Piceno (AST) for violations of data protection principles and failure to comply with an information request. The case stemmed from a complaint that the AST issued administrative certificates for absence from work that disclosed sensitive health data, such as the specific department or medical specialization, thereby violating principles of data minimization, integrity, confidentiality, privacy by design, and security. Despite the AST's explanations regarding organizational changes and DPO transitions, the Garante found these insufficient to excuse the violations, emphasizing that the relevant data protection principles and guidelines were well-established prior to these changes.

# Details

## Background

The case originated from a **complaint** filed by an individual (referred to as "Signora XX") alleging that the **Azienda Sanitaria Territoriale di Ascoli Piceno (AST)** provided "an attestation (to be presented to the employer as justification for absence) that indicates the department where the patient received the service (neurology, gynecology, orthopedics), thus nullifying the user's right not to want to inform the employer and the relevant human resources office in which area they are undergoing examinations, visits, or treatments".

The Garante initiated an investigation, requesting information from the AST. The AST initially **failed to respond** to this request, leading the Garante to issue a formal notification of violation under **Article 157 of the Code**. Following an extension request, the AST eventually provided a response. In its defense, the AST cited significant organizational changes, including the **suppression of ASUR Marche** and the establishment of new ASTs, a **7-month commissariamento** (commissionership), and **changes in Data Protection Officers (DPOs)**. The AST acknowledged that these factors, along with the recent COVID-19 pandemic, "did not facilitate the initiation of the foreseen procedures" but stated that they were not justifications for non-compliance.

The AST subsequently reported that it had taken steps to address the issue:

- A **task force** was created with the DPO to adopt measures to conform data processing to regulations.
- New procedures were implemented to prevent the disclosure of patient health status through correlation with department or structure information.
- Urgent corrective measures were put in place to ensure that **administrative certificates** (e.g., for justifying absence from work) **do not contain indications that could link the certificate to the service discipline or the patient's health status**.
- New standardized forms were prepared and communicated to relevant macro-areas, explicitly omitting department indications or specialization stamps.
- Software providers for administrative attestations were asked to **adapt their systems** to the Garante's provisions.
- **Employee training** on GDPR and privacy regulations, which began in 2018 and continued in 2022 and 2023, was ongoing for 2024 to ensure continuous updates on data protection. #pbd 

The Garante formally notified the AST of the violation, stating that the AST had processed health data in a manner not compliant with Articles **5, par. 1, lett. c) and f), 25, and 32 of the Regulation**, thereby violating principles of **minimization, integrity, confidentiality, privacy by design, and security**. In its further defense, the AST reiterated its organizational challenges, the transition of DPOs, and stated that the staff did not intend to cause harm. It described the violation as **"minor"** and an **"isolated event"** affecting only one individual, though potentially others, and offered an apology.

## Legal Analysis

The Garante confirmed that "personal data" refers to any information concerning an identified or identifiable physical person, and "health data" includes information related to physical or mental health, including healthcare services, that reveal information about a person's health status.

The Garante's investigation concluded that the AST's actions constituted an **unlawful processing of health data** and a **violation of its obligations to the Authority**. Specifically, the violations included:

- **Principle of Minimization (Art. 5, par. 1, lett. c) of the Regulation)**: Personal data must be "adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed". By including the department or specialization on administrative certificates, the AST processed data beyond what was necessary for justifying absence from work. #minimization 
- **Principle of Integrity and Confidentiality (Art. 5, par. 1, lett. f) of the Regulation)**: Data must be "processed in a manner that ensures appropriate security..., including protection, by adequate technical and organizational measures, against unauthorized or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage". Disclosing sensitive health information undermines the confidentiality of the data.
- **Privacy by Design (Art. 25 of the Regulation)**: Data controllers must implement "appropriate technical and organizational measures" to effectively implement data protection principles and integrate safeguards into processing from the outset. The AST failed to design its certificate templates to prevent this disclosure. #pbd 
- **Security of Processing (Art. 32 of the Regulation)**: The controller must adopt appropriate measures to ensure a level of security adequate to the risk, especially regarding unauthorized disclosure or access.
- **Obligation to Provide Information (Art. 157 of the Code)**: The Garante has the power to request information and documents from controllers for the performance of its duties. The AST's initial failure to respond constituted a direct violation of this article.

The Garante emphasized that the legal and regulatory framework supporting these principles, particularly concerning the handling of health data in administrative certificates, **predates the AST's recent regional reorganization and the pandemic**. It highlighted that the requirement to prevent explicit correlation between an individual and departments/structures that could indicate a health status is a **long-standing, consolidated orientation** of the Garante, reaffirmed multiple times since at least 2005. The Garante also noted that the **succession of DPOs does not exempt the data controller** from verifying compliance with data protection laws or from providing information when requested. Furthermore, it stressed that treating the DPO nomination as a mere formality or having only sporadic contact with the DPO undermines the "privacy by design and by default" approach promoted by the Regulation.

Despite the AST's subsequent corrective actions (modifying certificate issuance, verifying application, and providing training), the Garante concluded that the **AST's defenses were not sufficient** to overturn the findings of unlawfulness, as the violations had occurred. However, given that the "conduct has ceased its effects" and the AST has implemented corrective measures, the Garante determined that **further corrective measures under Article 58, par. 2, of the Regulation were not necessary** at the time of the decision.

## Sanction

For the violations, the Garante decided to impose administrative monetary penalties and an accessory sanction:

- The violations of **Articles 5, par. 1, lett. c) and f), 25, and 32 of the Regulation** are subject to pecuniary administrative sanctions under **Article 83, par. 4, lett. a) and par. 5, lett. a) of the Regulation**.
- The violation of **Article 157 of the Code** is also subject to administrative pecuniary sanction under **Article 83, par. 5, of the Regulation**.

The Garante assessed the **level of gravity** for the GDPR violations (Art. 5, 25, 32) as **high**, considering the type of personal data involved (health data), the number of potentially affected individuals, the nature of the processing, and the duration of the violation. Applying Article 83, par. 3, which states that the total fine does not exceed the amount specified for the most serious violation when multiple violations stem from a single conduct, the Garante determined the amount for these violations.

Considering aggravating and mitigating factors under Article 83, par. 2, including:

- The Authority learned of the event through a **complaint**.
- The processing involved **health data** of one interested party, potentially others.
- The subjective element of the violation was deemed **negligent (colposo)**.
- The controller **committed to implementing measures** to prevent recurrence.

The Garante decided to impose a fine of **€13,000.00** for the violations of Articles 5, 25, and 32 of the Regulation.

For the violation of **Article 157 of the Code**, related to the failure to respond to the information request, the Garante considered its gravity as **medium**, noting the absence of willful misconduct. A separate fine of **€4,000.00** was imposed for this violation.

The **total administrative pecuniary sanction** imposed on the Azienda Sanitaria Territoriale di Ascoli Piceno is **€17,000.00**.

Additionally, the Garante ordered the **publication of the full decision on its website**, as an accessory sanction, in accordance with Article 166, comma 7 of the Code, and Article 16 of the Garante's internal Regulation n. 1/2019, especially given the sensitive nature of the personal data involved.

The AST has the option to **settle the dispute by paying half of the comminated sanction** (€8,500.00) within 30 days. Otherwise, the full amount must be paid within 30 days of notification. The decision can be appealed before the ordinary judicial authority within 30 days (or 60 days if the appellant resides abroad).
