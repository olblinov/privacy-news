# Summary

The Polish Supreme Administrative Court (NSA) has definitively ruled that **land registry numbers constitute personal data**, affirming a previous decision by the Provincial Administrative Court in Warsaw (WSA) and supporting the stance of the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO). This landmark ruling concludes a long-standing dispute between UODO and the former Chief Geodesist of Poland (GGK), confirming that the GGK's publication of these numbers without a legal basis violated data protection regulations. As a result, an administrative fine of **100,000 PLN** previously imposed by the UODO on the GGK for disclosing land registry numbers on the Geoportal2 service has been upheld as justified. This decision reinforces that easy access to land registry data, which includes sensitive personal details, poses a significant risk of identity theft.

# Details

## Background

The legal matter centered on a dispute between the President of the Personal Data Protection Office (UODO) and the former Chief Geodesist of Poland (GGK) concerning whether land registry numbers should be classified as personal data. In 2020, the President of UODO imposed an administrative financial penalty of **100,000 PLN** on the GGK for disclosing land registry numbers on the Geoportal2 service.

The publication of these numbers was particularly problematic because, as highlighted by the Provincial Administrative Court in Warsaw (WSA), the numbers placed on the Geoportal2 service were not just identifiers but also **hyperlinks** that directly redirected users to the Ministry of Justice's electronic land registry website. This functionality **facilitated direct access** for users to the content of the land registries without even needing to manually enter the land registry number. The information contained within land registries for natural persons includes highly sensitive data such as names, surnames, parents' names, PESEL numbers (a national identification number in Poland), property addresses, and mortgage information. The easy accessibility of these details through the Geoportal2 service exposed a large number of individuals to the **risk of identity theft**.

## Legal Analysis

The Supreme Administrative Court (NSA), in its ruling on January 28, 2025, sided with the President of UODO, definitively concluding that **land registry numbers are personal data**. #personal_data  This decision by the NSA upheld the prior verdict of the Provincial Administrative Court in Warsaw (WSA) from May 5, 2021, and agreed with the supervisory authority's (UODO) position that the publication of land registry numbers without a legal basis constituted a violation of data protection regulations.

Mirosław Wróblewski, the President of UODO, commented that the NSA's decision confirms all arguments presented by UODO and **"ends the discussion"** on the matter, affirming that land registry numbers are personal data within the meaning of Article 4 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

According to **Article 4 of GDPR**, as cited by the President of UODO, "personal data" refers to **"any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person"**. An identifiable natural person is one who can be identified, directly or indirectly, through identifiers such as a name, an identification number, location data, an online identifier, or one or more specific factors that define the physical, physiological, genetic, mental, economic, cultural, or social identity of that natural person. The UODO and the courts argued that the disclosure of a land registry number **allows for the identification** of the individual whose data is contained within that specific land registry. Given the extensive personal information available in the land registry, its public accessibility through published numbers clearly leads to the identification of individuals.

## Sanction

The NSA's favorable ruling for the UODO means that the administrative financial penalty of **100,000 PLN** previously imposed by the President of UODO in 2020 on the Chief Geodesist of Poland (GGK) for publicly disclosing land registry numbers on the Geoportal2 service is now deemed **valid and justified**. This decision serves as a final confirmation of the penalty.
