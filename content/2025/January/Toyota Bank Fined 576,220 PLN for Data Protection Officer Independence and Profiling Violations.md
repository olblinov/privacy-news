# Summary

The President of the Office for Personal Data Protection (Prezes UODO) in Poland imposed administrative pecuniary fines totaling 576,220 PLN on C. S.A. (identified as Toyota Bank Polska S.A.) for violations of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). The first fine of 261,918 PLN was for failing to ensure the Data Protection Officer (DPO) directly reported to the highest management and did not receive instructions, thus compromising DPO independence. The second fine of 314,302 PLN was for neglecting to include profiling activities in the company's record of processing activities (ROPA) and for not conducting a separate data protection impact assessment (DPIA) for profiling, despite profiling being a significant operation for creditworthiness assessment.

# Details

## Background

The administrative proceedings were initiated by Prezes UODO _ex officio_. The investigation stemmed from control activities conducted at C. S.A. (Toyota Bank Polska S.A.). The scope of the control focused on C. S.A.'s processing of personal data of clients and potential clients, particularly concerning profiling activities related to creditworthiness assessment. The control aimed to ascertain, among other things, the scope and purpose of data processing, whether procedures for profiling were in place, how automated decision-making for credit risk was handled, if a DPO was appointed, if profiling was included in the ROPA, and if DPIAs were performed for profiling.

During the control, it was established that C. S.A. processed and profiled personal data, including names, addresses, PESEL numbers, financial details, and credit obligations, primarily to determine creditworthiness and generate scoring results (credit risk assessment points). It was found that the appointed DPO (SJ) did not report directly to the board, which is considered the "highest management" under GDPR Article 38(3). #dpo  Instead, the DPO was employed as a specialist within a department and reported to the department director (KN), who was also designated as the DPO's deputy. This arrangement contradicted C. S.A.'s own internal policy which stated the DPO should report directly to a board member. Furthermore, the company's record of processing activities did not explicitly list profiling as a separate activity, and a separate DPIA for profiling data was not conducted, although a DPIA for the general credit assessment process (which included profiling) was performed. #dpia 

C. S.A. responded to the findings, claiming that the DPO was factually independent and that their departmental placement was merely "administrative" for employee-related matters like leave approval or salary. The company also stated that it had initiated changes to reclassify the DPO's position to report directly to a board member and updated its ROPA and DPIA to explicitly include profiling activities following the control but before the final decision.

## Legal Analysis

Prezes UODO identified two distinct sets of violations by C. S.A.:

- **Violation of Art. 38(3) GDPR (DPO Independence):** The DPO must report directly to the highest management and not receive instructions. #dpo Prezes UODO found that C. S.A. failed to implement organizational measures to ensure this, as the DPO reported to a department director, not the board. The DPO's direct superior also being appointed as the DPO's deputy further compromised independence. C. S.A.'s arguments of _de facto_ independence were dismissed, as internal documents and the organizational structure clearly indicated formal and factual subordination to a non-board member. This situation was deemed a continuous and intentional violation lasting over three years, from June 24, 2020, to at least August 22, 2023. The lack of DPO independence weakens data security, especially for an entity processing sensitive and large-scale data.
    
- **Violations of Art. 30(1) and Art. 35(1) & (7) GDPR (Profiling Documentation):**
    
    - **Art. 30(1) GDPR (ROPA):** C. S.A. maintained a ROPA but did not explicitly account for profiling activities. Prezes UODO emphasized that profiling is a specific form of automated data processing that generates new information (like credit scores) and carries significant risks (e.g., stigmatization, discrimination). #profiling Therefore, it must be clearly and separately described in the ROPA to ensure transparency and effectiveness for supervisory oversight and data security. The failure to do so was considered a violation, despite C. S.A. arguing profiling was part of other "product-based" processing descriptions. This violation, though deemed unintentional (due to negligence/lack of awareness by a professional entity), was long-lasting, from January 15, 2019, to August 2023.
    - **Art. 35(1) & (7) GDPR (DPIA):** C. S.A. did not conduct a separate DPIA for data profiling. #dpia p Prezes UODO clarified that profiling, especially on a large scale by a financial institution, inherently carries high risks to individuals' rights and freedoms, even if automated decision-making is not exclusive. Official guidelines (Art. 29 Working Party and Prezes UODO's own communication) explicitly identify profiling by banks for credit assessment as requiring a DPIA. C. S.A.'s argument that profiling was implicitly covered within a broader credit assessment DPIA was rejected because the DPIA needs to clearly and concretely address the specific risks of profiling. This omission "dilutes" the true risks of profiling, making it inconsistent with the GDPR. This violation was also long-lasting, from January 15, 2019, to August 2023.

Prezes UODO determined that the two profiling-related violations (Art. 30(1) and Art. 35(1) & (7)) constituted "one coherent behavior" under GDPR Art. 83(3) as they shared the same intent, context (credit assessment, specific data types), and affected data subjects. They were not mutually exclusive. The DPO independence violation (Art. 38(3)) was a separate behavior due to a different act, timeframe, and less direct relation to specific processing operations.

## Sanction

Prezes UODO applied administrative pecuniary fines using the methodology of the European Data Protection Board (EROD) Guidelines 04/2022. Both categories of violations (DPO independence and profiling documentation) fall under GDPR Art. 83(4)(a), carrying a maximum fine of up to 10,000,000 EUR or 2% of the company's total annual worldwide turnover, whichever is higher. C. S.A.'s annual revenue was 430,699,360 PLN (approx. 98.66 million EUR).

For the **Art. 38(3) violation (DPO Independence)**, several factors were considered:

- **Aggravating factors:** The nature, gravity, and duration of the violation (over 3 years) by a professional entity processing sensitive data at a large scale. The violation was deemed intentional due to C. S.A.'s awareness of the incorrect DPO organization. Furthermore, C. S.A. had six prior GDPR violations, including a previous administrative fine, indicating a "generally negligent approach to data protection".
- **Mitigating factors:** C. S.A.'s satisfactory cooperation with the supervisory authority, providing explanations and initiating corrective actions (reorganizing the DPO's position).
- **Neutral factors:** No direct harm to data subjects, no direct financial benefit from the violation, and no application of certified codes of conduct or certification mechanisms.

Based on these factors, the initial calculation led to a higher fine, which was then significantly reduced for proportionality to **60,000 EUR (261,918 PLN)**. This amount represents approximately 0.6% of the maximum possible fine for this violation.

For the **Art. 30(1) and Art. 35(1) & (7) violations (Profiling Documentation)**, which were treated as a single coherent behavior:

- **Aggravating factors:** The significant gravity due to the type of sensitive data processed (financial, PESEL), the automated nature of profiling, the large scale of processing, and the fact that C. S.A. processes data for profit. The long duration of the violation (approx. 4.5 years) was also considered. Although unintentional, the lack of explicit profiling documentation was attributed to negligence by a professional entity. The categories of data involved (PESEL, financial data) are sensitive and require particular protection. Prior violations also served as an aggravating factor.
- **Mitigating factors:** C. S.A.'s good cooperation, including providing explanations and implementing corrective actions post-control by explicitly including profiling in their ROPA and DPIA.
- **Neutral factors:** Similar to the DPO violation, no direct harm to data subjects, no direct financial benefit, etc..

Following the EROD methodology and considering these factors, the fine was reduced for proportionality to **72,000 EUR (314,302 PLN)**. This amount represents approximately 0.72% of the maximum possible fine for these violations.

The **total combined fine** imposed on C. S.A. (Toyota Bank Polska S.A.) is **576,220 PLN**, equivalent to 132,000 EUR. Prezes UODO determined that these fines are effective, proportionate (representing about 0.13% of C. S.A.'s revenues), and deterrent, aiming to ensure future compliance by C. S.A. and other data administrators. The fines serve both a repressive function (punishment for non-compliance) and a preventive function (discouraging future violations).
