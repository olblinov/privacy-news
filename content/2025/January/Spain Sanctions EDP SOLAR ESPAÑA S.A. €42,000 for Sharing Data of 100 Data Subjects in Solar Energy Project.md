# Summary

The Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) initiated a sanctioning procedure against **EDP SOLAR ESPAÑA, S.A.**, triggered by a complaint regarding the dissemination of personal data of approximately **99 participants** in a collective solar energy project called "Barrio Solar". The AEPD found that EDP SOLAR ESPAÑA, S.A. had violated the **data minimization principle** under Article 5.1.c) of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) by including **excessive personal data** (such as names, DNI, mobile phone numbers, email addresses, postal addresses, CUPS, UTM, and cadastral references) in a collective agreement sent to all participants, which was not necessary for the stated purpose. The AEPD initially proposed a **€70,000 fine** but, due to EDP SOLAR ESPAÑA, S.A. recognizing responsibility and making a voluntary payment, the fine was reduced to **€42,000**, leading to the termination of the procedure. Additionally, EDP SOLAR ESPAÑA, S.A. was ordered to **adjust its data processing practices** to ensure only strictly necessary data is communicated to participants.

# Details

## Background

A complaint was filed with the AEPD on March 3, 2023, by an individual (A.A.A.) who, on February 22, 2023, received an email from ECODES.org related to the "Actur Barrio Solar" project. This email, sent to the complainant and "at least 99 different people," contained a PDF attachment with personal data including **name, surname, DNI, mobile phone number, email address, postal address, locality, and postal code** of all recipients. The email, bearing the EDP SOLAR logo, informed participants that their energy commercializers might request information about their self-consumption installations and stated that the attached documentation, including a "Contract of Coeficientes de Reparto (PDF)," should be sent to them.

ECODES, an independent non-profit organization working for sustainable development, explained that the "Barrio Solar" project is a collective self-consumption initiative for 100 neighbors. A convention between EDP ENERGÍA SAU (part of EDP España), the Zaragoza City Council, and ECODES established **EDP as the data controller** and **ECODES as the data processor**. Interested individuals provided initial contact data to ECODES, which then transferred it to EDP for contract formalization, where participants directly provided more personal data to EDP (e.g., domicile, CUPS, bank account number). EDP subsequently requested ECODES to forward an email with the relevant documents to the 100 participants, which ECODES did using BCC (blind carbon copy).

EDP, as the mandatary for 99 consumers (including the complainant, with five being legal entities), stated that the data sharing was necessary for the digital process of creating the collective self-consumption community in accordance with Royal Decree 244/2019 and the IDAE Guide. They argued that the final agreement document, signed digitally, had to be delivered with its integral content to avoid being detected as manipulated, which included some contact information derived from the electronic signature process. EDP stated that the complainant had granted a special power of attorney to EDP on July 5, 2021, to handle administrative procedures and sign the energy distribution agreement on their behalf.

The complainant exercised their right of access and also reiterated that they had not consented to the processing of their personal data by third parties beyond what was strictly necessary for the contractual relationship. EDP presented a consultation to the AEPD on February 28, 2023, seeking clarification on whether sharing certain personal data with other community members complied with data protection regulations, given the requirements of the energy regulations and the IDAE Guide. EDP interpreted the AEPD's response on March 14, 2023, as not opposing or finding their process contrary to data protection rules, noting that it was a "merely informative" and non-binding response.

ECODES identified that **100 people had their personal data communicated to the other 99 participants**. They categorized the disclosed data as general: **name, surname, DNI, mobile phone number, email address, postal address, locality, CUPS, and postal code**. ECODES believed the incident did not constitute a security breach because the disclosure was "deliberate, following the guidelines of Royal Decree 244/2019" and the email was sent via BCC. EDP maintained that no security incident or violation of rights occurred, as the data exchange was lawful and compliant with energy and data protection regulations.

Regarding actions taken, ECODES promptly responded to the affected party, informed EDP, and followed up. They also proposed that EDP directly send the distribution document to commercializers. EDP stated they did not consider it a security incident requiring notification but had proactively consulted the AEPD. EDP confirmed they had implemented security measures, including a Risk Analysis Treatment (RAT) and measures for personnel, access control, backups, and anonymization. ECODES's RAT described their data processing for "Barrio Solar" participants, including fields like NIF, CUPS, consumption, name, address, email, signature, and phone.

## Legal Analysis

The AEPD, competent to initiate and resolve this procedure, defined personal data, processing, and the role of the data controller as per GDPR. The AEPD confirmed that **EDP is the data controller** because it determined the purposes and means of the data processing, including the contractual relationship with participants and the means of communication.

The central issue revolved around the **principle of data minimization** (Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR), which states that personal data must be "adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes for which they are processed". The AEPD found that EDP had breached this principle.

The AEPD's investigation revealed that the attached PDF file, titled "(...)_Listado poderes.pdf," contained the "ACUERDO DE REPARTO DE ENERGÍA DE AUTOCONSUMO COLECTIVO" (Collective Self-Consumption Energy Distribution Agreement). This agreement included a table with NIF, CUPS, UTM, cadastral reference, and distribution coefficient for all 99 participants. Crucially, the same 319-page PDF also included individual "confirmation of power of representation" documents from each participant (generated by a third-party service) displaying their **phone numbers and email addresses**, along with their "power of representation" documents showing **names, surnames, DNI, and postal addresses**.

The AEPD concluded that:

- While the energy regulations (Royal Decree 244/2019 and IDAE Guide) require a distribution agreement with CUPS and coefficients, and potentially NIF, they do **not require the inclusion of phone numbers, email addresses, postal addresses, UTM, or cadastral references for all participants to be shared among themselves**.
- The context of the email was to instruct participants to send documents to their commercializers, but EDP, as the mandatary, was already empowered to handle these communications on behalf of the participants. Therefore, sharing all this additional data with every participant was unnecessary and disproportionate for the stated purpose.
- The inclusion of individual mandate and contract documents, which contained personal data, was deemed extraneous to the distribution agreement itself.
- The AEPD stated that while the distribution agreement might need names and surnames for identification by the distributor/commercializer, other data such as NIF, cadastral reference, participant's address, phone number, and email address were **unnecessary for all participants to know**.
- The AEPD found that there was **no intentionality** on EDP's part, but there was a **lack of diligence** in ensuring data protection compliance. EDP failed to recognize that more data was being processed and communicated than necessary, despite consulting the AEPD (which was done after the complaint and without a full understanding of the scope of the data shared).

## Sanction

The AEPD typified EDP's conduct as an **infraction of Article 5.1.c) of the GDPR** (data minimization principle), which is considered a **very serious infraction** under Article 83.5.a) of the GDPR and Article 72.1.a) of the LOPDGDD.

To determine the administrative fine, the AEPD considered several factors under Article 83.2 of the GDPR:

- **Nature, gravity, and duration of the infraction**: It led to a loss of control over personal data for 99 individuals (94 physical persons, 5 legal entities). The unnecessarily disclosed data included cadastral reference, UTM code, phone number, email, and addresses of the account holders.
- **Intentionality or negligence**: The AEPD determined there was no intentionality, but there was a **lack of diligence** on the part of EDP SOLAR ESPAÑA, S.A., failing to ensure data protection compliance.

The proposed fine was **€70,000**.

However, the AEPD offers reductions for recognition of responsibility and voluntary payment. EDP SOLAR ESPAÑA, S.A. acknowledged its responsibility and made a voluntary payment, taking advantage of **both 20% reductions**, which are cumulative. This reduced the final sanction amount to **€42,000**.

On December 5, 2024, EDP SOLAR ESPAÑA, S.A. proceeded to pay the **€42,000 fine**, which implied recognition of responsibility and renunciation of any administrative appeals against the sanction. This voluntary payment led to the **termination of the sanctioning procedure**.

In addition to the fine, the AEPD **ordered EDP SOLAR ESPAÑA, S.A. to adopt measures** to adjust its data processing to the regulations. Specifically, EDP must ensure that when communicating with participants, only **strictly necessary data** is shared. This measure must be implemented within 30 days of the resolution becoming firm and executive. Non-compliance with this order could result in further administrative sanctions.

EDP has indicated that, moving forward, to maximize privacy compliance, the copy of the agreement will be sent to participants **without including the justification of signature of the mandates** (which contain phone and email), thereby preventing these details from being visible to other participants. However, if a participant requests a full copy of the agreement, including the signature justifications, EDP will provide it, asserting that such an exchange would be compliant with energy regulations and AEPD criteria. ECODES is also awaiting clarification from the AEPD to modify its procedures for future projects.
