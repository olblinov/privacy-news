The case involves **ILVA A/S** and focuses on the **interpretation of the term ‘undertaking’** within Regulation (EU) 2016/679, known as the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), specifically concerning the **calculation of administrative fines** for data protection infringements. The core issue is whether, when a subsidiary infringes the GDPR, the fine should consider only the subsidiary's turnover or the **total worldwide annual turnover of the entire group** of which it forms part.

# Background of the Main Proceedings

The proceedings were initiated by the **Anklagemyndigheden (Public Prosecutor’s Office, Denmark)** against **ILVA A/S**. ILVA, a furniture store chain, is part of the **Lars Larsen Group**.

- **Alleged Infringement**: ILVA was charged with failing to fulfill its obligations under the GDPR as a data controller. Specifically, from May 2018 to January 2019, it allegedly improperly retained the personal data of at least 350,000 former customers.
- **Initial Fine Recommendation**: The Datatilsynet (Data Protection Agency, Denmark) recommended a fine of DKK 1.5 million (approximately EUR 201,000) for ILVA. This amount was calculated based on **both ILVA's turnover and the overall turnover of the Lars Larsen Group**. For reference, in the 2016/2017 financial year, the Lars Larsen Group's total turnover was approximately EUR 881 million, while ILVA's turnover was about EUR 241 million.
- **Aarhus District Court Judgment**: On 12 February 2021, the retten i Aarhus (Aarhus District Court, Denmark) found ILVA guilty and ordered a fine of DKK 100,000 (approximately EUR 13,400). The court considered ILVA to have acted negligently and decided that, since charges were only against ILVA and it was engaged in an independent retail activity, the **group's turnover should not be considered**.
- **Appeal and Referral**: The Public Prosecutor’s Office appealed this decision to the Vestre Landsret (High Court of Western Denmark), arguing that the term 'undertaking' in GDPR Article 83(4) to (6) requires reference to the group's turnover, in line with Articles 101 and 102 TFEU and recital 150 of the GDPR. ILVA, conversely, contended that only its own turnover should be considered, especially since the parent company was not charged. #enforcement_pattern #fine  The referring court found the GDPR unclear on this matter, leading to the preliminary ruling request.

# Legal Context (GDPR Provisions)

The request for a preliminary ruling specifically concerns the interpretation of **Article 83(4) to (6) of the GDPR**.

- **Principles and Lawfulness of Processing**: Article 5 lays down basic principles for personal data processing, and Article 6 defines conditions for lawful processing.
- **Supervisory Authority Powers**: Article 58(2)(i) grants supervisory authorities the power to **impose administrative fines pursuant to Article 83**.
- **General Conditions for Administrative Fines (Article 83)**:
    - **Effectiveness, Proportionality, Dissuasiveness**: Article 83(1) mandates that fines must be **effective, proportionate, and dissuasive**.
    - **Criteria for Determining Amount**: Article 83(2) lists factors to consider when imposing a fine, including:
        - The nature, gravity, and duration of the infringement.
        - The number of data subjects affected and level of damage.
        - Intentional or negligent character.
        - Actions taken to mitigate damage.
        - Degree of responsibility of the controller/processor.
        - Previous infringements.
        - Cooperation with the supervisory authority.
        - Categories of personal data affected.
        - Manner in which the infringement became known.
        - Compliance with previous measures.
        - Adherence to codes of conduct or certification mechanisms.
        - Any other aggravating or mitigating factors, such as financial benefits gained or losses avoided.
    - **Maximum Fine Limits**:
        - For certain infringements (Article 83(4)), fines can be up to **EUR 10,000,000 or 2% of the total worldwide annual turnover of the preceding financial year**, whichever is higher, in the case of an undertaking.
        - For other infringements (Article 83(5)) and non-compliance with supervisory authority orders (Article 83(6)), fines can be up to **EUR 20,000,000 or 4% of the total worldwide annual turnover of the preceding financial year**, whichever is higher, in the case of an undertaking.
- **Concept of ‘Undertaking’ (Recital 150)**: Recital 150 states that for the purpose of imposing administrative fines on an undertaking, the term ‘undertaking’ should be understood **in accordance with Articles 101 and 102 TFEU**.
- **National Legal Systems (Recital 151 and Article 83(9))**: Denmark's legal system, along with Estonia's, does not allow for administrative fines as set out in the GDPR. Therefore, Article 83(9) allows fines to be **initiated by the supervisory authority and imposed by competent national courts** as criminal penalties, provided they have an **equivalent effect to administrative fines** and are effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. Danish law (Law No 502) supplements the GDPR, allowing fines or imprisonment and stating that Article 83(2) must be followed when imposing penalties. Companies (legal persons) can be held criminally liable.

# Questions Referred for Preliminary Ruling

The Vestre Landsret referred two questions to the Court of Justice:

1. Whether the term **‘undertaking’ in Article 83(4) to (6) of the GDPR** should be understood as an undertaking within the meaning of Articles 101 and 102 TFEU, in conjunction with recital 150 of the GDPR and EU competition law case-law, meaning **any entity engaged in an economic activity, regardless of its legal status or financing**.
2. If the answer to Question 1 is affirmative, whether Article 83(4) to (6) of the GDPR should be interpreted as meaning that, when imposing a fine on an undertaking, **regard must be had to the total worldwide annual turnover of the economic entity of which the undertaking forms part**, or only the total worldwide annual turnover of the undertaking itself.

# Court's Consideration and Ruling

The Court examined the questions together, noting its prior judgment in _Deutsche Wohnen_ (5 December 2023, C‑807/21), which addressed aspects of Article 83 of the GDPR.

- **Relevance of 'Undertaking' Concept**: The Court clarified that the concept of ‘undertaking’ within the meaning of Articles 101 and 102 TFEU is **relevant specifically for determining the _amount_ of the administrative fine** under Article 83(4) to (6) of the GDPR, as indicated by recital 150. It does not impact whether or under what conditions a fine can be imposed on a legal person, which is exhaustively regulated by Article 58(2) and Article 83(1) to (6).
- **Definition of 'Undertaking'**: For competition rules (Articles 101 and 102 TFEU), an **‘undertaking’ covers any entity engaged in an economic activity, irrespective of its legal status or financing**. This concept designates an **economic unit**, even if it consists of several legal or natural persons, forming a unitary organization pursuing a specific economic aim. #enforcement_pattern #fine 
- **Calculating the Maximum Fine**: When the recipient of the fine is or forms part of an 'undertaking' (as defined by Articles 101 and 102 TFEU), the **maximum amount of the administrative fine is calculated based on a percentage of the _undertaking’s total worldwide annual turnover_** in the preceding business year.
- **Actual Fine Calculation and Economic Capacity**: The determination of the _maximum_ amount is distinct from the _actual_ calculation of the fine for a specific infringement. Article 83(1) requires fines to be **effective, proportionate, and dissuasive**. Article 83(2) lists factors related to the infringement and the controller's behavior. To ensure the fine is effective, proportionate, and dissuasive, it must consider not only these specific factors but also, where appropriate, the **actual or material economic capacity of the person on whom the fine is imposed**. To assess these conditions, it is necessary to **take into account whether that person forms part of an undertaking** as defined by Articles 101 and 102 TFEU.
- **Applicability to Criminal Fines in Denmark**: This interpretation also applies where infringements are penalized by fines imposed by **competent national courts as criminal penalties**, as is the case in Denmark. Article 83(9) ensures that such legal remedies are effective and have an equivalent effect to administrative fines, and the fines imposed must be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive. While national criminal procedural rules and the principle of proportionality from the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union must be respected, the application of the 'undertaking' concept for calculating fines does not present obstacles in criminal proceedings, as it helps strike a fair balance between protecting personal data and the rights of the accused economic unit.

The Court therefore ruled:

**Article 83(4) to (6) of the GDPR, read in the light of recital 150, must be interpreted as meaning that the term ‘undertaking’ in those provisions corresponds to the concept of ‘undertaking’, within the meaning of Articles 101 and 102 TFEU**. This means that **where a fine for infringement of the GDPR is imposed on a data controller which is or forms part of an undertaking, the maximum amount of the fine is to be determined on the basis of a percentage of the undertaking’s total worldwide annual turnover in the preceding business year**. Furthermore, **the concept of ‘undertaking’ must also be taken into account to assess the actual or material economic capacity of the recipient of the fine and thus to ascertain whether the fine is at the same time effective, proportionate and dissuasive**.
