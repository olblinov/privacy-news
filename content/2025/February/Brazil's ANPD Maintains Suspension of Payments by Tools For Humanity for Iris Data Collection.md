# Summary

Brazil's National Data Protection Authority (ANPD) has upheld its decision to suspend Tools For Humanity (TFH), the company behind the World ID project, from offering financial compensation (including WorldCoin cryptocurrency or any other format) for collecting individuals' iris data in Brazil. The ANPD rejected TFH's appeal for an additional 45 days to implement changes, mandating immediate compliance. The core of the ANPD's reasoning is that providing financial incentives for sensitive biometric data collection compromises the "free" nature of consent, rendering it invalid under the Brazilian General Data Protection Law (LGPD). This measure is deemed essential to protect data subjects' informational self-determination, especially given the sensitive, irreversible nature of iris data, and the potential vulnerability of individuals influenced by immediate financial needs.

# Details

## Background

The Director Board of the National Data Protection Authority (ANPD) denied an administrative appeal filed by Tools For Humanity (TFH), the entity responsible for the World ID project. This decision, published on February 11, 2025, confirmed the immediate suspension of any financial compensation, whether in WorldCoin (WLD) cryptocurrency or other formats, for digital identities (World ID) created by collecting individuals' iris data in Brazil. TFH had sought an additional 45 days to modify its application and halt the financial offerings, but this request was rejected. The ANPD determined that the suspension could be implemented immediately through other means, such as postponing scheduled appointments, until the application adjustments are completed.

The administrative process began in November 2024 to assess the legality of processing biometric data for World ID. #sensitive  The service aims to allow users to verify their uniqueness as a human being for authentication purposes on digital platforms, differentiating them from artificial intelligence systems or bots. The process involves downloading the World ID application, presenting a QR code to a specialized iris-reading camera called an "Orb," and undergoing facial and eye scans. If the Orb confirms the user is a living human, it creates a unique, anonymized binary iris code, stores fragments in separate databases, encrypts the photos and metadata into a "Personal Custody Package (PCP)," and transfers this package to the user's mobile device before deleting personal data from the Orb. Following this, users become eligible to receive WorldCoins, which can be converted into fiat currency, reportedly valued at approximately R$ 600.00.

TFH argued in its appeal that it does not retain personal data, as iris codes are either anonymized or encrypted on the user's device. #biometrics  #anonymization  They asserted that Worldcoin (WLD) is not "money" but rather a "cultural significance" and an incentive for using the World network, akin to loyalty programs. TFH claimed that users' free consent and choice are guaranteed because they are not coerced, can use the wallet without iris collection, provide granular consent, and face no repercussions for refusing or revoking consent. #consent  The company maintained that the risks associated with biometric data collection are "practically nonexistent" and that the collection itself is a data subject's right. Finally, TFH requested 45 days to implement "structural changes" to their technological architecture, which might lead to a Brazil-specific application. TFH also stated it had already complied with the initial order to indicate a data protection officer on its website.

## Legal Analysis

The ANPD's review of the appeal primarily focused on two points: (i) whether the financial compensation offered by TFH for iris collection invalidates the free consent of data subjects, and (ii) the company's request for additional time to comply with the ANPD's determination. This decision is a precautionary measure based on a preliminary legal analysis, considering the potential and immediate risks, especially concerning the validity of consent.

Under Brazil's LGPD, the processing of sensitive personal data, such as biometric data like the iris, requires the specific and explicit consent of the data subject (Art. 11, I, LGPD). For consent to be valid, it must be **free, informed, and unequivocal** (Art. 5º, XII, LGPD). The ANPD defines "free consent" as the data subject having a genuine choice to accept or refuse data processing without negative consequences or undue interventions from the controller that could compromise their autonomous will.

The ANPD concluded that the financial compensation offered by TFH constitutes an **undue interference** that affects the autonomous manifestation of the data subject's will, thereby rendering the consent not "free". #consent #change  This type of interference is particularly problematic as it can exploit information asymmetries and the vulnerability of data subjects, compelling them to consent to the collection of their personal data to meet immediate financial needs, often without fully understanding the risks or purpose of the collection. The ANPD also cited the European Data Protection Board (EDPB), which views the treatment of fundamental data protection rights as "negotiable commodities" as incompatible with the fundamental right to data protection, which should be accessible to all regardless of their financial status.

Evidence indicated that many individuals provided their biometric data primarily for the financial compensation, rather than to participate in a global digital identity project. Media reports highlighted individuals admitting they "came for the money" and were unaware of the project's purpose or associated risks. Furthermore, the ANPD noted that collection points are strategically located near public transport hubs and in peripheral areas of São Paulo, which potentially exacerbates the influence of financial compensation on individuals' consent, especially those in more vulnerable economic positions. #consent 

The ANPD found that the consent for processing sensitive personal data was largely driven by the financial offer. This leads to a "vício de consentimento" (vitiated consent), a practice expressly prohibited by Art. 8, § 3º, of the LGPD, thus justifying the ANPD's preventive action to protect data subjects' rights. The burden of proving that consent was obtained in compliance with the LGPD rests with the controller (Art. 8, § 2º, LGPD), and TFH failed to demonstrate this in its appeal.

The ANPD also emphasized the significant risks associated with this data processing, including: (i) the use of emergent and innovative technology with unknown effects, already under scrutiny by data protection authorities globally; (ii) the collection of sensitive and irreversible biometric data (as an iris scan, unlike a password, cannot be changed); and (iii) the use of blockchain technology, which typically prevents the modification or deletion of recorded information. Although TFH acknowledged these risks and stated it employs mitigation measures such as Zero Knowledge Proofs and industry-standard cybersecurity, the ANPD's concerns remained. #consent 

## Sanction

The ANPD's Director Board voted to **deny TFH's administrative appeal**. As a result, the **suspension of financial compensation** (including WorldCoin – WLD or any other format) for World IDs created through iris data collection in Brazil **is maintained** as initially ordered. The ANPD also **rejected TFH's request for an additional 45 days** to comply with the preventive measure.

The suspension of compensation **must be implemented immediately** upon notification. Compliance can be achieved through "any available legal means," including the **postponement of appointments** for iris collection, until TFH implements effective technical solutions within its application. TFH is required to submit an official declaration, signed by a legal representative, confirming the suspension of financial compensation within **10 business days** of being notified of the ANPD's decision. All other directives and warnings previously established in the initial Dispatch Decision nº 3/2025/FIS/CGF remain in effect. The Director-President and other Directors of the ANPD explicitly concurred with the rapporteur's vote to deny the appeal and maintain the suspension.
