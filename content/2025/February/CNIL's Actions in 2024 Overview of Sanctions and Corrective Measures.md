In 2024, the CNIL (Commission Nationale de l'Informatique et des Libertés) significantly intensified its enforcement actions, marking a notable increase in corrective measures.

# Overall Trends and Key Figures

The year 2024 saw **a sharp increase in the total number of corrective measures pronounced by the CNIL**, with the number of sanctions doubling. The number of compliance orders and reprimands also continued to increase.

**Key figures for 2024 include:**

- A **total of 331 corrective decisions**.
- **87 sanctions** issued, which is double the 42 sanctions issued in 2023 and more than four times the 21 sanctions in 2022.
- **€55,212,400 in cumulative fines**.
- **180 compliance orders**, an unprecedented number.
- **64 reprimands**, also an unprecedented number.

# Sanctions Imposed

Of the 87 sanctions handed down in 2024, 18 were issued under the ordinary procedure, and **69 were issued under the simplified procedure**, which was introduced in 2022. These sanctions included:

- 75 fines, 14 of which included injunctions under penalty.
- 8 decisions to liquidate an injunction (meaning payment of a fine for non-compliance with a previous order).
- 4 reminders of the law.
- 12 of these decisions were made public.

## Recurring Infringements Leading to Sanctions

The CNIL's restricted committee focused on several recurring infringements:

- **Commercial Prospecting:** The CNIL reiterated that organizations using personal data for electronic commercial prospecting, whether from partners or data brokers, **must ensure that the data collection methods comply with the GDPR**, including obtaining consent and providing clear information when necessary. This also applies to e-mail providers who insert advertisements in user inboxes, requiring prior consent. #direct_marketing 
- **Health Data Special Protection Requirements:** The CNIL issued decisions concerning the anonymization of health data, ruling that even large-scale data collections where the organization is unaware of individual identities remain **pseudonymous and non-anonymous if linked through an identifier**, as this presents a re-identification risk. Therefore, such data is still considered personal data subject to the law. #sensitive 
- **Accuracy of Data in Government Departments:** Three warnings were issued to government departments for **failing to ensure the accuracy of data in their databases**, particularly regarding criminal records that were not updated to reflect acquittal decisions.
- **Breach of Personal Data Security:** 11 organizations were sanctioned for **not implementing necessary data security measures**. This included issues like using insufficiently robust passwords, storing passwords in clear text, lacking an authorization policy, or using obsolete TLS protocol versions. #security 
- **Cookie Consent Mechanisms:** 11 organizations were penalized for **not allowing users to refuse cookies as easily as to accept them**, often by making the refusal mechanism more complex. #consent #cookies 

## Simplified Sanction Procedure

The simplified sanction procedure saw significant development in 2024, with the chair of the restricted committee (or a member) issuing **69 sanctions**, nearly three times the number from 2023. These resulted in:

- 50 fines.
- 12 fines with injunctions.
- 6 liquidations of injunctions.
- One call to order. The total amount of fines under this procedure was **€715,500**.

The main failures addressed under the simplified procedure were:

- **Failure to cooperate with the CNIL:** **27 organizations** were sanctioned for failing to respond to CNIL's requests.
- **Failure to respond to individuals' exercise of rights:** **23 decisions** concerned non-compliance with requests for deletion, opposition, or access, with access requests alone accounting for 16 decisions.
- **Failure to minimize data:** 10 decisions addressed issues such as excessive comments, systematic recording of entire phone conversations, or permanent video surveillance of employees at their workstations.
- Other issues included inadequate security measures and video surveillance of employees at their workstations.

# Compliance Orders

In 2024, the CNIL issued an increased number of **180 compliance orders**, which are decisions from the CNIL’s chair ordering an organization to comply within a set deadline. Major themes addressed in these formal notices included:

- **Access to the digital patient record (DPI):** Formal notices were served on several healthcare establishments to secure their computerized patient files, emphasizing that **patient data should only be accessible to those with a justified need to know**.
- **Failure to respond to a request to exercise a right:** Due to numerous complaints, dozens of organizations received compliance orders to respond to requests for data access, opposition, or deletion. In cases of inaction, a simplified sanction procedure was initiated against several of them.

# European Cooperation

The CNIL also engaged in European cooperation:

- 7 of the sanction decisions were adopted in cooperation with the CNIL's European counterparts, as part of the **one-stop shop mechanism provided by the GDPR**.
- The CNIL examined **12 draft decisions from European counterparts** that related to processing operations involving individuals residing in France.
