# Summary

The French data protection authority, CNIL, reclassified data handled by QWANT as personal data, not anonymous data, in 2019. This reclassification stemmed from a complaint and subsequent investigations which found that technical data transmitted by QWANT to Microsoft, although processed with measures to reduce re-identification, was only pseudonymized, not anonymized. As a result, QWANT's initial privacy policy was deemed inaccurate and incomplete, failing to specify the advertising purpose or legal basis for data transmission, violating GDPR transparency and information obligations. However, instead of imposing a fine, CNIL issued a **"rappel aux obligations légales" (reminder of legal obligations)**, acknowledging QWANT's good faith, cooperation, intention to minimize personal data use, and subsequent efforts to update its policies and reduce re-identification risks.

# Details

## Background

QWANT, a French company, launched its search engine in 2013. The company initially claimed that its search engine did not collect any personal data, especially concerning the display of advertisements linked to search queries. Data used for selling advertising space through MICROSOFT was presented as anonymous.

In March 2019, CNIL received a complaint against QWANT, alleging that the data collected constituted personal data rather than anonymous data, and consequently, personal data regulations were not being respected. Based on this complaint, CNIL conducted two controls in 2019, followed by extensive discussions with QWANT. These investigations revealed that QWANT transmitted primarily technical data, such as **truncated or hashed IP addresses used to generate an identifier**, to MICROSOFT #personal_data . This data enabled MICROSOFT to display contextual advertisements related to the user's search, count ad displays, and offer its own search results if QWANT's results were insufficient. QWANT asserted that the technical measures it implemented anonymized the collected data before transmission to MICROSOFT, and therefore, it believed it was not transmitting personal data. The initial version of QWANT's privacy policy stated that user queries were immediately "anonymized". #anonymization 

## Legal Analysis

In 2019, after in-depth analyses, and considering the highly technical nature of the case and the fact that it was the first such mechanism examined by CNIL, its president determined that, despite QWANT's deployed measures, the data transmitted to MICROSOFT could **not be qualified as anonymous**. CNIL had already classified the data processed by QWANT as personal data, not anonymous data, in 2019. Consequently, QWANT modified its privacy policy in 2020.

CNIL concluded that the dataset transmitted to MICROSOFT was **pseudonymized, not anonymized**, despite the significant precautions taken in 2019 to prevent re-identification. #anonymization  At the time of the controls, QWANT's privacy policy incorrectly stated that the data was anonymous. Because QWANT considered personal data regulations inapplicable, its policy **did not mention the advertising purpose of data transmission** to MICROSOFT #change #anonymization #transparency , nor the legal basis for this processing. Additionally, until February 21, 2020, the Italian and German versions of the privacy policy lacked information present in the French and English versions.

Therefore, at the time of the control, the information in QWANT's privacy policy regarding data transmission to MICROSOFT was **inaccurate and incomplete**. CNIL found that QWANT had thereby failed to meet its **obligations of transparency and information** as stipulated in Articles 12 and 13 of the GDPR. Due to the cross-border nature of the processing, CNIL shared its conclusions with its European counterparts in 2020.

## Sanction

CNIL issued a **"rappel aux obligations légales" (reminder of legal obligations)** against QWANT based on its 2019 findings. This measure is one of the corrective actions available to the CNIL president, along with formal notices and warnings, but it is **not considered a sanction**.

The decision to issue a reminder was justified by several factors. QWANT's stated intention was to develop a search engine that used minimal personal data, relying on **contextual rather than behavioral advertising**, which implies a much lower level of intrusiveness. The data transmitted to MICROSOFT was primarily technical and **not retained to build user advertising profiles**, meaning there was no long-term tracking of user activity. While CNIL ultimately deemed the data pseudonymized, it recognized that QWANT had implemented **numerous technical measures to significantly reduce the risk of re-identification**. The non-compliance was attributed to an **initial analytical error in data qualification**, rather than an intentional attempt by QWANT to circumvent GDPR provisions.

Furthermore, CNIL noted that QWANT updated its privacy policy following discussions with CNIL on October 22, 2020. The updated policy explicitly mentioned the transmission of **"pseudonymous" data** to MICROSOFT and clearly stated the legal basis and advertising purpose of this data transmission. QWANT also updated its privacy policy in various languages during 2020 to ensure consistent information across all versions. Finally, CNIL's president considered QWANT's **good faith and cooperation** throughout the entire procedure. These elements led the president to conclude that a reminder of legal obligations was a **proportionate corrective measure** for the company.
