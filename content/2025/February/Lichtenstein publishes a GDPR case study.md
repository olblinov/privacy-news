|  🇱🇮   | Verwaltungsgerichtshof (VGH), 3 September 2021, Nr. 2021/031 and 2021/032                                                                                                                                      | **Anonymity of a complainant** in a GDPR complaint (Art. 77 GDPR) is not always required to be disclosed to the data controller. The data protection authority must assess if withholding identity hinders the controller's defense rights or GDPR compliance. The **EFTA Court** clarified that the gratuity of the complaint procedure under Art. 77 GDPR extends to subsequent appeal procedures, and **costs must not be imposed** on the data subject if they become a party in such proceedings.                                                                                                             |
|  🇱🇮   | Verwaltungsgerichtshof (VGH), 15 December 2023, Nr. 2022/109 – Videoüberwachung Freizeitanlagen                                                                                                                | **Video surveillance of leisure facilities** is only permissible to a very limited extent. The VGH confirmed that such surveillance was excessive and not data protection compliant, as the **data subjects' interest in using the facility unmonitored largely outweighs the municipality's legitimate interest**. Permitted surveillance is limited to night-time or specific, confined problem areas, requiring an **appropriately short storage duration** for recordings.                                                                                                                                     |
|  🇱🇮   | Staatsgerichtshof (StGH), 26 March 2024, Nr. 2023/106 – PEID-Nummer                                                                                                                                            | **PEID numbers are automatically generated and contain no personal data**. The StGH ruled that random matches with personal data do not constitute a privacy violation, as the number itself does not inherently contain personal data. #personal_data                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
|  🇱🇮   | Staatsgerichtshof (StGH), 26 June 2023, Nr. 2023/016 – Gerichtsstand für Löschungsbegehren                                                                                                                     | A **top-level domain in a different state does not automatically alter jurisdiction** for deletion requests. The courts found that a .li domain did not establish jurisdiction in Liechtenstein for a deletion request against a defendant seated in Ireland.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
|  🇱🇮   | Staatsgerichtshof (StGH), 2 September 2024, Nr. 2024/056 – Arbeitsrecht und Datenschutzbeauftragte                                                                                                             | **Data protection officers are protected from dismissal due to fulfilling their duties** (Art. 38 Abs. 3 GDPR, Art. 7 Abs. 3 DSG), but this **protection does not prevent ordinary termination** if the termination is demonstrably independent of their data protection function and not a retaliatory dismissal.  #dpo                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundesverfassungsgericht (BVerfGE), 19 March 2020, 1 BvQ 1/20 – Krankenversicherungsdaten                                                                                                                      | **Use of health insurance data after pseudonymization/anonymization is permissible**. The court found that the disadvantages of temporarily applying new regulations allowing such data use, even if later found unconstitutional, did not clearly outweigh the disadvantages of their non-application if they were later found constitutional. #anonymization #sensitive                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundesverfassungsgericht (BVerfGE), 19 May 2020, 1 BvR 2835/17 – Bestandsdaten, Fernmeldeaufklärung                                                                                                            | **Overseas telecommunications intelligence by the Federal Intelligence Service (BND) violated fundamental rights** under the German Basic Law. The court found that the German state's authority is bound by fundamental rights even outside German territory, particularly regarding telecommunications secrecy (Art. 10 Abs. 1 GG) and freedom of the press (Art. 5 Abs. 1 Satz 2 GG), extending to foreigners abroad. Existing legal bases for data collection, processing, and transfer lacked sufficient purpose limitation, protective measures (e.g., for journalists), and independent oversight.          |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundesverfassungsgericht (BVerfGE), 27 May 2020, 1 BvR 1873/13 and 1 BvR 2618/13 – Bestandsdatenauskunft                                                                                                       | **Regulations on inventory data disclosure in Germany were unconstitutional**. While generally permissible, disclosure requires proportionate legal bases for both transmission by telecommunication providers and retrieval by authorities. These rules must sufficiently limit data usage, including **thresholds for intervention** and **significant legal protection**. Disclosure of dynamic IP addresses, due to higher intervention weight, must serve the protection or enforcement of legal goods of at least emphasized importance. Lack of these conditions made existing provisions unconstitutional. |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundesverwaltungsgericht (BVerwG), 16 September 2020, Nr. Az.: 6 C 10.19 – Auskunftsanspruch                                                                                                                   | The **data protection right to information (Art. 15 GDPR) is a highly personal right** and cannot be exercised by third parties, such as an insolvency administrator. The purpose of this right is to allow the individual data subject to control and verify the lawfulness of their own data processing.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundesgerichtshof (BGH), 28 May 2020, I ZR 7/16 – Cookie-Einwilligung                                                                                                                                          | **Active consent is required for cookies used to create user profiles for advertising or market research**. A pre-ticked checkbox does not constitute valid consent, both under previous law and the GDPR. This principle applies in Liechtenstein as well, as such cookies cannot be justified by legitimate interest under Art. 6 Abs. 1 Bst. f GDPR. #cookies #consent                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundesgerichtshof (BGH), 23 June 2020, KVR 69/19 – Soziale Medien, Nutzerdaten                                                                                                                                 | The **Federal Cartel Office's prohibition on Facebook's processing of user data without consent** from off-platform internet use **is enforceable under competition law**. #adtech #consent #legitimate_interest                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundesgerichtshof (BGH), 22 February 2022, Nr. VI ZR 14/21 – Auskunftsrecht, Identität Hinweisgeber                                                                                                            | Tenants **may have a right to information regarding the identity of a complaining neighbor**. A **balancing of interests** between the right to information (Art. 15 Abs. 1 Halbsatz 2 Bst. g DS-GVO) and the whistleblower's privacy rights (Art. 7 Abs. 1 and Art. 8 Charter) is necessary. The whistleblower's interest in confidentiality generally recedes if they made false or reckless statements about the data subject's personal data.                                                                                                                                                                  |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundesgerichtshof (BGH), 23 May 2023, Nr. VI ZR 476/18 – Voraussetzungen und Anforderungen eines Auslistungsanspruchs                                                                                          | A **delisting request does not require prior clarification of the accuracy of the listed content**. The applicant merely needs to provide **relevant and sufficient evidence** supporting their request and demonstrating that the information is obviously incorrect or significantly so.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundessozialgericht, 8 October 2019, B 1 A 3/19 R – Sozialdaten, Krankenkassen                                                                                                                                 | **Protection of social data prevents health insurance funds from involving private third parties**. Health insurance funds cannot offer their own service management in competition with approved service providers, nor can they involve private third parties for core tasks. This also constitutes a violation of national social data protection laws, as social data may only be collected and processed for legally compliant and explicitly defined purposes.                                                                                                                                               |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundesfinanzhof, 29 August 2019, X S 6/19 – Akteneinsichtsrecht, Finanzverfahren                                                                                                                               | **No right to inspect files under GDPR in judicial proceedings**. Beyond specific procedural rules, rights to file inspection cannot be derived from Art. 15 GDPR in judicial proceedings.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundesfinanzhof, 7 April 2020, II B 82/19 – Nichtanwendung GDPR bei Steuer-fahndung                                                                                                                            | The **GDPR is not applicable in matters of tax investigation** (Steuerfahndung). Activities related to investigating tax offenses fall under Art. 2 Abs. 2 Buchst. d GDPR, thus GDPR-based data protection requests against the tax office in this context have no legal basis.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
|  🇩🇪   | Bundesarbeitsgericht, 7 May 2019, 1 ABR 53/17 – Einsichtsrecht Betriebsrat in Bruttoentgeltlisten                                                                                                              | The **works council's right to inspect gross wage and salary lists is not limited to anonymized lists**. The GDPR does not prevent inspection of lists with named employees. Processing of employee data is permissible if necessary for exercising the rights and duties of employee representation arising from a law (Art. 26 Abs. 1 Satz 1 BDSG). #employment                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|  🇦🇹   | Verfassungsgerichtshof (VfGH), 4 March 2021, E 4037/2020 – Auskunftsrecht, Meinungsäusserungs- und Informationsfreiheit, Journalismus                                                                          | The **public interest in information can, under certain circumstances, override personal confidentiality interests**. In a case involving a journalist's request for salary information of former parliamentarians, the VfGH ruled that the right to information in journalistic activity, serving public interest, can take precedence over the confidentiality interests of the individuals concerned.                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
|  🇦🇹   | Verwaltungsgerichtshof (VwGH), 7 September 2021, Ra 2020/11/0213 – Verarbeitung von Gesundheitsdaten im öffentlichen Interesse                                                                                 | **Driver's license authorities may process health data without consent if it's for a task in the public interest**, such as improving road safety. The VwGH referred to Art. 6 Abs. 1 Bst. e GDPR and Art. 9 Abs. 1 Bst. g GDPR, confirming that road safety is a significant public interest that obviates the need for consent.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|  🇦🇹   | Verwaltungsgerichtshof (VwGH), 14 December 2021, Ro 2021/04/0007 – Parteiaffinität als besonders sensibles personenbezogenes Datum                                                                             | Even a **probabilistically calculated party affiliation constitutes a special category of personal data** under Art. 9 Abs. 1 GDPR. Processing such data without consent or a valid exception is unlawful, as it can imply a presumed political leaning for an identifiable person, falling under the protective scope of Art. 9 Abs. 1 GDPR. #personal_data #change                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
|  🇦🇹   | Verwaltungsgerichtshof (VwGH), 19 October 2022, Ro 2022/04/0001 – Geheimhaltungsverletzung in der Vergangenheit                                                                                                | A **data protection authority can formally determine a past breach of confidentiality**, even if it no longer exists. Unlike rights aiming at performance (e.g., erasure), the right to confidentiality is not aimed at an action by the controller. Thus, the DSB is competent to investigate and establish past violations of confidentiality.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
|  🇦🇹   | Bundesverwaltungsgericht (BVwG), 25 November 2019, W211 2210485-1/10E – Videoüberwachung, keine Öffnungsklausel in GDPR                                                                                        | **National data protection provisions that violate EU law must not be applied if they lack an "opening clause" in the GDPR**. The BVwG ruled that Austrian DSG provisions on video surveillance were non-compliant with EU law due to this, basing its decision on Art. 5 Abs. 1 Bst. e, Art. 6 Abs. 1 Bst. f, Art. 5 Abs. 1 Bst. a i.V.m. Art. 12 and 13 GDPR.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
|  🇦🇹   | Bundesverwaltungsgericht (BVwG), 2 March 2020, W211 2217212-1/9E – kein Vorrang von Verwarnung gegenüber Geldbusse                                                                                             | There is **no legal priority for a warning over a fine for first-time GDPR violations**, contrary to what some national laws might suggest. While proportionality (Art. 83 GDPR) applies, a warning is not appropriate for serious first offenses. The BVwG affirmed that national provisions cannot impose a binding precedence for warnings where the GDPR does not.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
|  🇦🇹   | Bundesverwaltungsgericht (BVwG), 29 April 2020, W274 2228071-1/6E – Exzessive Beschwerdeführung                                                                                                                | A **complaint can be rejected if it is manifestly unfounded or excessive** (e.g., frequent repetition, such as 90 complaints since June 2018 in the original case). The data protection authority is not obligated to conduct a substantive review in such cases.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|  🇦🇹   | Bundesverwaltungsgericht (BVwG), 28 May 2020, W274 2230370-1/4E – Zeugen Jehovas, Anwendbarkeit GDPR, Auskunftsrecht                                                                                           | The **GDPR does not apply to a sealed envelope containing a notice of withdrawal from a religious community**, as it is not a file system in the GDPR sense. Furthermore, the right to information (Auskunftsrecht) is not intended to provide access to administrative documents, which internal documents of a religious community's exclusion procedure are equated to.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|  🇦🇹   | Bundesverwaltungsgericht (BVwG), 21 December 2021, W258 2238615-1 – Mitarbeiterexzess                                                                                                                          | If **employees access a database with personal data for non-official purposes, they are considered controllers themselves**, not the employer. The employer is not liable for data processing conducted without authorization or for purposes unrelated to the employee's duties, in which case the employees are solely responsible.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
|  🇦🇹   | Oberster Gerichtshof (OGH), 20 December 2018, 6 Ob 131/18k – gerichtliche Geltendmachung von Löschrecht; keine Haushaltsausnahme im zivilgerichtlichen Verfahren                                               | The **household privilege does not apply in civil court proceedings**. The **right to erasure (Art. 17 Abs. 1 Bst. a GDPR) can be enforced in court proceedings**, as data no longer necessary for the original purposes must be deleted. Sensitive health and personal data used in legal proceedings are not covered by the household exemption if they are not exclusively for family purposes.                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
|  🇦🇹   | Oberster Gerichtshof (OGH), 29 August 2019, 6 Ob 152/19z – Medienprivileg, Urheberrecht                                                                                                                        | **Newspapers cannot justify unrestricted use of images by invoking media privilege** under the GDPR. National copyright law (e.g., § 78 UrhG) coexists with data protection law, addressing personality rights, and still requires a balancing of interests. The GDPR's total exception for journalistic content does not permit boundless image exploitation without considering personality rights.                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
|  🇦🇹   | Oberster Gerichtshof (OGH), 27 November 2019, 6 Ob 150/19f – Zivilrechtliche Abhilfemassnahme gegen Videoüberwachung                                                                                           | **Unlawful video surveillance due to constant surveillance pressure** can be addressed through civil law remedies. Cameras that record beyond one's own property, including public access ways or private gardens, fall outside the household privilege and create an unacceptable "surveillance pressure," violating privacy rights and the right to one's own image.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
|  🇦🇹   | Oberster Gerichtshof (OGH), 29 August 2022, 6 Ob 198/21t – Informationsfreiheit und Datenschutz bei Bewertungsportalen                                                                                         | The **significant public interest in information on review portals generally outweighs individual privacy interests**, especially when it concerns publicly perceptible professional practice, not private life. A comprehensive balancing of interests (Art. 6 Abs. 1 lit. f GDPR) must consider users' legitimate interests in information and the service provider's commercial interests against the data subject's privacy rights.                                                                                                                                                                            |
|  🇦🇹   | Oberster Gerichtshof (OGH), 23 October 2023, 6 Ob 205/22y – Keine Herausgabe eines verletzenden Videos                                                                                                         | Only the **deletion of an infringing video can be demanded, not its handover**. While personality rights violations may lead to claims for destruction or erasure of unlawfully obtained recordings, there is no right to the handover of such recordings.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|  🇫🇷   | Conseil constitutionnel, 27 December 2019, N° 2019-796 DC – Unzulässigkeit automatisierter Datenverarbeitung durch die Steuerverwaltung                                                                        | **Automated data collection and processing by tax authorities from public online sources for investigating tax offenses is inadmissible** if it broadly impacts privacy. Only content intentionally published by the person, and strictly necessary for the purpose, may be collected, excluding sensitive data (Art. 9 GDPR). The Constitutional Council found provisions allowing systematic collection and processing of large data volumes disproportionate when authorities already had means to identify violations.                                                                                         |
|  🇫🇷   | Conseil constitutionnel, 11 May 2020, N° 2020-800 DC – COVID-19, Verarbeitung von Gesundheitsdaten                                                                                                             | The **processing of health data for contact tracing purposes during a health emergency is permissible** despite being without consent, given the recognized constitutional goal of health protection and the necessity for pandemic containment. However, access for reasons not directly related to pandemic combat is unjustified, and temporal limitations must be respected.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
|  🇫🇷   | Conseil constitutionnel, 20 May 2020, N° 2020-841 QPC – «La Quadrature du Net»                                                                                                                                 | **Authorities cannot have unlimited access to all documents and connection data**. Provisions granting broad access to all documents and connection data, beyond what is strictly necessary (e.g., identity and contact details for copyright infringement), are unconstitutional.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
|  🇫🇷   | Conseil d’Etat, 18 May 2020, N° 440442 and 440445 – Drohneneinsatz zur Personenidentifikation                                                                                                                  | The **use of drones for identifying individuals is inadmissible** if they are equipped with optical zoom and lack technical safeguards to prevent identification of filmed persons. Such processing of personal data does not comply with data protection law and must cease until proper safeguards are in place or a new decree is issued.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|  🇫🇷   | Conseil d’Etat, 19 June 2020, N° 430810 – Geldbusse CNIL gegen Google                                                                                                                                          | A **valid consent requires clear and transparent information and a genuine choice**. Google was fined for insufficient clarity and transparency in obtaining consent for personalized advertising from Android users, as information was too general, "diluted," and consent was sought via pre-ticked checkboxes. #consent #transparency                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
|  🇫🇷   | Conseil d’Etat, 19 June 2020, N° 434684 – Cookie-Walls                                                                                                                                                         | **General and absolute prohibitions of "cookie walls" through data protection authority guidelines (soft law) are inadmissible**. While other aspects of cookie consent (e.g., active opt-in) remain legitimate, the CNIL cannot issue broad, absolute bans under the guise of soft law. #cookies                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|  🇫🇷   | Conseil d’Etat, 19 June 2020, N° 440916 – COVID 19, Gesundheitsdaten                                                                                                                                           | **Requirements for a health emergency management platform** include project urgency, absence of timely technical alternatives, and CNIL authorization. The processing of pseudonymized health data for public interest projects related to the COVID-19 pandemic can be justified. However, data security (e.g., hosting in Europe) and compliance with GDPR for international transfers (e.g., to the US) must be ensured. Information provided to data subjects must be complete and clear.                                                                                                                      |
|  🇫🇷   | Conseil d’Etat, 26 June 2020, N° 441065 – Verwendung von Wärmebildkameras zur Bekämpfung von COVID-19                                                                                                          | The **use of thermal cameras for temperature measurement near schools to combat COVID-19 was inadmissible**. This constitutes a privacy violation for students and staff because their participation is compulsory. Consent obtained was not considered free or informed, failing to meet GDPR requirements for voluntary, specific, and informed consent.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|  🇫🇷   | Conseil d’Etat, 13 October 2020, N° 444937 – Folgen von Schrems II auf Health Data Hub                                                                                                                         | The **national health data platform (Health Data Hub) was not prohibited despite "Schrems II" ruling**, but **special precautions under CNIL supervision are required**. While a risk of US authorities accessing data hosted by Microsoft was acknowledged, the important public interest in using health data for pandemic containment justified its temporary continuation, provided heightened security measures are taken to mitigate access risks until a sustainable solution is found.                                                                                                                     |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 14 February 2019, Nr. C-345/17 (Buivids) – Grenze des Haushaltsprivilegs                                                                                                      | The **household privilege does not apply when videos are uploaded and unrestricted on social media platforms like YouTube**. Making recordings accessible to an indefinite number of people online goes beyond purely personal or family activities, making GDPR provisions fully applicable.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 12 January 2023, Nr. C-154/21 (Österreichische Post) – Auskunftsrecht über die Empfänger personenbezogener Daten                                                              | A data subject has the **right to receive information about the specific recipients of their data** to exercise their rights under Art. 16, 17, 18 GDPR. The controller can only limit this to categories of recipients if specific identification is not yet possible or if the request is manifestly unfounded or excessive. #rights                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 4 May 2023, Nr. C-487/21 (Österreichische Datenschutzbehörde und CRIF) – Auskunftsrecht, Recht auf Kopie                                                                      | The **right to a copy extends to the faithful and understandable reproduction of personal data**, including excerpts or full documents/databases, if essential for the data subject to effectively exercise their GDPR rights, while respecting others' rights and freedoms. #personal_data #rights                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 4 May 2023, Nr. C-300/21 (Österreichische Post) – Recht auf Schadenersatz                                                                                                     | A **claim for compensation requires that damage has occurred causally linked to a GDPR violation**. There is **no materiality threshold** for non-material damages. The terms of material and non-material damage must be interpreted uniformly across member states. Member states determine compensation modalities, adhering to EU principles of equivalence and effectiveness. #damages                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 4 July 2023, Nr. C-252/21 (Meta v. Bundeskartellamt)                                                                                                                          | **Lawful consent requires a genuine choice and conscious decision, not bypassed by general terms and conditions**. A dominant market position can impair the voluntariness of consent. **Necessity of legal bases (Art. 6 Abs. 1 Bst. b-f GDPR) must be narrowly interpreted**. Sensitive data is only "manifestly made public" if there's explicit intent to make it publicly available. There is a **principle of loyal cooperation between competition and data protection authorities**. #consent                                                                                                              |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 22 June 2023, Nr. C-579/21 (Pankki S) – Umfang Auskunftsrecht                                                                                                                 | The **GDPR right to information applies also to past data processing operations**. Generally, it **does not include information about the specific employees** of a controller who processed the data, unless this information is essential for the data subject to effectively exercise their GDPR rights, and provided the rights and freedoms of these employees are respected.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 26 October 2023, Nr. C-307/22 (FT) – Kopie der Patientenakte                                                                                                                  | The **first copy of a patient's medical record must be provided free of charge and without requiring justification**. The patient has the right to a complete copy of documents containing personal data, if necessary for understanding them, including diagnoses and treatment information.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 5 December 2023, Nr. C-683/21 (Nacionalinis visuomenės sveikatos centras) and Nr. C-807/21 (Deutsche Wohnen)                                                                  | **GDPR fines may only be imposed for culpable violations (intentional or negligent)**. Legal entities are liable for violations by any person acting on their behalf in the course of business, even if not specifically identified. **Joint responsibility requires factual participation** in determining processing purposes and means, not a formal agreement. Fines for companies are based on a percentage of their total global annual turnover. #enforcement_pattern                                                                                                                                       |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 14 March 2024, Nr. C-46/23 (Budapest Főváros v. Nemzeti) – Befugnisse der Aufsichtsbehörde                                                                                    | To effectively enforce the GDPR, **data protection supervisory authorities may initiate investigations and impose sanctions _ex officio_**. They can order the deletion of unlawfully processed personal data even if the data subject has not made a specific request, encompassing data collected directly from the data subject or from other sources.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 11 July 2024, Nr. C-757/22 (Meta v. Verbraucherzentrale) – Datenschutzhinweise; Verbandsklagebefugnis                                                                         | **Untimely, incorrect, or absent data protection notices result in unlawful data processing**, against which **collective action is generally permissible**. A violation of the information obligations (Art. 13 and 14 GDPR) is a rights-infringing data processing, enabling the use of the collective action mechanism provided in Art. 80 Abs. 2 GDPR.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 26 September 2024, Nr. C-768/21 (TR v. Land Hessen) – Entscheidungsspielraum der Aufsichtsbehörde                                                                             | A data subject **does not have a right to a specific sanction** by the data protection supervisory authority against the controller. The supervisory authority is not obligated to take a remedial measure or impose a fine in every case if such intervention is not appropriate, necessary, or proportionate to remedy the deficiency and ensure full compliance.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 12 September 2024, Nr. C-17/22 (HTB v. Müller) and C-18/22 (Ökorenta v. WealthCap)                                                                                            | **Reliance on legitimate interest (Art. 6 Abs. 1 Bst. f GDPR) requires absolute necessity of data processing and a balancing of interests**. The processing must be absolutely necessary for the legitimate interest, and the data subject's interests or fundamental rights must not override it. **Certain jurisprudence can constitute a legal obligation** (Art. 6 Abs. 1 Bst. c GDPR) if clear, precise, foreseeable, and serving a public interest goal proportionally. #legitimate_interest                                                                                                                 |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 4 October 2024, Nr. C-621/22 (Koninklijke v. niederländische Datenschutzbehörde)                                                                                              | The **legitimate interest (Art. 6 Abs. 1 Bst. f GDPR) requires that the interest cannot be achieved by a less intrusive means** than the planned data processing. The controller must always assess whether legitimate interests can be achieved through milder, less intrusive means. #legitimate_interest                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 4 October 2024, Nr. C-21/23 (ND v. DR) – Gesundheitsdaten bei Online-Arzneimittelbestellungen; GDPR und Wettbewerbsrecht                                                      | **All entered order data for online pharmaceutical purchases constitute health data** under Art. 9 Abs. 1 GDPR, even for non-prescription drugs. Such processing requires explicit consent. The **GDPR does not preclude competition law enforcement of data protection violations**; competitors can sue for unfair business practices stemming from GDPR breaches, strengthening data subject rights. #sensitive #personal_data                                                                                                                                                                                  |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 4 October 2024, Nr. C-446/21 (Schrems v. Meta) – Grundsätze der Datenverarbeitung; Verwendung von durch die betroffene Person selbst veröffentlichten personenbezogenen Daten | Even with general consent, **data processing for personalized advertising is not limitless**; it must adhere to **principles of purpose limitation, data minimization, and storage limitation** (Art. 5 GDPR) #consent #minimization #retention . An "obviously made public" statement (Art. 9 Abs. 2 Bst. e GDPR) does not equate to explicit consent for other processing purposes, nor does it override the general prohibition on processing special categories of personal data, regardless of accuracy or intent to collect.                                                                                 |
|  🇪🇺   | Europäischer Gerichtshof (EuGH), 4 October 2024, Nr. C-507/23 (A v. Patērētāju) – Recht auf Schadenersatz                                                                                                      | A **GDPR violation alone is not sufficient to constitute "damage"** under Art. 82 Abs. 1 GDPR.
