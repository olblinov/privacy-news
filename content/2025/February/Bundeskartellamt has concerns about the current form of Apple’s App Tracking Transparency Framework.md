The Bundeskartellamt has raised concerns regarding the current form of Apple's App Tracking Transparency Framework (ATTF). The Bundeskartellamt is a German competition authority responsible for tasks such as cartel prosecution, merger control, abuse supervision, and consumer protection, especially concerning the digital economy.

# **Bundeskartellamt's Concerns Regarding Apple's App Tracking Transparency Framework (ATTF)**

## **Preliminary Legal Assessment of Apple's ATTF**

On February 13, 2025, the Bundeskartellamt sent its preliminary legal assessment of Apple's ATTF to Apple Inc. and Apple GmbH. Since its implementation in April 2021, the ATTF requires providers of apps in the iOS App Store to obtain additional user consent before accessing certain data for advertising. However, the Bundeskartellamt's preliminary view is that these strict ATTF requirements **only apply to third-party app providers and not to Apple itself**. This differential treatment may be prohibited under **Section 19a(2) of the German Competition Act (GWB)**, which contains special abuse control provisions for large digital companies, and under the general abuse control provisions of **Article 102 TFEU**. Apple has been given the opportunity to comment on these allegations.

## **Statements from Andreas Mundt, President of the Bundeskartellamt**

Andreas Mundt, President of the Bundeskartellamt, highlighted that Apple operates a comprehensive digital ecosystem, including numerous services, connected devices, the App Store, and Apple ID, which provides Apple with **extensive access to user data relevant for advertising**. Apple utilizes some of this data to offer advertising space for personalized advertising in its App Store, generating significant revenue.

Mundt noted that personalized advertising is also crucial for other companies that offer free apps, some of which compete with Apple's own services. These companies, unlike Apple, often do not possess a wealth of broad and deep user data. However, the ATTF significantly **hampers these competing app publishers from accessing user data** relevant for advertising. The Bundeskartellamt's core concern is ensuring that users can make a **free and informed decision** about whether their data can be used for personalized advertising. The key question is whether Apple is permitted to apply **stricter criteria to other providers than to itself** when requesting such user consent, as this may constitute **unequal treatment and self-preferencing**, which are prohibited under competition law.

## **Context: Apple's Paramount Significance Decision**

The Bundeskartellamt's current proceeding against Apple regarding the ATTF is based on its prior decision from April 3, 2023. In that decision, the Bundeskartellamt found that Apple is of **paramount significance for competition across markets** within the meaning of Section 19a(1) GWB. This classification subjects Apple to **extended abuse control**. Apple has lodged an appeal against this decision with the Federal Court of Justice, and the court's decision is anticipated on March 18, 2025.

## **Detailed Explanation of the ATTF and its Impact**

The ATTF was implemented by Apple in April 2021 with the iOS 14.5, iPadOS 14.5, and tvOS 14.5 updates. It establishes specific preconditions for **user tracking by third-party apps across different services and companies** (known as third-party tracking). This tracking is vital for advertisers or app publishers who wish to display personalized advertisements or use user data for other purposes, especially for free apps financed through advertising.

Under existing data protection rules, app publishers must obtain user consent to access data. The ATTF adds another layer for third-party companies, requiring user consent for the use and combination of their data across companies. This consent is requested through a pop-up dialogue when a non-Apple app is first started. Access to the **Identifier for Advertisers (IDFA)**, which is crucial for the advertising industry, is also conditional on this consent.

Crucially, these strict ATTF rules **do not apply to Apple** when it uses and combines user data across services within its own ecosystem (first-party tracking). While users can restrict Apple from using their data for personalized advertising, Apple is not subject to the additional ATTF rules and its associated consent dialogues. The Bundeskartellamt highlights that the consent dialogues for Apple's own apps and for third-party apps **differ substantially in design and wording**, making it more likely that users will consent to Apple's data processing compared to third-party apps.

## **Three Core Competition Concerns Raised by Bundeskartellamt**

The Bundeskartellamt's preliminary view identifies three key aspects of the ATTF that raise competition concerns, potentially favoring Apple's own apps and impeding other market participants:

1. **Definition of "tracking":** The ATTF defines "tracking" in a way that **only covers data processing for advertising purposes across companies** by third parties. This definition, based on current findings, **does not cover Apple's own practice** of combining user data across its ecosystem (App Store, Apple ID, connected devices) and using it for advertising.
2. **Number of consent dialogues:** Third-party apps may present users with **up to four consecutive consent dialogues** under the ATTF. In contrast, Apple's own apps show a maximum of two dialogues, and these do not explicitly refer to Apple's own first-party data processing across services.
3. **Design of consent dialogues:** The Bundeskartellamt believes that Apple has designed its consent dialogues to **encourage users to allow Apple to process their data**. Conversely, the consent dialogues for third-party apps are designed to **steer users towards refusing third-party data processing**.

## **Affected Parties and International Cooperation**

Companies impacted by the ATTF include app publishers, content providers (such as media publishers) offering their own apps, as well as advertisers and technical service providers in the advertising industry. The Bundeskartellamt is conducting this proceeding in close cooperation with the **European Commission and other national competition agencies** that are also examining the ATTF in their own national competition proceedings.
