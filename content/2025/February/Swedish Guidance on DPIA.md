The Integritetsskyddsmyndigheten (IMY), Sweden's data protection authority, provides extensive guidance and support for organizations to comply with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). This includes guidance on camera surveillance, AI, and credit reporting, as well as general data protection matters. IMY's mission is to protect individuals' personal data, ensuring it is handled correctly and securely. They achieve this by reviewing compliance, educating, and guiding entities that process personal data, promoting a sustainable and privacy-friendly digitalization.

IMY offers detailed guidance on Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs), referred to as "konsekvensbedömning". This guidance is specifically for organizations that process personal data under GDPR and need support in conducting these assessments. The goal is to simplify the process and reduce uncertainty regarding implementation and understanding of the regulations.

# What is a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)?

A DPIA is an **ongoing and documented process** that helps data controllers comply with GDPR, especially for high-risk processing activities. It provides a deeper understanding of the planned data processing, the risks it poses to individuals' rights and freedoms, and the necessary security and protective measures to mitigate those risks.

The GDPR mandates that data controllers use a **risk-based approach**, adapting data protection measures to the risk level of the processing. High-risk processing, which might involve sensitive personal data, new technologies, or extensive surveillance, necessitates more extensive security measures, active data protection efforts, and closer follow-up, including conducting DPIAs.

A well-executed and documented DPIA serves as a **practical tool** for data controllers to assess risks and ensure compliance with GDPR. It helps gain a comprehensive overview of the data processing and its consequences, and minimize the processing to avoid unnecessary scope relative to the purpose. It also supports the requirement for **data protection by design** and helps determine if **prior consultation with IMY** is necessary. A properly conducted and documented DPIA is a crucial component in fulfilling the **accountability principle** of GDPR.

A DPIA must, at a minimum, include:

- A **systematic description** of the planned personal data processing and its purposes.
- An **assessment of the necessity and proportionality** of the processing in relation to its purposes.
- An **assessment of the risks** to individuals' rights and freedoms.
- The **measures planned to manage risks** and demonstrate GDPR compliance.

Additionally, a data controller undertaking a DPIA should:

- **Consult with the data protection officer (DPO)**, if one has been appointed.
- **Verify compatibility** with any approved codes of conduct within the relevant sector.
- **Obtain feedback from data subjects** or their representatives, when appropriate.
- **Conduct a review** as needed, to assess if the processing is being carried out in accordance with the DPIA.

IMY's practical guide is based on criteria for acceptable DPIAs from the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) guidelines, which elaborate on the minimum requirements of GDPR Article 35. The scope and detail of DPIAs will vary depending on the complexity of the processing and the organization's size. Data controllers must **document their assessments and decisions** within the DPIA to demonstrate GDPR compliance, though the extent of documentation is flexible.

# IMY's Guidance and Supporting Tools

IMY's guidance includes:

- **"En praktisk guide" (A Practical Guide):** This guide proposes a **ten-step process** for conducting an acceptable DPIA. It is designed primarily for those with little to no prior knowledge of DPIAs and should be read in its entirety before starting the assessment.
- **"Rättsligt tolkningsstöd" (Legal Interpretation Support):** This annex delves into the legal framework and interpretation support, intended for those seeking deeper legal understanding.
- **Templates to support the work:**
    - **"IMY:s mall för Bedömning av behovet av konsekvensbedömning enligt dataskyddsförordningen" (IMY's template for Assessing the Need for a DPIA):** Corresponds to **Step 1** of the practical guide. It helps document the assessment of whether a DPIA is mandatory.
    - **"IMY:s mall för Konsekvensbedömning enligt dataskyddsförordningen" (IMY's template for DPIA according to the GDPR):** Corresponds to **Steps 3–10** of the practical guide. It assists in documenting the DPIA itself.
    - **"Excelblad Riskhantering vid konsekvensbedömning" (Excel sheet Risk Management for DPIA):** Used to document risk management in **Step 5**.

Organizations can provide feedback on the guidance via email.

# IMY's Proposed 10-Step Process for a DPIA

IMY's "Practical Guide" outlines a documented process in ten steps:

## Step 1. Bedöm behovet av att genomföra en konsekvensbedömning (Assess the need to conduct a DPIA)

Before any processing begins, or if a processing changes in a way that alters its risks, the data controller must determine if a DPIA is required. It's crucial that this assessment considers the **type of processing**, and not measures that might reduce or eliminate risks within a specific planned processing. The DPO should always be consulted on this need.

The assessment of whether a DPIA is mandatory should be based on:

- **Factors in Article 35.1 of GDPR**: These include the **use of new technology**, the **nature, scope, context, and purposes of the processing**.
- **Examples in Article 35.3 of GDPR**: These include:
    - **Systematic and extensive evaluation** of personal aspects based on automated processing, including profiling, leading to legal or similarly significant effects for individuals.
    - **Large-scale processing of special categories of data** (Article 9.1) or data relating to criminal convictions and offenses (Article 10).
    - **Systematic monitoring of a public area on a large scale**.
- **IMY's list according to Article 35.4 of GDPR**: This list complements the examples in Article 35.3 and is based on EDPB criteria. As a general rule, a DPIA is required if **at least two of the nine criteria** in IMY's list are met. However, a DPIA might still be needed even if only one criterion is met in an individual case. The criteria include:
    1. Processing involves **evaluation or scoring of individuals** (e.g., genetic tests for disease risk, credit reporting, profiling internet users).
    2. Processing personal data to make **automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects** for the data subject.
    3. Processing involves **systematic monitoring of individuals** (e.g., camera surveillance of public places, collecting internet usage data in public spaces).
    4. Processing **sensitive personal data** (Article 9) or data of a very personal nature (e.g., patient records, location data, financial data).
    5. Processing personal data on a **large scale**.
    6. **Combining personal data from two or more processing operations** in a way that deviates from what data subjects could reasonably expect (e.g., merging registers).
    7. Processing personal data of **vulnerable individuals** (e.g., children, employees, asylum seekers, elderly, patients).
    8. Using **new technology or new organizational solutions** (e.g., IoT applications).
    9. Processing personal data to **prevent data subjects from accessing a service or entering into a contract** (e.g., bank checking credit databases for loan decisions).

A DPIA is **not required** when:

- The processing type is **unlikely to pose a high risk** to individuals' rights and freedoms.
- The processing is **very similar to a previously assessed processing** for which a DPIA was already conducted, provided they are alike in nature, scope, context, purpose, and risks.
- An **overall DPIA has been performed as part of legislative work** (Article 35.10 GDPR), though often the data controller still needs to supplement this with their own assessment of practical, technical, and organizational conditions.

## Step 2. Sätt ihop en arbetsgrupp och planera arbetet (Form a working group and plan the work)

After determining the need for a DPIA, the next step is to plan its execution and allocate resources. A working group should be assembled, ideally consisting of individuals with knowledge of the information to be processed, technical implementation, information security and risk management, and data protection legislation. For smaller organizations, one person might fill multiple roles, but involving several people with diverse expertise is generally preferred to ensure different perspectives are considered.

A person in the working group should be designated as responsible for coordinating and driving the process. While DPOs often possess the most knowledge, it's important to remember that they are **not responsible for performing the DPIA itself**. The data controller organization should identify who is authorized to decide on or adopt the DPIA. A timeline should also be established, including time for anchoring the DPIA with management and the DPO, and for gathering data subjects' feedback when appropriate.

## Step 3. Gör en systematisk beskrivning av personuppgiftsbehandlingen (Make a systematic description of the personal data processing)

This step requires a thorough and methodical description of the data processing and its purposes. The description should be clear and comprehensive to provide a complete overview for readers such as data subjects, management, DPOs, and supervisory authorities. It forms the basis for the rest of the DPIA.

The systematic description should include:

- **Nature of the processing (Behandlingens art):**
    - **General description and background:** Define and delimit the object of the DPIA (e.g., product, service, software, process), provide background, and explain the reason for the planned processing. For complex systems, it might be appropriate to divide the description into several parts. Documentation from needs analyses or risk analyses can be useful.
    - **Categories of personal data:** List the types of personal data to be processed (e.g., age, gender, education, salary, images, voice recordings). This also includes technical data (e.g., IP addresses), tracking data (e.g., cookies), or vehicle registration numbers. Note if any are **special categories of data** (Article 9, e.g., health data, biometric data, genetic data) or **data relating to criminal convictions and offenses** (Article 10). Also, identify **particularly sensitive personal data** like data on children or social security numbers.
    - **Categories of data subjects:** List the groups of individuals whose data will be processed (e.g., employees, customers). Indicate if they are in a position of **dependence** (e.g., employees, patients, students) or if they include **children or other vulnerable persons** (e.g., elderly, disabled individuals).
- **Scope of the processing (Behandlingens omfattning):** Estimate the number of individuals affected, the total amount of personal data, and the variety of data categories. Also, describe the **geographical scope**, including any transfers outside the EU/EEA (third countries).
- **Context of the processing (Behandlingens sammanhang):** Describe the processing from a broader perspective, considering internal and external factors, such as previous experiences, if the processing is innovative, if it might be questioned, the degree of control individuals have over their data, and relevant codes of conduct.
- **Purpose of the processing (Behandlingens ändamål):** Clearly define the reason for the processing and its benefits (for the organization, public, or third parties), as well as the intended outcome for individuals. The purpose must be specified before processing begins and cannot be changed later.
- **Necessary resources (Nödvändiga resurser):** Identify the resources and information assets required for the processing (e.g., software, servers, hardware, networks, cloud services).
- **Functional description of the processing (Funktionell beskrivning av behandlingen):** Detail how the processing will occur, including data collection methods (e.g., surveys, web forms, interviews, public databases), data registration and transfer, systems used, storage duration, and deletion or anonymization routines. Justify the storage period.
- **Roles and responsibilities (Roller och ansvarsfördelning):**
    - **Data controller:** Specify the data controller(s) and whether the processing involves joint controllership. Documentation of responsibility division is crucial.
    - **Recipients including data processors:** Indicate if personal data will be transferred to external recipients, and if data processors are involved, specifying their identity, services, and processing country. List any data processing agreements.

## Step 4. Genomför en rättslig analys (Conduct a legal analysis)

This step involves assessing if the legal conditions for carrying out the processing are met, indicating its necessity and proportionality. If the processing is not compliant with data protection laws, it is illegal and cannot proceed.

The legal analysis should cover:

- **Applicable regulations (Gällande regelverk):** Compile all relevant regulations, including GDPR, national data protection laws, marketing laws, sector-specific legislation, approved codes of conduct, certifications, and industry practices.
- **Data protection principles and legal basis (Dataskyddsprinciperna och den rättsliga grunden för behandlingen):** The DPIA must describe how the planned processing adheres to the fundamental data protection principles:
    - **Legality, correctness, and transparency (laglighet, korrekthet och öppenhet):**
        - **Legality:** The processing must comply with GDPR and other laws, and have a valid **legal basis** (Article 6.1 a-f). The six legal bases are consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task/authority, and legitimate interests. If consent is the basis, it must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous, and individuals must be able to withdraw it easily. If legitimate interest is the basis, a balancing test must be documented. Specific rules apply to processing special categories of data (Article 9), criminal data (Article 10), and personal/coordination numbers.
        - **Correctness:** Processing must be fair, reasonable, and proportionate, non-discriminatory, and understandable for data subjects.
        - **Transparency:** Data subjects must clearly understand how and why their data is processed, including their rights. Information should be easily accessible and clearly formulated, especially for children.
    - **Purpose limitation (ändamålsbegränsning):** Data must be collected for specified, explicit, and legitimate purposes and not further processed in a manner incompatible with those purposes.
    - **Data minimization (uppgiftsminimering):** Data processed must be adequate, relevant, and not excessive for the purposes. Alternative methods that minimize intrusion should be considered.
    - **Accuracy (riktighet):** Personal data must be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date. Routines for correcting or deleting inaccurate data are essential.
    - **Storage limitation (lagringsminimering):** Data should not be kept longer than necessary for its purposes. Storage periods must be justified and routines for data purging should be in place.
    - **Integrity and confidentiality (integritet och konfidentialitet):** Appropriate technical and organizational measures must be implemented to ensure data integrity (unchanged, not lost, destroyed, or edited) and confidentiality (accessible only to authorized persons).
- **Data subjects' rights (Registrerades rättigheter):** Ensure routines are in place to uphold data subjects' rights as per GDPR Chapter III, including rights to information, access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability, and not to be subject to solely automated decision-making.
- **Safeguards for international transfers (Skyddsåtgärder för internationella överföringar):** If data will be transferred outside the EU/EEA, document the safeguards ensuring an adequate level of protection (e.g., binding corporate rules, standard contractual clauses, adequacy decisions).
- **Overall assessment (Sammantagen bedömning):** Make a combined assessment of whether the legal conditions for the processing are met, including its necessity and proportionality. This assessment should be revisited after risk management and stakeholder feedback.

## Step 5. Hantera risker: identifiera, analysera och åtgärda risker (Manage risks: identify, analyze, and mitigate risks)

This step involves identifying, analyzing, and mitigating risks to individuals' rights and freedoms. The DPO should be consulted during this process.

The risk management process includes:

1. **Identify risks:** Focus on risks to individuals' **fundamental rights and freedoms**, primarily their right to data protection, privacy, and integrity, but also other rights like freedom of expression or non-discrimination. Risks can stem from internal (e.g., misuse of data, lacking security) or external (e.g., cyberattacks) threats, and can be accidental or intentional. Beyond direct harm, consider negative effects like discrimination, identity theft, financial loss, or reputational damage.
2. **Analyze risks:** Assess the **likelihood** of an unwanted event occurring and its **severity** if it does. This assessment must be objective and based on the nature, scope, context, and purpose of the processing. Factors influencing likelihood include data type and volume, incident history, and effectiveness of security measures. Factors influencing severity include consequences for rights, data type (e.g., sensitive data, children's data), scope of impact, and perceived predictability of the processing. IMY's template uses a four-level scale for likelihood and severity, described in text, rather than a combined numerical risk matrix. #methodology 
3. **Mitigate risks (åtgärda riskerna):** Identify and implement appropriate **technical and organizational security measures** to reduce the identified risks and ensure an adequate security level. These measures should be tailored and proportionate to the specific risk. Examples include:
    - **Technical measures:** Authentication functions, logging, technical access limitation, encryption, user-friendly interfaces, automatic data removal, anonymization, pseudonymization, privacy-enhancing technologies, incident detection/handling tools.
    - **Organizational measures:** Decisions on data collection limits, tailored templates for data minimization, routines for purging and storage, access rights management, clear work distribution, continuous staff training, qualification requirements for data-handling staff, confidentiality agreements, compliance checks, updated data processing agreements, clear information to data subjects. Risk management can be integrated with existing organizational risk management processes. Legal disclaimer is not a valid risk mitigation measure.
4. **Follow up and re-assess risks:** After implementing mitigation measures, the risk assessment should be updated, and a new risk evaluation performed, taking the measures into account. If risks remain high even after mitigation, further actions are needed. #change #methodology  If this is not possible, options are to abandon the processing or request prior consultation with IMY.

## Step 6. Begär förhandssamråd med IMY om risken förblir hög (Request prior consultation with IMY if the risk remains high)

If the risks remain high after considering mitigation measures, the data controller is obliged to request **prior consultation with IMY** before commencing the processing. The DPIA itself must be included as part of the documentation submitted for prior consultation. IMY may request supplements or reject the request if the DPIA is missing or incomplete.

## Step 7. Hämta in synpunkter från berörda (Obtain feedback from stakeholders)

This step involves gathering feedback from relevant parties.

- **DPO recommendations (Dataskyddsombudets rekommendationer):** The DPO must be consulted throughout the DPIA process, particularly before deciding whether to conduct a DPIA and during risk management. Their recommendations and opinions should be documented, and a separate written statement from the DPO is generally recommended. If the data controller decides not to follow a formal DPO recommendation, a detailed justification must be documented.
- **Feedback from data subjects (Synpunkter från de registrerade):** Data controllers are required to obtain feedback from data subjects "when appropriate" (Article 35.9). This is appropriate when processing affects a large number of data subjects, involves sensitive data, or automated decision-making. The feedback helps inform the risk assessment and other parts of the DPIA. Data subjects' feedback is **not binding**, but the risk assessment must still be carried out according to GDPR requirements. Consent to processing does not substitute for obtaining feedback. Feedback might not be necessary if it would compromise commercial or public interests, processing security, or if it's impractical or disproportionate.
    - **Who to consult:** Depends on who is affected by the processing. This can include representatives of data subjects' interests (e.g., consumer organizations, guardians for children, union representatives for employees) or staff close to data subjects.
    - **How to collect feedback:** Methods vary, but surveys are an option. Questions should be clear and concise. Data controllers should describe the identified risk factors to enable informed responses.
    - **Documentation:** Compile the results of the feedback. If the data controller proceeds against data subjects' views, a justification must be documented. Justification is also needed if no feedback is collected.
- **Feedback from other stakeholders (Synpunkter från övriga intressenter):** Opinions from other stakeholders, such as information security managers or technical experts, may also be relevant and should be documented.

## Step 8. Gör en sammantagen bedömning (Make an overall assessment)

At this stage, after risk management and stakeholder feedback, an overall assessment should be made to determine if the planned processing can be carried out. The data controller must decide if the identified risks are sufficiently managed, and if the planned processing remains necessary and proportionate to its purposes.

## Step 9. Förankra bedömningen i organisationen (Anchor the assessment within the organization)

Authorized persons in the data controller's management should be informed about the DPIA, identified risks, risk assessment, and planned mitigation measures. Organizations should have a process for this anchoring, which might involve summaries or presentations to management. It's crucial to clarify who is responsible for implementing risk-reducing measures and for any remaining risks of the processing.

## Step 10. Följ upp konsekvensbedömningen kontinuerligt (Continuously review the DPIA)

A DPIA is an **ongoing process**, not a one-time event. Data controllers need to periodically check if the processing aligns with the DPIA and whether risks have changed. This review can be integrated into regular operational processes.

- The frequency of review depends on the risks and technology involved.
- The first follow-up should occur relatively soon after processing begins, ideally after about one year.
- Subsequently, a review should typically happen every two years.
- Reviews should be more frequent for new technologies, extensive processing, or sensitive data.
- Reviews may be less frequent for well-established processes.
- Crucially, a review must be conducted whenever the **risk profile changes**, regardless of the regular schedule. Risks can change due to internal factors (e.g., organizational changes) or external factors (e.g., new laws, security breaches, new AI models).

IMY also works to ensure that individuals' personal data has the same protection across the entire EU, collaborating with other data protection authorities.
