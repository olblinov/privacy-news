# Summary

Persónuvernd, the Icelandic Supervisory Authority, imposed an administrative fine of **5,000,000 ISK (approximately 33,854 EUR)** on the Primary Health Care of the Capital Area for unlawfully processing personal data through the integration of medical record systems. The investigation found that the Primary Health Care failed to comply with national legal requirements, specifically Article 20.2 of Act No. 55/2009 on Medical Records, which mandates a permit from the Minister of Health and confirmation from Persónuvernd regarding data security for integrated medical record systems. While one agreement met these conditions, eleven other agreements for integrating various parties into their medical records system did not, leading to a violation of GDPR principles regarding the lawfulness of processing and sensitive data handling.

# Details

## Background

The Icelandic Supervisory Authority (Persónuvernd) initiated an investigation into the processing of personal data within the shared medical record system of the Primary Health Care of the Capital Area on September 26, 2024. This proactive investigation was prompted by a previous ruling (case No. 2023071182) concerning a complaint about the processing of medical records by the Icelandic Transport Authority's medical officer. That ruling revealed that an agreement between the Primary Health Care and the Transport Authority regarding a shared medical record system did not meet the requirements of Act No. 55/2009 on Medical Records, particularly concerning the need for a ministerial permit and Persónuvernd's confirmation of data security. The current investigation was specifically limited to examining the lawfulness of the data processing.

The Primary Health Care of the Capital Area had entered into agreements with **twelve different entities** for a shared medical record system or access to its system. These agreements included:

- Heilsugæslan Lágmúla (now Kirkjusandi) (April 15, 2016).
- Heimaþjónustu Reykjavíkur (April 2016), later replaced by Velferðarsvið Reykjavíkurborgar (October 17, 2023).
- Heilsugæslan Höfða (May 18, 2017).
- Heilsugæslan Salahverfi (January 10, 2018).
- Heilsugæslan Urðarhvarfi (August 29, 2018).
- Knattspyrnusamband Íslands (Icelandic Football Association) (December 17, 2018).
- Fluglæknasetrið (Aviation Medical Center) (August 1, 2020).
- Samgöngustofu (Icelandic Transport Authority) (September 1, 2020).
- Janus endurhæfingu ehf. (April 13, 2023), agreement expired on December 11, 2023.
- Heilsugæslan Höfða Suðurnesjum (October 6, 2023).
- Velferðarsvið Reykjavíkurborgar (October 17, 2023).
- Vinnumálastofnun (Directorate of Labor) (January 30, 2023), access granted without a written agreement.

The Primary Health Care stated that their system is a single, unified medical record system, not twelve independent systems, despite the multiple agreements. It contains records for approximately **517,429 individuals** (living and deceased, domestic and foreign), with an estimated **450,000 actual medical records**. Around **195,000 individuals** have had their records accessed by one of these third parties. The Primary Health Care emphasized that **only qualified healthcare professionals** working for or on behalf of the contracting parties were granted access.

Following Persónuvernd's prior ruling, the Primary Health Care closed access for the Icelandic Football Association, Aviation Medical Center, Icelandic Transport Authority, and Directorate of Labor on September 24 and October 4, 2024, recognizing that these entities might not meet the conditions of Article 20 of Act No. 55/2009. The Primary Health Care also applied to the Ministry of Health on October 3, 2024, for permission to continue integrating with Heilsugæslan Höfða, Heilsugæslan Salahverfi, Heilsugæslan Urðarhvarfi, Heilsugæslan Höfða Suðurnesjum, and Velferðarsvið Reykjavíkurborgar.

## Legal Analysis

The case concerned the processing of personal data in a shared medical record system, falling under the scope of Act No. 90/2018 on Data Protection and the Processing of Personal Data, and thus within the authority of Persónuvernd. The Primary Health Care of the Capital Area was identified as the data controller for this processing.

All processing of personal data must have a **lawful basis**. Sensitive personal data, such as health information, requires additional conditions for processing. The processing needs to be **necessary for tasks performed in the public interest or in the exercise of official authority**, as per Article 9 (5) of Act No. 90/2018 and Article 6 (1)(e) of GDPR. For sensitive data, it must also be **necessary for reasons of public interest in the area of public health**, such as ensuring the quality and safety of healthcare services, and carried out on the basis of law that provides appropriate safeguards, as per Article 11 (1)(9) of Act No. 90/2018 and Article 9 (2)(i) of GDPR. The data controller must ensure that data is processed lawfully and be able to demonstrate compliance.

Specifically, the legal framework for shared medical record systems in Iceland is outlined in **Chapter VI of Act No. 55/2009 on Medical Records**. Article 20(1) of this Act allows two or more healthcare institutions or healthcare professionals' offices to maintain medical records in a common electronic medical record system **with the permission of the Minister**. Article 20(2) specifies conditions for such permission:

1. It must be demonstrated that the shared system **better ensures patient safety** in treatment.
2. The operation of the system must comply with applicable regulations (currently Regulation No. 550/2015).
3. There must be **confirmation from Persónuvernd** that the security of personal data in the shared system is ensured in accordance with data protection laws and regulations.

Persónuvernd found that the integration of electronic medical record systems could be based on public interest grounds (ensuring patient safety), fulfilling the general and sensitive data processing requirements.

**The primary issue** was that the **Minister's permission and Persónuvernd's confirmation were not obtained for most agreements**. While the agreement with Heilsugæslan Lágmúla (2016) did go through a permit process and received an opinion from Persónuvernd, the other agreements did not follow this formal procedure. Persónuvernd noted that even for the Lágmúla agreement, it had requested additional documentation (risk assessment, security measures, and patient rights regarding access restrictions) which was not provided before the ministerial permit was granted. However, since the permit was granted, Persónuvernd did not challenge the lawfulness of that specific integration.

For all other agreements, Persónuvernd concluded that the Primary Health Care **failed to demonstrate lawful processing** because the conditions of Article 20(1) and (2) of Act No. 55/2009 were not met. This means that for each new party joining the shared system, a new assessment must confirm that it genuinely enhances patient safety more effectively than separate systems or data interconnection (as per Article 18 of Act No. 55/2009), and Persónuvernd must re-confirm data security. The Primary Health Care's argument that a shared system prevents information gaps (e.g., in opioid prescriptions) and is more efficient than interconnected systems was noted, but did not override the lack of required permits and confirmations.

The Primary Health Care acknowledged the deficiencies in handling these agreements and that the conditions of Article 20(2) of Act No. 55/2009 were not observed.

## Sanction

Persónuvernd imposed an **administrative fine of 5,000,000 Icelandic Krona (ISK)** on the Primary Health Care of the Capital Area. This amount is equivalent to **33,854 EUR**. The fine was levied because the Primary Health Care violated Articles 5, 6, and 9 of GDPR, which fall under Article 46(3) of Act No. 90/2018, allowing for fines up to 2.4 billion ISK or 4% of global annual turnover. The fine must be paid to the State Treasury within one month of the decision date.

In determining the fine, Persónuvernd considered several **mitigating factors** in favor of the Primary Health Care:

- **No apparent damage** occurred due to the violations.
- The Primary Health Care **cooperated readily** with Persónuvernd's inquiries.
- The Primary Health Care **admitted to the violations**.
- The Primary Health Care **applied for the necessary permits** for the remaining integrations and **closed access** for parties not deemed eligible for such access.

However, several **aggravating factors** led to the imposition and amount of the fine:

- The violations of GDPR were considered **serious**.
- The violations affected **many thousands of individuals** (around 195,000 individuals had their records accessed, with 450,000 records in the system), indicating **extensive processing**.
- The violations were **long-standing**, as the unlawful processing had been ongoing for several years.
- The agreements themselves explicitly stated the requirement for ministerial permission and Persónuvernd's confirmation according to Article 20 of Act No. 55/2009, yet these were not obtained for most cases, suggesting a **minimum level of intent (negligence)**.
- The violations involved **sensitive health data**, which is subject to stricter protection requirements.

Given all factors, Persónuvernd concluded that an administrative fine was warranted and set the amount at 5,000,000 ISK. Despite the finding of unlawful processing, Persónuvernd decided not to issue further instructions for immediate remedial action at this time, considering that the Primary Health Care had already initiated the permit application process and closed inappropriate access. This decision was also influenced by the significant patient interest in maintaining the current medical record arrangement for ongoing treatment until the Minister's decision on the permit applications.
