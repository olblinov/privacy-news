# Summary

The Italian Data Protection Authority (Garante per la protezione dei dati personali) sanctioned E.ON Energia S.p.A. with a fine of **€892,738.00** for **multiple data protection violations**. The core issues included the illegitimate collection of personal data through its "ClimaSmart Climatizzatore" Facebook advertising campaign, where data was accepted via an online form **without adequate verification of its origin or the identity of the person providing it**, leading to "inadmissible recycling of personal data" and illicit telemarketing. Furthermore, E.ON was found to have **failed in its accountability obligations** by mismanaging consent recording for existing customers, exhibiting insufficient oversight of internal staff and external partners, and failing to adequately respond to data subject access requests. The Authority's decision, issued on November 27, 2024, also included a prohibition on further processing of the complainants' data and an order to update internal documentation.

# Details

## Background

The proceeding against E.ON Energia S.p.A. was initiated on **August 13, 2024**, following **two separate complaints** received by the Authority. The Authority decided to **jointly examine both complaints** to ensure a comprehensive assessment and efficiency, as they were directed at the same controller and involved similar issues, promoting economy and speed.

- **Complaint n. 352614** involved an individual who received unwanted promotional calls from E.ON. During one such call, she was informed that her personal and contact data, including an institutional email address, had been collected through a **Facebook advertising campaign** where she had supposedly filled out an online form to request contact. However, the complainant explicitly stated that she had **never activated any account on the Facebook platform**. E.ON itself acknowledged that the complainant's data was "likely entered into the form by a third party without the interested party's authorization". Additionally, this complaint highlighted E.ON's failure to respond to her request for exercising data protection rights, an omission initially attributed to a human error by a third-party supplier, which E.ON later clarified was an error by its own personnel.
    
- **Complaint n. 367993** concerned an individual who, after signing two contracts for electricity and gas supply with E.ON in November 2023, began receiving a **multitude of unwanted promotional phone calls**. The complainant linked these calls to the activation of her E.ON supplies, suspecting an illicit transfer of her personal data to third parties by the company. E.ON responded, admitting that "due to a mere material error," the **privacy consents entered into their systems during contract implementation were recorded differently** from what the complainant had authorized at the time of signing. Despite E.ON's assurance of correction, the complainant continued to receive promotional contacts. E.ON later explained that some contacts were "caring calls" aimed at confirming service satisfaction, while attributing other unwanted calls to third parties unrelated to the company, possibly due to her past public role. The complainant also expressed significant personal and psychological distress due to these calls.
    

## Legal Analysis

The Authority concluded that E.ON Energia S.p.A. had violated several key provisions of the GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) and the Italian Data Protection Code (d.lgs. 196/2003, as amended). The Authority noted that E.ON's defense indicated an **incomplete assimilation of the rationale and scope of its obligations**, providing statements that were later retracted and incomplete documentation lacking temporal references.

- **Violations of Lawfulness and Accountability (Articles 5, 6, 7, 24 GDPR; Article 130 Code)**:
    
    - **Illegitimate Data Collection via Facebook Campaign**: For digital promotional campaigns, the processing of personal data collected through online forms was deemed **unlawful if "measures suitable to verify the source and identity of the subject providing the information"** are not implemented. E.ON's acknowledgment that the complainant's data was "likely entered into the form by a third party without the interested party's authorization" highlighted this issue. #direct_marketing #consent #change 
    - **"Inadmissible Recycling of Personal Data"**: The Authority emphasized that without such verification measures, online forms could be "surreptitiously exploited to make outbound contacts under the guise of re-contact requests from the data subject". This practice "gives a semblance of legality to the processing of illicitly obtained personal data and contact lists, effectively creating an inadmissible recycling of personal data and fueling the telemarketing underworld". The "pervasive use of this type of form and social media platforms" meant a **"very numerous" number of individuals could have been affected**.
    - **Mismatched Consent Recording**: E.ON's admission of "mere material error" in transcribing consents for marketing purposes demonstrated a lack of "measures suitable to verify and ensure the correspondence between the consents given by the data subjects and the information recorded on the company systems", leading to telemarketing activities without a proper legal basis.
    - **"Customer Caring" Calls**: While E.ON described these as non-commercial "caring calls", the Authority noted that their "insistence and frequency of contacts imply a secondary commercial purpose". Even these types of contacts, regardless of the medium, must preserve the data subject's right to object and be kept within "reasonable temporal and attempt limits" to avoid an "inadmissible invasion of the client's personal sphere". In the complainant's case, four contact attempts within two weeks and nearly twenty communications (emails and SMS) over four months were deemed excessive.
- **Deficient Accountability (Articles 24, 28 GDPR; Article 2-quaterdecies Code)**:
    
    - E.ON tried to attribute problematic conduct to third parties or errors by its collaborators and commercial partners as an "exemption from all responsibility", **without demonstrating that it had adopted all legally required measures to prevent such violations**. The Authority also noted E.ON's retraction regarding the party responsible for the "human error," indicating a lack of full control over internal flows. #vendor_management 
    - **Internal Personnel (Article 2-quaterdecies Code)**: The Authority found a "failure to fulfill obligations related to the appointment, direction, and training of individuals operating within the controller's organization". E.ON's defense of "human errors" was not accepted as there was a **lack of documented proof of effective and specific training**. The provided instructions were "generic and dated" and lacked customization for specific job roles or data categories.
    - **External Processors (Article 28 GDPR - _culpa in eligendo e culpa in vigilando_)**: The investigation revealed "the omission of measures to ensure the selection of partners who offer sufficient guarantees" and a lack of "omitted control and supervision over personal data processing activities carried out by these subjects on behalf of the Company". E.ON's documentation on supplier selection and monitoring was considered "merely formal". #vendor_management #enforcement_pattern #change 
- **Failure to Respond to Data Subject Rights (Articles 12, 15-22 GDPR)**: E.ON failed to implement adequate procedures to ensure an "adequate and timely response" to data subject requests for exercising their rights. The Authority rejected E.ON's argument that a prior phone clarification was sufficient, as the formal request for specific and articulated information was made later. The issue in complaint 352614 was initially blamed on a third-party, then clarified as an internal E.ON error.
    

## Sanction

The Authority imposed the following measures on E.ON Energia S.p.A.:

- **Prohibition of Further Processing**: E.ON is **prohibited from any further processing of the personal data of the complainants**.
- **Mandatory Update of Procedures**: E.ON is ordered to **update the documentation used to authorize personnel** operating under its direct authority to process personal data, in accordance with Article 2-quaterdecies of the Code. E.ON must communicate its initiatives to comply within **thirty days**.
- **Administrative Monetary Penalty**: A fine of **€892,738.00** was imposed.
    - The maximum potential fine based on E.ON's annual turnover (December 2023) was €89,273,777.20. The applied sanction represents **0.04% of its turnover** and **1% of the maximum allowable fine**.
    - **Aggravating Factor**: The **gravity of the violations** was considered an aggravating factor, particularly in the context of telemarketing, a phenomenon for which the Authority has provided extensive guidance. The "peculiar gravity inherent in using re-contact forms to give a semblance of legality to illicitly acquired personal data," combined with the "vast audience of data subjects involved" due to pervasive social media use, was specifically highlighted.
    - **Mitigating Factor**: E.ON's **degree of cooperation with the Authority** and its efforts to improve corporate compliance during the proceeding were considered mitigating factors.
- **Publication of the Decision**: The decision is to be **published on the Garante's website**. This measure is deemed "reasonable and proportionate" given the nature of the processing, the company's conduct, and the risks posed to the rights and freedoms of data subjects, especially concerning digital campaigns.

E.ON has the right to appeal this decision to the ordinary court within **thirty days** (or sixty days if residing abroad).
