# Summary

On December 19, 2024, the French data protection authority, CNIL, imposed a **€40,000 administrative fine** on a real estate company for **disproportionate employee surveillance and related data privacy violations**. The company utilized **TIME DOCTOR software** to monitor employees' work time and productivity, which included tracking "inactivity" periods and taking regular screen captures, and also maintained a **video surveillance system** that continuously filmed and recorded audio of employees in their workspaces. #employment  The CNIL found that these practices violated several core principles of the GDPR, specifically regarding data minimization (Article 5.1.c), the lawfulness of processing (Article 6), the obligation to inform individuals (Articles 12 and 13), data security (Article 32), and the requirement to conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (Article 35). The CNIL opted to publish the decision to raise awareness about such surveillance practices but decided not to name the company, considering the limited number of affected individuals and the company's swift action to remove the TIME DOCTOR software after the inspections.

# Details

## Background

The sanctioned entity is a **simplified joint-stock company operating as a real estate agency**, primarily assisting professional buyers in identifying profitable real estate properties. In 2022, the company employed approximately [...] people, with a majority being interns.

The company implemented two main surveillance measures:

- **TIME DOCTOR software:** This software was introduced in September 2021 to monitor the activity of non-executive employees in their "marketing/communication," "hunting," "rental management," and "tech" departments, especially in the context of telework. The data collected by the software was accessible to supervisors via a web application. Two versions of the software were used: an "interactive" version on personal computers that required manual activation and deactivation by employees, and a "silent" version installed on company-provided computers that activated and deactivated automatically with the computer's startup and shutdown. The software was subsequently **deactivated on October 17, 2022**. #employment #profiling 
- **Video surveillance system:** In July 2022, the company installed a system comprising **two cameras** at its secondary establishment. The stated purpose of this system was to prevent property damage, such as theft.

The CNIL's involvement began in 2022 following **complaints**. A CNIL delegation conducted on-site inspections at the company's secondary establishment on **October 17 and 20, 2022**. The purpose of these inspections was to verify the company's adherence to the provisions of the French Data Protection Act, the GDPR, and other relevant data protection laws and regulations. Following the controls, the company provided supplementary information to the CNIL between October and December 2022. The company also stated that it removed the video surveillance cameras in July 2024 and provided a detailed information notice to its employees in July 2024.

## Legal Analysis

The CNIL's restricted committee found the company in violation of several articles of the GDPR:

- **Managements related to Excessive Surveillance (Article 5, paragraph 1, c) and Article 6 of the GDPR):**
    
    - **Video Surveillance System:** The system was found to continuously capture **high-definition images and sound** of employees in their workspaces, including break areas. These captations were viewable in real-time via a mobile application. The CNIL determined that the company **failed to justify any exceptional circumstances** for continuous audio and visual capture. Such permanent surveillance was deemed **neither adequate, relevant, nor limited to what was necessary** for the stated purpose of theft prevention, thereby constituting an **excessive intrusion** into employees' rights, violating the principle of **data minimization** (Article 5.1.c). #minimization 
    - **TIME DOCTOR Software:** The software was used for both **measuring work time and evaluating employee productivity**.
        - For work time measurement, it automatically detected and tallied **"idle minutes"**—periods ranging from 3 to 15 minutes without keyboard strokes or mouse movements. These "inactivity" times could lead to **salary deductions** if not justified or recuperated by the employee. The CNIL found this method unreliable for measuring actual work time, as employees might be engaged in other legitimate work activities (e.g., meetings, phone calls) without interacting with their computers.
        - For productivity measurement, the software tracked time spent on websites and applications pre-categorized as "productive" or "non-productive" by the company. It also performed **regular "screencasts" (screen captures)** of employees' computers at intervals of 3 to 15 minutes. This systematic and intrusive monitoring could potentially capture **private information** like personal emails or passwords.
        - The CNIL concluded that these treatments amounted to **disproportionate and permanent automated surveillance**, which could not be justified by the employer's legitimate interest #legitimate_interest , particularly as less intrusive alternatives existed. The use of the "silent" version of the software, which employees could not manually deactivate, further exacerbated the disproportionate nature. Therefore, these processing operations lacked a **lawful basis** under Article 6 of the GDPR.
- **Managements related to the Obligation to Inform Individuals (Articles 12 and 13 of the GDPR):**
    
    - **TIME DOCTOR:** The **written information** provided to employees through internal regulations, IT charter, and employment contracts was **incomplete**. It failed to mention data retention periods, as well as several fundamental data subject rights, including the right to access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, objection, data portability, and the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority. While the company claimed to provide oral information, it could not prove that employees had requested it, nor was there a written record, which prevented establishing its completeness or ensuring its long-term accessibility as required by Article 12 of the GDPR.
    - **Video Surveillance System:** No written or oral information was given to employees regarding the video surveillance. The only signage was a basic pictogram and the phrase "video surveillance area" on a service door, which did not meet the comprehensive information requirements of the GDPR and did not refer to any supplementary information document.
- **Managements related to the Obligation to Ensure Data Security (Article 32 of the GDPR):**
    
    - The company allowed **shared access to the administrator account** for the TIME DOCTOR software, which enabled multiple users (one supervisor and three associates) to view sensitive employee activity data. The CNIL emphasized that **individual user accounts are essential for proper traceability** of access and actions within a system, especially for administrator accounts with extensive data access rights. Shared accounts hinder accountability and complicate security investigations, making it a breach of fundamental security measures.
- **Managements related to the Obligation to Conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) (Article 35 of the GDPR):**
    
    - The company **failed to perform a DPIA** prior to implementing the TIME DOCTOR software #dpia . The CNIL determined that the treatments carried out by the software, including systematic surveillance through "idle minutes" and screen captures, were **likely to result in a high risk for the rights and freedoms of individuals**, particularly given the vulnerable position of employees due to their subordinate relationship with the employer (notably, 60% were interns dependent on the company for diploma validation). Such systematic monitoring of vulnerable individuals explicitly falls under the types of operations for which a DPIA is required by CNIL guidelines. The company's argument that it only used basic monitoring was rejected because it intentionally activated intrusive options.

## Sanction

The CNIL's restricted committee imposed an **administrative fine of 40,000 euros** on the company for the established violations.

In determining the sanction and its amount, the CNIL considered several criteria outlined in Article 83 of the GDPR:

- **Gravity and Nature of the Violations:** The CNIL highlighted that the company violated several **fundamental GDPR principles**, including lawfulness, transparency, and security. The **continuous video and audio capture** and the **permanent, highly intrusive monitoring** via TIME DOCTOR (tracking "idle minutes" and taking frequent screen captures) were deemed particularly severe. These disproportionate treatments, especially affecting **vulnerable employees (60% of whom were interns)**, lacked a valid legal basis. Additionally, the **lack of complete information** provided to employees, the **security failures** regarding shared administrator accounts, and the failure to conduct a **DPIA** (which could have prevented the high-risk processing) further contributed to the gravity.
- **Intentionality and Negligence:** The CNIL found that the company's activation of the "time out" and "screencast" options in TIME DOCTOR demonstrated **intentionality** in committing those specific infringements. The permanent video surveillance was considered to reveal, at the very least, **negligence**. Other violations (information, security, DPIA) were attributed to negligence, compounded by the intentional nature of the intrusive surveillance.
- **Cooperation and Remedial Actions:** The company was acknowledged for its **prompt cooperation** in providing requested information during the control process. It also **deactivated the TIME DOCTOR software immediately** after the controls in October 2022. However, the CNIL noted that the video surveillance cameras were only removed in July 2024, and a comprehensive information notice was provided to employees in July 2024, nearly two years after the initial controls. Despite these later compliance efforts, the infringements were considered to be constituted for the past.
- **Financial Situation:** The CNIL noted that the company's **revenue and net results had increased** between 2020 and 2023, indicating its financial capacity. The fine was considered **dissuasive yet proportionate** to the company's financial standing and the gravity of the breaches.

The CNIL decided to **make the deliberation public** on its website and Légifrance. This decision was based on the gravity of the violations and the aim of informing individuals about their rights concerning such surveillance practices. However, in light of the **limited number of affected employees**, the company's reduced size, and its immediate discontinuation of the TIME DOCTOR software upon control, the decision will be published **without explicitly naming the company**.
