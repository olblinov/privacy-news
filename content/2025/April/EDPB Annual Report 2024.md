# Introduction to the 2024 Annual Report

The European Data Protection Board (EDPB) published its 2024 Annual Report on April 23, 2025, providing an **overview of its work, key milestones, and reflections** on data protection in a rapidly evolving digital landscape. Anu Talus, the EDPB Chair, expressed pride in the achievements of the past year, reaffirming the commitment to **safeguarding individuals' fundamental rights to privacy and data protection**. The report highlights the adoption of the 2024-2027 strategy, an increase in consistency opinions, and ongoing efforts to provide guidance and legal advice. The EDPB also expanded its outreach activities to businesses and non-expert individuals and acquired new roles under recent digital legislations.

# Key Strategic Developments

The **EDPB strategy 2024-2027** outlines key priorities and actions to **strengthen and modernise data protection across Europe**, ensure consistent GDPR enforcement, and address emerging challenges, including cross-regulatory cooperation. This strategy aims to **harmonise data protection** through four main pillars:

- **Pillar 1: Advancing harmonisation and promoting compliance** to ensure consistent and effective application of data protection laws.
- **Pillar 2: Reinforcing a common enforcement culture** to strengthen collaboration among Data Protection Authorities (DPAs) for complex cases and cross-border cooperation.
- **Pillar 3: Addressing technological challenges** with a human-centric approach to emerging technologies, safeguarding fundamental rights.
- **Pillar 4: Enhancing the EDPB’s global role** by engaging with international partners to promote high data protection standards worldwide.

The 2024-2025 Work Programme implements this strategy through key actions, such as providing **concise, practical, and clear guidance** for various audiences, supporting compliance measures, strengthening GDPR enforcement and DPA cooperation, establishing common positions in the cross-regulatory landscape, monitoring new technologies, and promoting global dialogue on privacy.

# Expanding Roles and Responsibilities

In 2024, the EDPB's and DPAs' responsibilities expanded due to new EU digital legislations. These include:

- **AI Act**: Designates DPAs as **Market Surveillance Authorities (MSA)** for certain high-risk AI systems, reinforcing their role in data protection. The EDPB issued a statement emphasizing a prominent role for DPAs at the national level due to their expertise in AI-related issues.
- **Data Act**: DPAs ensure personal data processing aligns with GDPR standards, supported by enhanced cooperation frameworks.
- **Digital Markets Act (DMA)**: The EDPB became a member of the DMA High-Level Group, providing guidance to the European Commission to ensure alignment between data protection law and sectoral regulations.
- **Digital Services Act (DSA)**: The EDPB actively participated in the European Board for Digital Services, supporting oversight of large online platforms and search engines and contributing to the Age Verification Taskforce.
- **Data Governance Act (DGA)**: Alongside the Data Act, these legislations build on the GDPR, requiring the EDPB to actively contribute to cross-regulatory cooperation.

The EDPB Secretariat's work in 2024 reflected this growing complexity, involving proactive input from other regulators, such as meeting with the EU AI Office before adopting the opinion on AI Models.

# Guidance and Legal Advice

The EDPB plays a central role in providing guidance and legal advice, demonstrated by the significant increase in consistency opinions and the adoption of new guidelines.

## Consistency Opinions (Art. 64 GDPR)

In 2024, the EDPB adopted **28 consistency opinions**, which are crucial for the uniform interpretation and application of the GDPR across the EU.

- **Art. 64(1) GDPR Opinions**: These opinions are mandated for specific measures DPAs intend to adopt, such as lists of processing operations requiring Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs), draft codes of conduct, accreditation of certification bodies, draft decisions on Standard Contractual Clauses (SCC), authorisations for custom contractual clauses, and approvals of Binding Corporate Rules (BCRs). In 2024, **20 such opinions were adopted**, contributing to harmonisation.
- **Art. 64(2) GDPR Opinions**: These address matters of general application or those with significant cross-border implications, and can be requested by the EDPB Chair, DPAs, or the European Commission. In 2024, the Board adopted **eight Art. 64(2) GDPR opinions**, a significant increase from previous years. Notable examples include:
    - **Opinion 04/2024 on ‘Main Establishment’**: Clarified that a data controller's "place of central administration in the Union" is a main establishment only if it makes decisions on processing purposes/means and has the power to implement them. The one-stop-shop mechanism applies only under these conditions, and the burden of proof falls on data controllers.
    - **Opinion 08/2024 on ‘Consent or Pay’ Models**: Concluded that most current implementations by large online platforms are **unlikely to meet GDPR requirements for valid consent**, particularly regarding the "freely given" criterion. The EDPB emphasized that platforms offering only a choice between consenting to behavioral advertising or paying a fee often fail to provide a true alternative, creating an **imbalance of power** and potential **detriment to users**. Recommendations for DPAs included urging platforms to offer a free alternative without behavioral advertising, ensuring transparency, and avoiding prohibitively high fees.
    - **Opinion 11/2024 on Facial Recognition at Airports**: Addressed the use of facial recognition for streamlining passenger flow, highlighting concerns about sensitive biometric data processing. The EDPB found that only storage solutions where biometric data is **solely in the individual's hands or in a central database with the encryption key solely in their hands** could be compatible with data protection principles like integrity, confidentiality, and security by design and default.
    - **Opinion 22/2024 on Processor and Sub-processor Reliance**: Clarified data controllers' obligations when engaging processors and sub-processors. Controllers must have information on the identity of all processors and ensure they provide "sufficient guarantees" for data protection. While processors propose sub-processors, the **ultimate decision and responsibility remain with the controller**. The Opinion also addressed requirements for data transfers outside the EEA and the wording of controller-processor contracts.
    - **Opinion 28/2024 on AI Models**: Focused on data protection aspects of processing personal data in AI model development and deployment. It stated that AI models trained on personal data **cannot always be considered anonymous**, requiring case-by-case assessment. It confirmed that **legitimate interest can be a legal basis for AI model training under certain conditions**, provided a three-step test (identifying legitimate interest, necessity, and balancing test) is applied. The opinion also highlighted that unlawful data processing during development can impact the lawfulness of an AI model's subsequent deployment unless duly anonymized.

## General Guidelines

In 2024, the EDPB adopted **four new guidelines** to help organisations achieve and maintain GDPR compliance:

- **Guidelines 01/2023 on Article 37 of the Law Enforcement Directive (LED)**: Provide recommendations for cross-border data transfers by law enforcement authorities of EU countries, explaining appropriate safeguards and essential requirements for legally binding instruments.
- **Guidelines 02/2023 on the Technical Scope of Art. 5(3) of the ePrivacy Directive**: Clarified the scope of this provision for modern online tracking technologies, covering URL and pixel tracking, local processing, IP-only tracking, intermittent IoT reporting, and unique identifiers.
- **Guidelines 01/2024 on processing of personal data based on Article 6(1)(f) GDPR**: Explored legitimate interest as a legal basis, detailing the three cumulative conditions: identification of a legitimate, specific, and present interest; necessity of processing; and a balancing exercise against individuals' fundamental rights.
- **Guidelines 02/2024 on Article 48 GDPR**: Provided clarity on regulating access to personal data by courts and authorities in third countries and its interaction with Chapter V GDPR, emphasizing that such disclosures constitute a transfer and require a legal basis and ground for transfer.

## Participation in Legislative Discussions

The EDPB actively participated in legislative discussions, issuing statements to highlight data protection considerations:

- **Statement 1/2024 on child sexual abuse regulation**: Emphasized the need for measures to comply with fundamental rights and raised concerns over potential general and indiscriminate monitoring of private communications.
- **Statement 2/2024 on the financial data access and payments package**: Called for additional data protection safeguards in transaction monitoring mechanisms to ensure necessity and proportionality.
- **Statement 3/2024 on DPAs’ role in the AI Act framework**: Recommended DPAs be designated as MSAs for high-risk AI systems and stressed enhanced collaboration and transparency in AI deployments.
- **Statement 4/2024 on GDPR enforcement procedural rules**: Supported harmonised procedural rules but called for adequate DPA resources and practical measures for cross-border cases.
- **Statement 5/2024 on law enforcement access to data**: Underlined the importance of safeguarding fundamental rights, raising concerns about broad data retention obligations and measures that could hinder encryption effectiveness.
- **Statement 6/2024 on the Second Report on GDPR Application**: Welcomed reports, stressed legal certainty and coherence between digital legislation and GDPR, and called for additional financial and human resources for DPAs and the EDPB.

# Stakeholder Engagement and Outreach

The EDPB continued to engage proactively with stakeholders to foster dialogue and mutual understanding.

- **Public Consultations**: Served as a vital tool for integrating stakeholder perspectives into guidelines, with feedback thoroughly evaluated and incorporated. Consultations concluded on Guidelines concerning the ePrivacy Directive's Technical Scope and legitimate interest, and a new consultation was launched on Article 48 GDPR guidelines.
- **Stakeholder Events**: Two high-profile events focused on "Consent or Pay" models and AI Models and GDPR compliance, fostering interdisciplinary discussions and gathering insights.
- **Making GDPR Understandable**: The EDPB continued efforts to provide information in clear, non-technical language for a broader audience, including businesses and non-expert individuals. The **Data Protection Guide for Small Business** was made available in **18 languages** in 2024, and a series of summaries of EDPB guidelines were launched to highlight important points.
- **Survey on Practical Application of Adopted Guidance**: Following a 2023 survey, the EDPB implemented recommendations to improve accessibility, such as creating less technical factsheets, including visualisations, and adding executive summaries to all guidelines. The 2024 survey further evaluated effectiveness, with stakeholders finding guidelines and opinions helpful but suggesting more explicit analysis and timely official translations.

# Enforcement and Cooperation

The EDPB plays a critical role in supporting GDPR enforcement and fostering cooperation among DPAs across Europe.

## EDPB Activities to Support Enforcement

- **Coordinated Enforcement Framework (CEF)**: The EDPB selected the **right of access under Art. 15 GDPR** as the focus of its third coordinated enforcement action in 2024, involving 30 participating DPAs. A report adopted in January 2025 consolidated findings from evaluations of 1,185 data controllers, highlighting mixed compliance levels, with larger organizations generally more compliant than SMEs. The report identified **seven areas for improvement**.
- **Support Pool of Experts (SPE)**: This initiative strengthened DPA enforcement capacity, with **nine projects launched in 2024**. Deliverables included a case digest on Security of Processing and Data Breach Notification, an updated EDPB Website Auditing Tool, and a Standardised Messenger Audit. Projects also focused on DPO training and AI Risk Assessment (e.g., Optical Character Recognition, Named Entity Recognition). A **Mobile Apps Bootcamp** was organised, bringing together 50 auditors from 24 countries for training on compliance assessments.
- **Memorandum of Cooperation with PEReN**: Signed in April 2024, this agreement enhances technical collaboration, particularly in areas like mobile application auditing, data science, algorithmic transparency, and tools for trustworthy AI.
- **ChatGPT Taskforce**: Established to address the unique risks associated with ChatGPT's processing activities, it adopted a proactive approach with participating DPAs developing a standardised questionnaire for investigations. Key investigation areas included data accuracy, transparency, fairness, individual rights, web scraping risks, and processing of personal data in model training. The taskforce emphasized the need for "data protection by design and by default" in AI systems.
- **Secondment Programme**: Formalised in 2024, this programme facilitated **61 secondments across 27 authorities**, fostering collaboration, knowledge exchange, and professional networks among DPAs.

## Cooperation Mechanisms

The GDPR provides a robust framework for DPA collaboration, including mutual assistance, joint operations, and the one-stop-shop mechanism. In 2024:

- The case register documented **350 cross-border cases**, indicating a high degree of coordination.
- **982 procedures were initiated under the one-stop-shop mechanism**, resulting in **485 final decisions**.

## Binding Decisions (Art. 65 and 66 GDPR)

These powers are crucial for resolving disputes between DPAs and ensuring consistent GDPR application. Notably, **no binding decisions were adopted by the EDPB in 2024**, which suggests progress in building consensus and efficient resolution of cases at the national level.

## One-Stop-Shop Case Digest

A thematic case digest on the **right of access** (Art. 15 GDPR), also touching on Art. 12 GDPR, was drafted based on 52 selected decisions from January 2019 to April 2024. This digest provides insights into how DPAs interpret the components of the right of access, including confirmation of data processing, access to personal data, and information about processing, as well as exceptions and limitations.

# National Enforcement Actions and Fines

This section provides a non-exhaustive selection of national enforcement actions undertaken by Data Protection Authorities (DPAs) across various European Economic Area (EEA) countries in 2024. These cases highlight diverse regulatory responses to GDPR infringements, including investigations, compliance orders, and significant sanctions and fines. Recurring challenges observed include insufficient technical and organizational measures to secure personal data, processing without a proper legal basis or consent, unlawful processing of special categories of personal data (like health data), failure to provide clear information or uphold individual rights (such as the right to erasure and access), and lack of data breach notifications or inadequate risk assessments. Many of these cases were resolved through the one-stop-shop cooperation mechanism, reflecting coordinated efforts to ensure consistent GDPR application.

Here's a detailed summary of the national enforcement actions and fines:

- **Austria**
    - In 2024, the Austrian DPA performed **647 investigations**, received **3,491 complaints**, and adopted **63 sanctions**, totaling **€1,682,880 in fines**.
    - The most frequent infringements related to unlawful processing of (sensitive) personal data due to violations of data protection principles (Art. 5, Art. 6, and Art. 9 GDPR), infringements of data subjects' rights (Art. 15 to Art. 22 GDPR), and infringement of the obligation to cooperate with the AT DPA (Art. 31 GDPR).
    - **Case 1:** The AT DPA imposed a **€1.5 million fine** in August 2024 on an organization for the **unlawful operation of a video surveillance system** with external and internal cameras (violation of Art. 5 GDPR and Art. 6 GDPR). The fine considered the "undertaking" concept under TFEU to ensure effectiveness, deterrence, and proportionality.
    - **Case 2:** The AT DPA initiated two _ex officio_ investigations regarding plans for an anonymized "abortion register" by two federal states. One investigation was discontinued as no register was planned. For the other, despite claims of anonymity and purpose (identifying supply bottlenecks, abortion, and teenage pregnancy risks), the DPA doubted the sufficiency of anonymization and compatibility with the data controller's tasks (registering medical practices/clinics). A **warning** was issued pursuant to Art. 58(2)(a) GDPR for **lack of a legal basis**.
- **Belgium**
    - In 2024, the Belgian DPA performed **130 investigations**, received **469 complaints**, issued **24 compliance orders**, and adopted **eight financial sanctions**, totaling **€708,371.00 in fines**. Infringements included data breaches in hospitals, data broker issues, and biometric data processing.
    - **Case 1: The Cumuleo.be case**
        - A cross-border complaint was received regarding the publication of the complainant's salary on Cumuleo.be.
        - The Belgian DPA, acting as Lead Supervisory Authority (LSA), determined the website had **rightfully rejected the erasure request** under Art. 17(3) GDPR, as the public interest in freedom of expression outweighed the right to erasure in this instance. The French DPA (CNIL) concurred with the draft decision to dismiss the complaint. #rights 
    - **Case 2: A cookie banner case**
        - In September 2024, the Belgian DPA took action against Mediahuis for the **unlawful use of cookie banners**, following complaints from a Dutch citizen.
        - The DPA reiterated that **deceptive design patterns are unlawful**, requiring "accept all" and "reject all" options to be displayed equivalently.
        - Mediahuis was ordered to adapt its cookie banners without misleading button colors, with a **€25,000 penalty per day** for non-compliance after 45 days. An appeal is ongoing. #cookies 
    - **Case 3: A data broker case**
        - In January 2024, the Belgian DPA imposed **€174,640 in administrative fines** and corrective measures on Black Tiger Belgium, a big data and data management organization.
        - Violations included **unfair processing of data without proactively, individually, and transparently informing individuals**, alongside breaches related to data protection rights exercise and processing activities register.
- **Bulgaria**
    - In 2024, the Bulgarian DPA performed **968 investigations**, received **824 complaints**, issued **38 compliance orders**, and adopted **25 sanctions**, totaling **€159,885 in fines**.
- **Croatia**
    - In 2024, the Croatian DPA performed **623 investigations**, received **1,280 complaints**, issued **153 compliance orders**, and adopted **191 sanctions**, with **38 fines**.
    - Infringements included data breaches concerning registered vehicle owners, processing personal data of business entity owners, cookie and video surveillance processing, DPO appointment issues, and inappropriate technical and organizational measures to protect sensitive data (including health data).
    - **Case 1:** A hospital was fined **€190,000** for **failing to provide copies of health data** and for **irretrievably losing requested medical documentation** due to a lack of backups. This violated Art. 32(1)(b) GDPR, and other provisions including Art. 33(1), Art. 28(3), Art. 6(1), Art. 5(1)(e), Art. 12(1), Art. 13(1)(c), Art. 13(2)(a)(b), and Art. 38(1).
    - **Case 2:** A parking fee collection organization was fined **€80,000** for **unlawfully processing personal data of at least 27,122 individuals** for unlawful purposes and without a lawful basis. Data was obtained from the Ministry of Interior's web service, violating Art. 5(1)(b) and Art. 6(1) GDPR. The organization also failed to implement adequate technical and organizational measures, violating Art. 32(1)(b) and Art. 32(4) GDPR.
    - **Case 3:** A data controller providing basic and financial data on business entities was fined **€40,000** for **processing personal data in violation of Art. 6(1)(f)**, read in conjunction with Art. 5(1)(a) and (e) GDPR. Violations of Art. 12, Art. 13, Art. 14, Art. 30, and Art. 38(3) and (6) GDPR were also identified, infringing the rights of 170,782 individuals.
- **Cyprus**
    - In 2024, the Cypriot DPA performed **44 investigations**, received **513 complaints** (115 for spam), issued **23 compliance orders**, and adopted **22 sanctions** totaling **€133,900.00 in fines**. Ten cases involved Art. 5(1)(f), 24(1), and 32(1) GDPR breaches; four concerned Art. 12 GDPR; one related to Art. 28(3) and 35 GDPR; one to Art. 5(1)(c) GDPR; and one to Art. 15 GDPR.
    - **Case 1: Aylo Freesites Ltd**
        - The CY DPA conducted an _ex officio_ audit of Aylo Freesites Ltd, which operates adult content websites, investigating cookie consent, biometric data processing via third parties, DPIAs, and data processing agreements.
        - Violations of several data protection principles were identified, including accountability, transparency, lawfulness, fairness, data minimization, storage limitation, data security, and the necessity of a legal basis.

    - **Case 2: State Health Services Organisation (SHSO)**
        - The CY DPA investigated **13 data breach notifications** from SHSO, concerning the loss of patients' medical files (10 cases) and registration forms (3 cases).
        - Investigations revealed that SHSO lacked **appropriate technical and organizational measures** (Art. 24(1) GDPR and Art. 32(1) GDPR) and its medical file management procedure was inadequate. Personal data was not processed with appropriate security (Art. 5(1)(f) GDPR).
        - A total fine of **€46,500** was imposed (€5,000 per lost medical file, €500 per lost registration form). Reprimands were issued for one medical file where creation could not be confirmed and for delayed data breach notifications. SHSO was ordered to communicate breaches to affected data subjects in seven cases.
- **Czech Republic**
    - A fine of **CZK 351 million (approximately €13.9 million)** was imposed on a **software organization** for **infringing Art. 6 GDPR and Art. 13(1) GDPR**.
    - The Lead Supervisory Authority (LSA) found that the data controller collected and transferred **pseudonymized internet browsing history** of about 100 million users to its sister organization without a legal basis.
    - The data controller also **misinformed users** by claiming the data was anonymized and used only for statistical trend analytics. The DPA concluded that even incomplete browsing history could be personal data if re-identification was possible. #personal_data  The infringement was considered more serious given the organization's cybersecurity expertise.
- **Denmark**
    - In Denmark, the DPA reports infringements to the police, and the court decides on fines.
    - In 2024, the Danish DPA performed **518 investigations**, received **1,777 complaints**, and proposed **four sanctions totaling at least €2.98 million in fines**.
    - **Case 1:** A municipality was reported to the police for **violating Art. 32 GDPR** by **not encrypting up to 300 computers** that contained confidential and sensitive personal data about children. Three computers were stolen. The Danish DPA recommended a fine of **€26,000**, viewing encryption as a basic, easy-to-implement security measure.
    - **Case 2:** A private hospital was reported to the police for **failing to supervise its three data processors** to the required extent, violating the **principle of responsibility (Art. 5(2) GDPR)**. The hospital could not ensure and demonstrate that personal data was processed in accordance with Art. 5(1) GDPR. The Danish DPA recommended a fine of **€200,000**.
- **Estonia**
    - In 2024, the Estonian Data Protection Inspectorate (EDPI) received **733 complaints** and **184 data breach notifications** affecting over 910,000 individuals. The EDPI issued **116 compliance orders**, conducted **73 own initiative inspections**, and adopted **nine sanctions** totaling **€164,100 in fines and penalty payments**.
    - **Case 1:** A fine of **€85,000** was issued to Asper Biogene OÜ. **€80,000** was for **inadequate security measures for processing personal data** (failing to ensure security per GDPR requirements). **€5,000** was for **breach of duty to avoid conflicts of interest** and appoint a competent Data Protection Officer (DPO).
    - **Case 2:** Viljandi Hospital was fined **€40,000** because employees were asked to provide urine samples to identify the person responsible for medicine theft. The hospital appealed and was successful at first instance, with the EDPI appealing the decision.
    - **Case 3:** The EDPI has an **ongoing supervision for a data leakage** involving approximately **700,000 files with personal and health data**. This was one of Estonia's largest sensitive data leakages.
- **Finland**
    - In 2024, the Finnish DPA conducted **9 audits**, received **1,932 complaints**, issued **9 compliance orders**, and adopted **3 sanctions** totaling **€4,206,000 in fines**. Fines related to defining storage periods for customer data, lawfulness of processing, and neglecting data security.
    - **Case 1:** Posti was fined **€2.4 million** for **unlawful processing of personal data (Art. 6(1) GDPR)**. The organization automatically created electronic mailboxes for customers without a separate request, processing data based on a contract for a wider set of services. The DPA found the service could be provided without automatic mailbox creation. Posti also failed to inform customers clearly and had technical settings that did not meet data protection requirements, leading to reprimands and orders for correction (Art. 5(1)(a), Art. 12(1), Art. 13(1)(c), and Art. 25(1) GDPR). #change 
    - **Case 2:** Online retailer Verkkokauppa.com Oyj was fined **€856,000** for **not specifying the storage period of customer account data (Art. 5(1)(e) GDPR)**. The mandatory customer account creation for online purchases also violated data protection law. The organization was ordered to specify a storage period and rectify its practice, and was reprimanded (Art. 5(1)(e) GDPR and Art. 25(2) GDPR). #retention 
    - **Case 3:** Loan comparison provider Sambla Group was fined **€950,000** for **data security neglect (Art. 5(1)(f) GDPR)**. Due to poor security, customer loan application contents were accessible to third parties via personal links. The organization was ordered to cease processing, and was reprimanded for shortcomings (Art. 5(1)(f) GDPR, Art. 25 GDPR, and Art. 32 GDPR). It was also ordered to notify customers of the data breach.
- **France
    - In 2024, the French DPA performed **321 investigations**, received **17,193 complaints** (closing 15,266), issued **180 compliance orders**, **64 reprimands**, and a total of **87 sanctions** amounting to **€55.2 million in fines**. # EDPB Secretariat Operations

The EDPB Secretariat provides **analytical, administrative, and logistical support** to the Board, ensuring its effective functioning and facilitating the adoption of decisions, opinions, and guidance under the GDPR. In 2024, the Secretariat's work reflected increased complexity and breadth of GDPR implementation. It organised over **530 meetings** (up from 360+ in 2023) and managed over **4,200 IT assistance requests** from members (up from 3,400 in 2023). The Secretariat's operational budget was **€8.36 million**, and it comprises a team of **395 highly specialised professionals**. Significant advancements were made in IT systems, with the Internal Market Information (IMI) system facilitating over **5,644 procedures**. The EDPB HUB, the primary internal communication platform, saw substantial growth, with over **12,307 content pieces created and shared**. The EDPB website received **329,432 visits** in 2024. The Secretariat also handled **38 Access to Documents (AtD) requests** and managed **six data breaches** in 2024.
