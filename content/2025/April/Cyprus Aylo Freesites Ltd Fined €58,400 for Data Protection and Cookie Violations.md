# Summary

The Cypriot Commissioner for Personal Data Protection has issued several decisions in the fourth quarter of 2024, addressing a range of data protection violations and imposing various sanctions, including significant fines, reprimands, and compliance orders. Notably, Aylo Freesites Ltd (formerly Mindgeek) received a total fine of **€58,400** for multiple data protection principle violations and illegal cookie use, following a self-initiated audit. Other cases included an employer's failure to inform a former employee about handling personal items, leading to advice; a company fined **€4,000** and reprimanded for unlawful data transfer, lack of security measures, and non-cooperation; and a health organization fined **€4,500** and ordered to comply for failing to fully satisfy a complainant's right of access to exam data and non-cooperation. The Commissioner also addressed issues concerning access to recorded calls, publication of personal data in a book, improper CCTV installation in a medical office, and unauthorized disclosure of personal data by an insurance company, issuing reprimands or orders for corrective actions.
# Details

## Background

The "Office of the Commissioner for Personal Data Protection" in Cyprus oversees and enforces data protection regulations. The decisions for October-December 2024 detail various investigations initiated either through public complaints or self-initiated controls by the Commissioner's Office.

Key cases include:

- A **self-initiated inspection** was conducted at **Aylo Freesites Ltd (formerly Mindgeek)**, a company managing globally known adult content websites. The inspection focused on issues such as consent for cookies, processing of biometric data via third parties, data protection impact assessments (DPIAs), and data processing agreements.
- A **complaint from a natural person against their employer** concerned the placement of personal items and documents from their former office into boxes and storing them without the employee's knowledge or consent, while the employee was on unpaid leave.
- A **complaint against CMC Certus Management Consultants Ltd** involved individuals whose personal documents, sent for translation, were allegedly shared and transferred, with some documents posted on a translation platform and payment made to a Georgian company.
- A **complaint against the Health Insurance Organization** stemmed from a complainant's request for a recorded phone call as proof of a beneficiary category change, which the organization initially stated was not possible to provide.
- **Two complaints against a natural person** related to the publication of personal data in a book without the prior consent of the individuals involved.
- A **complaint against a medical practitioner** concerned the operation of a Closed-Circuit Video Surveillance (CCTV) system in a medical office, with claims that one camera was aimed at the examination room, another recorded the entrance of an adjacent apartment, and that audio was being recorded.
- A **complaint from a candidate against the Health Insurance Organization** detailed the organization's failure to provide full access to her corrected exam paper, detailed scoring, calculation methods, and correct answers, after she took exams for job positions.
- A **complaint against Eurolife Ltd** involved the disclosure of personal data to unauthorized recipients when the company delivered an open employment termination letter to a relative of the complainant.

## Legal Analysis

The Commissioner's Office identified several violations of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) in these cases:

- **Aylo Freesites Ltd:** Found to have violated multiple **basic data protection principles**, including **accountability, transparency, legality, data minimization, storage limitation, data security, and the necessity of a legal basis for processing**. #enforcement_pattern  These significant gaps were noted four years after the Regulation's implementation. Illegal use of cookies was also identified.
- **Former Employee Complaint:** The employer's failure to inform the complainant about the handling of their documents was deemed a violation of the **Principle of Legality, Objectivity, and Transparency** as established by **Article 5(1)(a)** of the Regulation.
- **CMC Certus Management Consultants Ltd:** Found in violation of **Article 44** of the Regulation for the transfer of complainants' documents to a company in Georgia. A further violation of **Article 5(1)(f)** was identified due to the absence of appropriate technical or organizational measures to prevent the leakage and exposure of documents on the platform. Additionally, the company was cited for **non-cooperation** with the Commissioner's Office, as required by **Article 31** of the Regulation.
- **Recorded Call Access:** The Health Insurance Organization was found to have violated **Article 15(1)** of the Regulation by not providing the complainant with the recorded call, despite the complainant's request being validly exercised.
- **Personal Data Publication in Book:** While specific articles for violations are not explicitly stated for the publication without consent, the Commissioner issued orders implying a lack of legal basis for processing, transparency, and potentially purpose limitation.
- **CCTV in Medical Office:** The medical practitioner was found to operate a CCTV system in a manner that required corrective action to align with data protection principles. The Commissioner provided guidance and issued orders for compliance.
- **Exam Access Right:** The Health Insurance Organization was found to have violated **Article 31** of the Regulation due to non-cooperation, failing to respond to inquiries and provide requested information. It also violated **Article 15(1)** by not fully satisfying the complainant's right of access to all her exam-related data.
- **Eurolife Ltd Data Disclosure:** The company was found to have violated **Articles 5(1)(a) (legality, fairness, transparency), 5(1)(f) (integrity and confidentiality), 6 (lawfulness of processing), and 24(1) (responsibility of the controller)** of the Regulation by delivering an open termination letter to an unauthorized relative.

## Sanction

The Commissioner's Office imposed a range of administrative measures, including fines, reprimands, and orders:

- **Aylo Freesites Ltd:** Initially received an **Order** for compliance. Following corrective measures by the company, a final decision was issued, imposing an **administrative fine of €48,000** for violations that occurred before compliance #enforcement_pattern  and an additional **€10,400** for the illegal use of cookies #cookies . The company paid the imposed fines within the deadline.
- **Former Employee Complaint:** No administrative fine was imposed, considering the specific circumstances and a relevant decision from the CJEU. However, the employer was **advised** to fulfill their obligation to be transparent with employees regarding the processing of their personal data, in accordance with **Article 57(1)(c)** of the Regulation.
- **CMC Certus Management Consultants Ltd:** Received an **administrative fine of €2,000** for the violation of Article 44 and another **administrative fine of €2,000** for the violation of Article 5(1)(f). Additionally, a **reprimand** was issued due to the company's lack of cooperation with the Commissioner's Office, as required by Article 31. The company paid the imposed fines.
- **Recorded Call Access:** An **administrative reprimand** was issued to the Health Insurance Organization. No administrative fine was imposed due to mitigating factors, primarily the satisfaction of the complainant's request for a category change within a month of receiving their message.
- **Personal Data Publication in Book:** The individual received **Orders**: not to proceed with the publication of additional copies of the book or any other book with similar content without prior consent of affected persons, and to remove all information concerning the complainants from the book.
- **CCTV in Medical Office:** The medical practitioner received **Orders** to take appropriate action to ensure the CCTV installation complies with the Commissioner's instructions and to inform the Office of their actions with supporting evidence. The Office is awaiting completion of these actions.
- **Exam Access Right:** The Health Insurance Organization received an **administrative fine of €3,000** for non-cooperation (Article 31) and an **administrative fine of €1,500** for not fully satisfying the right of access (Article 15(1)). The organization was also issued an **Order** to submit the requested information within ten days and another **Order** to fully satisfy the complainant's right of access within three weeks. The organization paid the fine and informed the Office that the right of access was satisfied based on the Commissioner's instructions, with confirmation from the complainant still pending.
- **Eurolife Ltd Data Disclosure:** The company received a **reprimand**. Additionally, an **Order** was issued for the immediate review and amendment of their employment termination notice delivery procedure within one month to ensure compliance with the Regulation and establish a proper legal basis.
