# Overview of the NIST Privacy Framework Update

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) has released a **draft update to its Privacy Framework (PFW), designated as _NIST Privacy Framework 1.1 Initial Public Draft_**. This update is intended to help organizations manage the privacy risks associated with personal data flowing through complex information technology systems. The PFW aims to assist society in benefiting from personal data while simultaneously protecting individual privacy. **Failure to effectively manage these privacy risks can negatively impact individuals, society, and organizations** through brand damage, financial losses, and hindered growth prospects. This is described as a "modest but significant update".

## Purpose and Context

The necessity for changes to the Privacy Framework stems partly from its relationship with the widely utilized **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)**, which received its own update in February 2024. The draft update, released on April 14, 2025, aims to respond to stakeholder needs and enhance the document's usability. The **PFW can be used independently for privacy risk management, but it also maintains compatibility with CSF 2.0**, allowing organizations to manage both privacy and cybersecurity risks comprehensively.

## Relationship with Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

Privacy risk is closely linked to, and often overlaps with, cybersecurity risk. To facilitate their combined use, both the Privacy Framework and the Cybersecurity Framework share the **same high-level structure**. A key shared element is the "Core," which provides a granular set of activities and outcomes to aid in risk management discussions. The PFW 1.1 Public Draft's Core has been **realigned with the CSF 2.0 Core in many areas**, making it easier for users to apply both frameworks in conjunction.

# Key Changes in PFW 1.1 Draft

Among the notable revisions in the draft update for PFW 1.1 are several targeted changes designed to improve its utility and relevance.

## Targeted Revisions to the Core

The PFW's draft update includes **specific changes to its core structure and content**. Some of these revisions are made to maintain alignment with CSF 2.0, particularly focusing on the **Govern Function** (which deals with risk management strategy and policies) and the **Protect Function** (which pertains to privacy and cybersecurity safeguards). Other improvements are a direct response to feedback gathered from stakeholders over the past five years, including input from channels such as the NIST Privacy Workforce Public Working Group.

## New Section on AI and Privacy Risk Management

A significant addition to the PFW 1.1 draft is a **new section dedicated to Artificial Intelligence (AI) and privacy risk management**. The initial version of the PFW was released before AI tools, such as chatbots, became widespread. Section 1.2.2 of the draft PFW now briefly outlines how AI and privacy risks intersect and how PFW 1.1 can be leveraged to manage AI-related privacy risks.

## Relocation of PFW Use Guidelines

The guidelines for using the PFW have been **moved from Section 3 of the document to the web**. This online material is structured as an **interactive FAQ page**, designed to help users quickly find answers to their questions. Keeping this information online also allows for **timely updates** in response to evolving user needs. In addition to these interactive FAQs, NIST also maintains a **PFW Learning Center**, which offers quick-start guides in multiple languages and a PFW 1.1 Highlights video detailing the updates.

# Public Feedback and Next Steps

NIST is actively soliciting **public comments on the draft _NIST Privacy Framework 1.1 Initial Public Draft_**. Feedback can be submitted via privacyframework@nist.gov until **June 13, 2025**. A template for submitting comments is available on the NIST Privacy Framework website. Following the comment period, NIST will review the feedback and consider additional changes before releasing a final version later in the calendar year.

# About NIST

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) is an agency of the United States government.

## Government Affiliation and Website Security

NIST's official website uses the **.gov domain**, indicating it belongs to an official government organization in the United States. Secure .gov websites use HTTPS, symbolized by a lock icon or "https://" in the URL, ensuring a safe connection.

## Organizational Structure and Relevant Divisions

NIST encompasses various laboratories and divisions involved in diverse scientific and technological fields. The **Information Technology Laboratory** and specifically the **Applied Cybersecurity Division** and the **Cybersecurity and Privacy Applications Group** are directly involved in the development and updates of frameworks like the Privacy Framework. NIST also maintains resources such as the Computer Security Resource Center (CSRC) and engages in various programs and research areas including cybersecurity, information technology, and standards development.
