# Enforcement and Sanctions

The CNIL significantly **intensified its repressive action** in 2024.

## Inspections and Controls

- The CNIL carried out **321 controls** in 2024, including **166 on-site**, **99 online**, **44 on documents**, and **12 on audition**. These targeted both public and private bodies, focusing on areas like cookies, cybersecurity, and video devices.
- **Priority Themes for Controls in 2024**: These themes accounted for about 30% of controls.
    1. **Data Collection for the Olympic and Paralympic Games**: **22 controls** were conducted between April and October 2024 on security zones (QR codes), "augmented" video protection cameras, spectator/participant data, and volunteer HR management. The CNIL generally found overall compliance but verified adherence to legal frameworks, particularly for experimental augmented cameras.
    2. **Minors' Data Collected Online**: Controls on **10 companies** (social networks, messaging apps, video games) with millions of users focused on age control mechanisms, security measures, and data minimization. Common breaches included **excessive data retention**, **lack of clear information for young users**, and **tracking without consent**.
    3. **Large-Scale Data Breaches**: Following a surge in very large-scale breaches, the CNIL launched controls on major public and private entities that were victims of attacks (e.g., France Travail, Free, Viamedis, Almerys, Auchan). These revealed that **basic security measures often recommended by CNIL and ANSSI could have prevented the breaches**.
    4. **Public Authority Use of Video Analysis Software**: Controls on the Ministry of Interior and several communes examined the use of automatic image analysis software. The CNIL issued a **formal notice to the Ministry of Interior** for non-transmission of compliance commitments and Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs), and for isolated use of facial recognition. **Six communes also received formal notices** for using augmented cameras for real-time analysis of public behavior, which is not currently authorized by law.
- **Deceptive Cookie Banners**: Over **40 online controls** were conducted in response to complaints about misleading cookie consent banners. The CNIL reiterated that refusing cookies must be as simple as accepting them and issued formal notices to several website publishers. #cookies 

## Record Number of Complaints and Corrective Measures

- **Complaints Received**: 2024 was a **record year** for complaints, with a total of **17,772 complaints** received in raw data. Excluding a series of 2,423 complaints at year-end still under investigation, the CNIL processed **15,639 complaints**, which was more than the **15,350 net complaints received**.
- **Most Common Complaint Themes**: The "telecoms, web and social networks" theme generated the most complaints (**49%**), followed by commerce (**19%**) and work (**13%**). Data leaks, including theft of bank details and identity theft risks, were a particularly strong concern.
- **Indirect Rights Exercise (EDI)**: The CNIL processed **14,654 indirect rights requests** out of 24,947 received in 2024. The majority (**91%**) concerned the national financial accounts file (FICOBA). As of January 6, 2025, individuals now contact tax services directly for FICOBA access, ending the CNIL's teleservice for this.
- **Corrective Measures**: The CNIL issued a total of **331 corrective measures** in 2024.
    - **Formal Notices (Mises en demeure)**: **180 formal notices** were issued in 2024. Major themes included security of computerized patient records (DPI), where access must be limited to authorized personnel, and organizations' failure to respond to data subject rights requests (access, opposition, erasure).
    - **Legal Reminders (Rappels aux obligations légales)**: **64 reminders** were issued by the President. Three ministries received reminders for not ensuring data accuracy in their databases, specifically regarding judicial records not being updated for acquittals or relaxations.

## Sanctions and Fines

- The CNIL issued **87 sanctions** in 2024, a **marked increase** and almost double the number from 2023. These sanctions totaled **€55,212,400 in fines**.
- **Simplified Procedure**: This procedure saw a significant increase, with **69 sanctions** handed down in 2024, nearly three times as many as in 2023. These totaled **€715,500 in fines**. The most frequent breaches sanctioned via this procedure were **non-cooperation with the CNIL** (27 organizations) and **non-respect of individuals' rights** (23 decisions). #enforcement_pattern  Other common issues included data minimization breaches and insufficient data security measures (e.g., weak passwords, cleartext storage, obsolete protocols).
- **Ordinary Procedure**: **18 sanctions** were pronounced through the ordinary procedure, including 13 fines (two with injunctions under penalty payment) and two penalty payment liquidations, totaling **€54,496,900**.
- **Key Sanctioned Breaches**:
    - **Commercial Prospecting**: Repeated sanctions for non-compliant data collection for electronic commercial prospecting, emphasizing the need for consent and clear information. #direct_marketing 
    - **Health Data Protection**: Decisions underlined that pseudonymized health data, if re-identifiable, remain personal data requiring CNIL authorization. #sensitive 
    - **Data Security Insufficiencies**: Sanctions for inadequate technical measures, such as insecure passwords or obsolete protocols. #security 
- **Noteworthy Sanctions**:
    - **Orange**: Fined **€50 million** on November 14, 2024, for displaying advertisements within its email service without users' prior consent. This was deemed direct electronic prospecting requiring consent. #direct_marketing 
    - **Real Estate Company**: Fined **€40,000** on December 19, 2024, for **disproportionate surveillance of employees** using software that detected inactivity and took regular screenshots, which was considered an infringement on fundamental rights. #profiling 
    - **Uber**: In cooperation with the Dutch data protection authority, a **€290 million fine** was pronounced against Uber B.V. and Uber Technologies Inc. in July 2024 for **non-compliant transfers of drivers' personal data outside the EU**. #transfers 

## Litigation

The CNIL's decisions are largely upheld by the Conseil d’État (Council of State). In 2024, out of 30 decisions rendered by the administrative judge, all but one (a non-lieu) **confirmed the legality of the CNIL's decisions**. A significant decision on November 19, 2024, confirmed a **€200,000 fine against SAF LOGISTICS**, an air freight company, for collecting sensitive personal data (e.g., political affiliation, ethnicity) from employees without consent and for non-cooperation. The court affirmed the responsibility of the French subsidiary even if the processing was initiated by its Chinese parent company.

# Focus Areas: AI, Minors, Cybersecurity

## Artificial Intelligence (AI)

The rise of artificial intelligence, particularly generative AI, is a central technological and economic prospect, but it inherently requires the collection of large amounts of data, often personal. #ai 

- **EU AI Act**: The European AI Act, entering into application in 2025, aims to regulate the development, placement on the market, and use of AI systems that may pose risks. It establishes four risk levels and complements GDPR requirements. The CNIL, as a member of the European Data Protection Board (EDPB), advocates for data protection authorities to act as national market surveillance authorities for the AI Act to ensure coordination with GDPR.
- **CNIL's AI Action Plan**: Following its 2023 action plan, the CNIL published its first recommendations in 2024 in the form of **12 practical information sheets** (9 in their final version) to guide the development of AI systems in a data protection-compliant manner. These fiches provide concrete answers on legal and technical challenges, covering aspects like defining legal regimes and purposes, actor qualification, legal bases, data reuse testing, DPIA, privacy by design, data collection/management, legitimate interest, informing individuals, exercising rights, data annotation, and security.
- **Generative AI Guidance**: The CNIL also published an initial series of questions and answers in July 2024 to help actors reconcile generative AI with GDPR, emphasizing the need for precautions when using personal data. It recommends starting with concrete needs and assessing risks based on intended uses and system limitations.
- **Awareness Campaign**: In January 2024, the CNIL conducted a unique awareness campaign in La Roche-sur-Yon, using a simulated voice assistant named "Germain" to demonstrate how users inadvertently share personal data due to the human-like nature of AI interactions. This led to the publication of **8 practical tips** for interacting with voice assistants, advising users to share minimum information, avoid sensitive data, delete data regularly, and verify information.
- **Deepfakes/Hyperfakes**: The CNIL's Digital Innovation Lab (LINC) partnered with PEReN to study the risks of AI in the context of hyperfakes (deepfakes). Research showed that online tools are easily accessible, highly performant, and can produce fakes that are almost undetectable by humans, posing risks like identity theft, fraud, and disinformation.

## Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is a priority axis for CNIL's 2025-2028 strategic plan.

- **Data Breach Upsurge**: In 2024, the CNIL was notified of **5,629 personal data breaches**, a **20% increase** from 2023. A concerning trend is the **upsurge in very large-scale breaches**, with the number affecting over one million people doubling in a year (from about twenty to forty successful attacks). These affected various sectors, including internet access providers, third-party payers, e-commerce, and public services.
- **Common Breach Methods**: CNIL analysis of notifications revealed recurring methods: compromised login information, undetected intrusions/exfiltrations, and significant involvement of subcontractors. Common attack pathways involve obtaining login data (e.g., via phishing, malware, stolen credentials), accessing information systems (e.g., through unprotected internet access or firewall vulnerabilities), and massive data access/extraction due to overly broad authorizations or lack of monitoring.
- **Notification Obligations**: Organizations must notify the CNIL of a data breach that poses a risk to individuals, ideally within **72 hours**. **One-third of sanctions issued by the CNIL targeted a lack of data security**.
- **Cybersecurity Collaborations**: The CNIL works closely with the **Agence nationale de la sécurité des systèmes d'information (ANSSI)**, the cyber section of the Paris public prosecutor's office (J3), and **Cybermalveillance.gouv.fr** to prevent and limit consequences for individuals.
- **New Data Security Guide**: The CNIL published a **new edition of its personal data security guide** in March 2024 to assist organizations in implementing security measures. The updated guide includes new fiches on cloud computing, mobile applications, AI, and APIs. It provides practical advice, bad practices to avoid, and advanced measures. The CNIL highlights basic indispensable measures like **timely updates**, **robust unique passwords**, **periodic user awareness**, **email access protection**, and **regular offline backups**.
- **Cloud Security**: In January 2024, the CNIL issued **two new practical fiches on cloud security** to address concerns about GDPR compatibility and data transfers. The CNIL also raised concerns in July 2024 about a proposed European cloud certification (EUCS) that might not adequately protect data from unauthorized access by non-EU authorities, unlike France's SecNumCloud 3.2.
- **Computerized Patient Records (DPI)**: The CNIL issued formal notices to several health establishments in early 2024 to secure DPIs after identifying illegitimate access incidents in 13 controls since 2020. Key measures reiterated include **robust authentication**, **specific authorizations** based on role and care team, and **traceability of access** with regular checks for fraudulent access. A public consultation on a guide for DPI security is planned for Q1 2025.

# Cooperation and Partnerships

The CNIL actively reinforces its cooperation at national, European, and international levels to ensure the protection of personal data in an increasingly interconnected digital world.

## National Partnerships

- **With Arcom and DGCCRF**: On June 27, 2024, the CNIL, Arcom (the French regulatory authority for audiovisual and digital communication), and the DGCCRF (General Directorate for Competition, Consumer Affairs and Fraud Control) signed a convention for the implementation of the **European Digital Services Act (DSA)**. This aims to hold digital economy actors accountable for illicit content and algorithmic transparency, facilitating information sharing for investigations and complaints. An updated cooperation protocol was also signed with the DGCCRF on November 18, 2024, aiming to harmonize legal interpretations, share expertise, and conduct joint economic analyses, especially on manipulative design patterns ("dark patterns").
- **With _Départements de France_**: On June 12, 2024, the CNIL renewed its partnership with the association representing French departments (local authorities) until 2027. Priorities include facilitating data circulation for public policy and supporting AI use for service improvement while respecting GDPR.
- **With Professional Bodies**: Partnerships were renewed with the **Conseil supérieur de l’ordre des experts-comptables** (accountants) on June 27, 2024, to promote data protection culture among its 22,000 professionals, and with the **Conférence des grandes écoles** (leading higher education institutions) on February 19, 2024, to continue GDPR awareness actions for staff and students.
- **With Autorité de la concurrence (ADLC)**: A mission led by Bruno Lasserre in November 2024 explored synergies between data protection and competition, concluding that these regulations cannot operate in silos in the digital economy. The mission proposed **15 operational recommendations** to foster cooperation, such as establishing dedicated contact points and encouraging the ADLC to refer cases involving privacy and personal data to the CNIL in concentration matters.

## European Cooperation

The CNIL plays a **key role within the European Data Protection Board (EDPB)**, actively contributing to the drafting and adoption of various documents.

- **"Consent or Pay" Model**: On April 17, 2024, the EDPB issued an opinion on the "Consent or Pay" model offered by some large platforms, where users either consent to behavioral advertising or pay a fee. The EDPB stated that a binary choice between consent and payment is often insufficient for valid consent and strongly encouraged platforms to offer a **free alternative without behavioral advertising** (e.g., contextual advertising).
- **ePrivacy Directive "Tracking"**: On October 7, 2024, the EDPB adopted final guidelines clarifying the notion of "tracking" under the ePrivacy Directive, specifically addressing concepts like "information," "terminal equipment," and "storage/access to stored information," and applying them to various advertising ecosystem practices like tracking links, pixels, and unique identifiers.
- **AI Models**: The EDPB adopted an opinion on December 18, 2024, regarding the processing of personal data for the development and deployment of AI models, which marks the first harmonized European position on the matter.
- **Facial Recognition in Airports**: On May 24, 2024, the EDPB adopted an opinion on facial recognition used by airports and airlines, stating that RGPD-compatible scenarios are those where individuals retain control over their biometric data, typically involving mobile phone-based storage or activation. This opinion was in response to a CNIL request for a harmonized EU position.
- **Enforcement Cooperation**: The CNIL received **630 draft decisions from its European counterparts** in 2024, including **12 related to financial sanctions exceeding €20,000**. Its close cooperation with the Dutch DPA led to the significant **€290 million fine against Uber** for non-compliant data transfers. The CNIL also participated in the EDPB's coordinated enforcement framework (CEF), which focused on the **right of access** in 2024.
- **Cross-Regulatory Interplay and Cooperation (CIC)**: An EDPB task force, co-coordinated by the CNIL, transformed into a sub-group in late 2024 to address the intersection of data protection, competition, and consumer protection, as well as interregulation with the European digital package.

## International Actions

- **G7 Data Protection Authorities**: From October 7-11, 2024, in Rome, the G7 DPAs, under the Italian authority's presidency, adopted a **common position on their role in promoting trustworthy AI** and emphasized the importance of protecting minors' fundamental rights in AI development.
- **California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA)**: The CNIL and CPPA signed a **cooperation declaration** in Paris on June 25, 2024. This agreement outlines joint research on new technologies, sharing best practices and experiences (including investigations), and organizing workshops.

# Resources

## Financial Resources

In 2024, the CNIL's budget amounted to **€28,238,114**. Despite budget cuts of €142,000 for mass salaries and €16,743 for operating expenses imposed by the government in February 2024, the CNIL maintained rigorous and effective management of its allocated funds. **87% of its operating budget consists of incompressible expenses**, leaving a limited margin for innovative projects. The CNIL warns that further budget reductions in 2025 could jeopardize its ability to satisfactorily carry out its essential missions.

## Human Resources

The CNIL ended 2024 with **298 agents** (full-time equivalents), a gain of **10 new positions** from 2023. These recruitments were crucial for supporting the evolution of its missions and responding to increased demands. The new positions reinforced the repressive chain and professionalized various roles. While efforts were made to revalue individual compensation for staff, budget constraints in 2024 led to delayed recruitments and a reduction in apprentices and temporary contracts, preventing the CNIL from fully reaching its employment ceiling of 292 ETPT. The CNIL's workforce has an average age of **40 years**, with **62% of agents having joined between 2019 and 2024**. The staff is predominantly female (**62%**) and **84%** of agents hold category A positions.
