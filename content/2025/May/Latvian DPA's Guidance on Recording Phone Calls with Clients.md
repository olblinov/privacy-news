# Core Principles for Recording Client Phone Calls

When businesses record phone calls with clients, this action is considered **personal data processing**. A **voice recording becomes personal data when it can be linked to a specific individual**. Consequently, such data processing must adhere to the requirements of the Data Regulation (GDPR).

# Requirements for Data Processing

## Purpose of Recording

Businesses must first establish an **appropriate and precise purpose** for recording phone calls. For instance, a purpose could be to assess the quality of consultations, evaluate employee competence, and ethical conduct in client communication. It is crucial that the **recorded data is used exclusively for the specified purpose** and not for any unrelated objectives. For example, if a store records calls to evaluate consultant behavior, the recordings should only be used for that specific assessment.

## Legal Basis for Processing

The legal basis for processing recorded phone calls is typically the **controller's legitimate interests**. This basis applies when a business processes data to protect its interests, such as improving the quality of consultations or services, enhancing communication with clients, and potentially boosting sales. Before implementing such processing, a **balancing test must be conducted** to ensure that this data processing does not excessively infringe upon clients' rights to data protection.

## Data Retention

Recordings of conversations should only be stored for **as long as necessary to achieve the defined purpose**. Once the purpose has been met (e.g., after the consultation quality has been assessed and improvements identified), the saved recordings **must be deleted**. Businesses are required to **pre-determine the storage period** before beginning to record calls and must be able to explain the justification for the chosen duration (e.g., why two months were chosen over one or three). An example illustrates recordings being kept for two months, and then deleted, because the analysis was time-consuming, but the purpose was achieved.

## Data Security and Access

Access to these recordings should be limited to **only those authorized personnel whose duties are directly related to the purpose of processing the recordings**. Businesses are responsible for implementing **appropriate technical and organizational security measures** to safeguard the data against unauthorized access, loss, or destruction.

## Informing Clients (Transparency)

Businesses are obligated to **inform clients about the recording at the very beginning of the phone call**. This notification should include the **purpose of the recording** and **contact information** where clients can direct questions about data processing. Other essential information, such as storage terms, the legal basis for processing, and data subject rights, must be provided in the company's **privacy policy**.

A standard initial announcement might be: "Informējam, ka sarunas kvalitātes novērtēšanas nolūkos saruna tiks ierakstīta". However, a more recommended introduction for recorded calls is: **“Lai nodrošinātu labāku klientu apkalpošanu, šī saruna tiek ierakstīta. Ja jums ir jautājumi, lūdzu, sazinieties ar mums pa e-pastu...”**. The announcement can also mention the business's name, the purpose of data processing, contact details, or a link to a more detailed explanation about the data processing.

It is important to note that **recording conversations without prior notification does not comply with the Data Regulation requirements and is not permissible**. In practice, it is advisable for businesses to always inform callers about the recording and data processing, regardless of whether they are easily identifiable or anonymous, as providing a short notice is simpler than later evaluating if a specific person could be identified.
