# Current Compliance Checks on AI Systems

Due to the current enthusiasm surrounding Artificial Intelligence (AI), many companies are taking their first steps with AI systems. The development and deployment of AI systems often involve processing a **large amount of personal data**. Berlin authorities are currently examining the extent to which companies in Berlin comply with data protection regulations in several ongoing procedures.

# Key Focus Areas in Data Protection Procedures

The ongoing procedures primarily focus on two essential questions regarding AI systems:

## Legal Basis for Data Processing

The first question is: **Which legal basis is used for the processing of personal data?**.

- Many companies rely on **legitimate interest** as per Art. 6 Abs. 1 Satz 1 lit. f DSGVO.
- The lawfulness of such processing depends on two conditions:
    - It must be necessary for safeguarding the **legitimate interests of the controller or third parties**.
    - **No overriding protective interests, fundamental rights, or fundamental freedoms of the data subjects** should outweigh these interests.

## Transparency and Information Obligations

The second question is: **Is the development or deployment of AI made transparent to the data subjects?**.

- Many companies either **do not inform data subjects at all or do so insufficiently** about their data processing in connection with their AI systems.
- For instance, AI chatbots have been found on websites without any information about the associated processing of personal data.
- This failure to comply with **information obligations** under Art. 13 or Art. 14 DSGVO can **directly impact the lawfulness of data processing**.
- According to rulings by the European Court of Justice (ECJ), if controllers invoke legitimate interest but fail to even inform data subjects of this interest, the processing is considered inadmissible.

# Examples from Ongoing AI Review Procedures

The source provides three examples from the ongoing AI review procedures. #enforcement_pattern 

## Case 1: AI-based Debt Collection

- Authorities examined a company offering **AI-based debt management**.
- A key aspect of the review was whether the purported **anonymization of debtor data** used for training the AI models genuinely resulted in anonymous data **without re-identification possibilities**. #anonymization 
- The company primarily uses these AI systems to enable a **personalized approach** for more successful debt collection.
- Additionally, the review focused on the **profiling of individual debtors** and any potentially **prohibited automated decision-making** regarding the initiation of legal steps by the company against the debtors. #profiling 

## Case 2: Commercial Photo Platform

- Authorities investigated a commercial photo platform that, to their current knowledge, offered photos already uploaded to the internet (at least partially classified as personal data) to companies for a fee, including for **training AI models**.
- This practice was **only partially described in the platform's privacy policy**.

## Case 3: Real Estate Brokerage Platform

- This case came to light as part of a **complaint procedure**.
- The operator of a real estate brokerage platform used both past and newly incoming **customer communication for training an AI system** aimed at more efficient processing of customer inquiries.
- However, the operator **failed to inform the customers about this processing**. #transparency 

# Status of Review Procedures

All the mentioned review procedures are **currently ongoing**. Further information and additional examples related to AI procedures will be available in the **2024 Annual Report**, scheduled for publication on **May 26, 2025**.
