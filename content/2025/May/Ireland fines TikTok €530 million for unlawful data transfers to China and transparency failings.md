# Summary

The Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC), acting as the Lead Supervisory Authority for TikTok, has issued a final decision against TikTok Technology Limited, finding it infringed the GDPR regarding **transfers of EEA User Data to China and its transparency requirements**. The DPC imposed administrative fines totaling **€530 million** and ordered TikTok to bring its processing into compliance within six months, with a further order to suspend transfers to China if compliance is not achieved within this timeframe. The inquiry revealed that TikTok failed to verify and guarantee an essentially equivalent level of protection for EEA user data accessed remotely by staff in China and provided an inadequate privacy policy regarding data transfers.

# Details

## Background

The Irish Data Protection Commission (DPC) launched an inquiry into TikTok Technology Limited, serving as TikTok's Lead Supervisory Authority. The inquiry focused on two main areas: **the lawfulness of TikTok's transfers of personal data of users in the EEA to the People's Republic of China** and whether TikTok's provision of information about such transfers to users met its transparency requirements under the GDPR.

During the inquiry, TikTok initially informed the DPC that it did not store EEA User Data on servers in China. However, in February 2025, TikTok discovered and subsequently informed the DPC in April 2025 that **limited EEA User Data had, in fact, been stored on servers in China**, contradicting its earlier evidence. Although TikTok stated this data has since been deleted, the DPC is seriously considering further regulatory action regarding this erroneous information and storage issue, in consultation with other EU Data Protection Authorities.

## Legal Analysis

The DPC's decision found TikTok infringed the GDPR on two primary counts:

- **Unlawful Data Transfers (Article 46(1) GDPR)**: TikTok's transfers of personal data to China infringed the GDPR because the company **failed to verify, guarantee, and demonstrate that the personal data of EEA users, remotely accessed by staff in China, was afforded a level of protection essentially equivalent to that guaranteed within the EU**. The GDPR requires a high level of protection for personal data throughout the EEA, and this protection must continue when data is transferred outside the EEA. Transfers to a third country without an Adequacy Decision from the European Commission (which China does not have) require organizations to verify that the recipient country's laws and practices provide an essentially equivalent level of protection, often through measures like Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs).
    
    - The DPC noted that TikTok's own assessment of Chinese law, provided during the inquiry, identified aspects that materially diverge from EU standards, such as the Anti-Terrorism Law, Counter-Espionage Law, Cybersecurity Law, and National Intelligence Law. #transfers #change 
    - TikTok's failure to adequately assess Chinese law's level of protection directly impacted its ability to select appropriate safeguards and ensure essential equivalence. The DPC considered these findings even as TikTok maintains that transfers via remote access are not subject to the Chinese laws in question.
- **Transparency Requirements (Article 13(1)(f) GDPR)**: The DPC found that TikTok's October 2021 EEA Privacy Policy was inadequate. #transparency 
    
    - **It did not name the third countries, including China**, to which personal data was transferred. #change 
    - It **failed to explain the nature of the processing operations** that constituted the transfer, specifically not mentioning remote access to personal data stored in Singapore and the United States by personnel based in China.
    - TikTok updated its Privacy Policy in December 2022, which the DPC assessed as compliant for the data transfers within the decision's scope. This updated policy identified the third countries (including Brazil, China, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, and the United States) and explained that personal data stored in the US and Singapore was subject to remote access by entities in TikTok's corporate group in those locations. #transparency 
    - The infringement period for transparency was from July 29, 2020, to December 1, 2022.

## Sanction

The DPC's decision, notified to TikTok, includes:

- **Administrative fines totaling €530 million**. This amount is composed of:
    
    - **€45 million** for the infringement of Article 13(1)(f) GDPR (transparency requirements).
    - **€485 million** for the infringement of Article 46(1) GDPR (unlawful data transfers).
- An **order requiring TikTok to bring its processing into compliance** with Chapter V of the GDPR within **6 months**.
    
- An **order suspending TikTok's transfers to China** if processing is not brought into compliance within this 6-month timeframe. The DPC considers this 6-month period reasonable for TikTok to cease the transfers, notwithstanding ongoing changes like "Project Clover".
