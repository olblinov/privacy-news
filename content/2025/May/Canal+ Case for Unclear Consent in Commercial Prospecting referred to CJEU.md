# Summary

The Conseil d'État in France is handling an appeal by Groupe Canal + against a €600,000 administrative fine imposed by the CNIL for alleged General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and ePrivacy Directive violations related to electronic commercial prospecting. The core legal issue revolves around the sufficiency of consent obtained by initial data collectors (Internet Service Providers or FAI) when they inform subscribers that their data may be used by "partners" for commercial prospecting, without specifically identifying those partners. #consent Due to the complex and determinative nature of these questions for the dispute, the Conseil d'État has decided to stay the proceedings and refer the matter to the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) for a preliminary ruling on the interpretation of "informed consent" regarding recipient categories.

# Details

## Background

Groupe Canal + is appealing a **€600,000 administrative fine** imposed by the **Commission nationale de l'informatique et des libertés (CNIL)** through its deliberation n° SAN-2023-015 dated October 12, 2023. The fine was issued for alleged breaches of several articles of the **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** (specifically, articles 7(1), 12, 13, 14, 15, 28, 32, and 33) and **Article L. 34-5 of the Code des postes et des communications électroniques**.

The case originated because Groupe Canal + **commissioned electronic commercial prospecting operations** targeting approximately 3.9 million individuals in 2021. The personal data of these prospects had been collected by two Internet Service Providers (FAI) from their subscribers. During the data collection, individuals were asked to consent to their data being used for prospecting by the FAI's "partners" by ticking a box in the forms. However, these "partners," including Groupe Canal +, were **not explicitly identified by name** in the collection form, through a hyperlink, or by any other means. Groupe Canal + subsequently acquired these lists of individuals but did not obtain explicit consent directly from them for its prospecting activities. The CNIL concluded that Groupe Canal + had therefore failed to fulfill its obligation to obtain proper consent for electronic commercial prospecting.

Groupe Canal + argues that the consent given by the individuals, as collected by the FAI, was **sufficiently informed** for them to receive commercial prospecting on its behalf, given that they had agreed to the use of their data by the FAI's partners.

## Legal Analysis

The legal analysis centers on the interpretation of "consent" and "information" requirements under EU data protection law. The relevant legal provisions include:

- **Article 4(11) of the GDPR**, which defines consent as "any freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous indication of the data subject's wishes by which he or she, by a statement or by a clear affirmative action, signifies agreement to the processing of personal data relating to him or her".
- **Article 7(1) of the GDPR**, which states that where processing is based on consent, the controller must be able to demonstrate that the data subject has given consent.
- **Article L. 34-5 of the French Code des postes et des communications électroniques**, which transposes **Article 13 of Directive 2002/58/CE (ePrivacy Directive)**. This article **prohibits direct electronic prospecting** using a person's contact details unless that person has given prior consent. For this article, consent is defined as "any freely given, specific and informed indication of the data subject's wishes by which a person accepts that personal data concerning him or her be used for direct prospecting purposes".
- **Article 13(1) and Article 14(1) of the GDPR**, which require that when personal data is collected from the data subject, or not from the data subject, the data controller must provide clear and complete information, including their identity and contact details, and the **recipients or categories of recipients** of the personal data.

The core legal question for the Conseil d'État is whether consent given to an initial data collector (the FAI) for data use by a _category_ of recipients, such as "partners" of the FAI, can be considered **"informed consent"** if the identities of these specific partners were not known to the data subject at the time of consent. Groupe Canal + is considered the data controller for the commercial prospecting operations it implemented. As such, it must demonstrate that the individuals provided informed consent for their data to be processed for this purpose. #consent 

The Conseil d'État has identified **two serious and determinant questions** that require clarification from the CJEU:

1. Whether **Article 4(11) of the GDPR, read in conjunction with Articles 13 and 14 of the GDPR and Article 13 of the 2002 Directive**, should be interpreted to mean that consent given to an initial data collector for data use by a category of recipients (like FAI "partners") can be considered "free, specific, informed, and unambiguous," allowing any entity within that category to perform electronic commercial prospecting without seeking new consent. Alternatively, do these provisions require that any recipient whose identity was not known at the time of initial consent must obtain new consent before conducting commercial prospecting as a new data controller?. #consent #change 
2. If consent to a category of recipients can be considered informed, **what degree of precision is required for the notion of "category"** of recipients? Can it simply refer to "any partner" of the initial collector, as was the case here?.

## Decision

The Conseil d'État, in its decision dated May 5, 2025, has taken the following actions:

- It has **admitted the intervention** of several professional unions and associations: DMA France, CPA, Alliance Digitale, and Le Geste, recognizing their sufficient interest in the annulment of the challenged decision.
- It has decided to **stay the proceedings** concerning the appeal filed by Groupe Canal +.
- This stay is pending a **preliminary ruling from the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU)** on the two specific questions outlined in the "Legal Analysis" section. This referral is made in application of Article 267 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union.

The Conseil d'État will await the CJEU's pronouncement before making a final decision on Groupe Canal +'s request to annul or reduce the administrative fine.
