# Summary

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) has ordered national clothing retailer Todd Snyder, Inc., to pay a **$345,178 fine** and implement significant changes to its business practices. This decision resolves allegations that Todd Snyder violated the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) by failing to properly process consumer requests to opt out of data sale or sharing for 40 days, demanding excessive information for privacy requests, and requiring identity verification before consumers could exercise their opt-out rights. The CPPA emphasized that businesses are responsible for ensuring their privacy management solutions comply with the law and that this action underscores the critical importance of Californians' opt-out rights in protecting personal information.

# Details

## Background

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) Board issued a decision against national clothing retailer Todd Snyder, Inc., requiring the company to pay a fine and alter its business practices. The CPPA’s Enforcement Division alleged that Todd Snyder violated the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA). Specifically, the allegations included:

- **Failing to properly oversee and configure its privacy portal's technical infrastructure**, which resulted in a failure to process consumer requests to opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information for 40 days.
- **Requiring consumers to submit more information than necessary** to process their privacy requests.
- **Requiring consumers to verify their identity** before they could opt out of the sale or sharing of their personal information.

## Legal Analysis

The core of the legal matter revolves around Todd Snyder, Inc.'s alleged **violations of the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA)**. The CPPA's Enforcement Division identified three key areas of non-compliance:

- The company's failure to adequately manage and configure its privacy portal's technical infrastructure led to a **40-day delay in processing consumer requests to opt out** of the sale or sharing of their personal information.
- Todd Snyder was accused of **demanding more information than necessary** from consumers when they submitted privacy requests. #minimization 
- The retailer also **required consumers to verify their identity** before they could exercise their right to opt out of the sale or sharing of their personal information. The CPPA highlighted that these allegations regarding improper verification mirrored a previous Enforcement Advisory issued by the agency, which had warned businesses against collecting excessive information from consumers asserting their privacy rights.

Michael Macko, head of the CPPA’s Enforcement Division, stressed that **businesses are ultimately responsible for ensuring their privacy management solutions comply with the law** and work as intended, stating that "Using a consent management platform doesn’t get you off the hook for compliance". The Board's decision underscored the **importance of Californians’ opt-out rights**, emphasizing that these rights enable consumers to assert control over their personal information and protect themselves from potential harms arising from businesses collecting and sharing vast amounts of data, including sensitive categories such as reproductive health, immigration status, and financial condition. Tom Kemp, the CPPA’s Executive Director, further remarked that this decision serves as a significant reminder that the Enforcement Division actively scrutinizes how businesses honor Californians' privacy rights.

## Sanction

To resolve the allegations, Todd Snyder, Inc. agreed to pay a **fine of $345,178**. In addition to the monetary penalty, the company is required to implement changes to its business practices. These changes specifically include:

- **Properly configuring its mechanisms for submitting and managing opt-out preferences**.
- **Providing CCPA compliance training for its employees**.

The Board's decision became effective immediately on May 1st, 2025. This action is part of the CPPA's broader efforts to actively enforce California’s privacy laws. Other recent enforcement actions by the CPPA include a $632,500 fine against American Honda Motor Co. for CCPA violations, a settlement with data broker Background Alert requiring it to shut down, and an enforcement action against National Public Data, Inc., for a data breach. The CPPA has also been involved in launching the bipartisan Consortium of Privacy Regulators and partnering with international data protection authorities to advance privacy protections.
