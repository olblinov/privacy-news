# Summary

The French competition authority, Autorité de la concurrence (Adlc), sanctioned Apple €150 million on March 28, 2025, for abusing its dominant position in the mobile application distribution sector on iOS and iPadOS terminals. The sanction relates to the implementation of Apple's App Tracking Transparency (ATT) device, which, while aiming to protect user privacy, was found to have anti-competitive modalities that were neither necessary nor proportionate. The Adlc's decision, which considered opinions from the CNIL (Commission Nationale de l'Informatique et des Libertés), highlighted that ATT's implementation created excessive complexity for users of third-party applications, distorted the neutrality of the system, and particularly penalized smaller publishers, thus causing economic prejudice to app publishers and advertising service providers.

# Details

## Background

Since April 2021, app publishers seeking to track users for advertising purposes across multiple applications or websites have been required to obtain explicit user authorization through a partially standardized window designed by Apple, known as **App Tracking Transparency (ATT)**. This mechanism, introduced with iOS and iPadOS version 14.5, requests user consent for data collection by third-party applications within the Apple ecosystem before a newly downloaded app is used. If consent is granted, the app can access the Identifier for Advertisers (IDFA), which allows tracking across third-party applications and sites.

The Autorité de la concurrence (Adlc) was seized with a complaint by several professional associations in online advertising against Apple. These associations perceived the ATT solicitation as an obstacle to targeted advertising, which is a significant source of funding for app publishers and other online advertising stakeholders. Although the Adlc initially rejected a request for interim measures on March 17, 2021, it decided to proceed with a substantive investigation. In its analysis, the Adlc took into account two opinions provided by the CNIL, which helped clarify issues related to personal data protection.

## Legal Analysis

The Adlc acknowledged that the objective of the ATT device—to enhance user privacy protection—is **not subject to criticism in principle**. However, the Adlc found that the specific implementation modalities of ATT were **neither necessary nor proportionate** to Apple's stated goal of protecting personal data. The authority emphasized that while a dominant operator like Apple is free to set additional consumer protection rules, it must reconcile this legitimate objective with competition law. Disproportionate or objectively unjustified rules from a dominant platform operator can negatively affect market functioning and ultimately harm consumer interests.

The key issues identified by the Adlc were:

- **Artificial Complexity and Double Solicitation**: The Adlc found that ATT does not allow for the collection of valid consent under applicable law, such as the GDPR, which means publishers still need to use their own consent management platforms (CMPs). This results in a **multiplication of consent windows**, excessively complicating the user journey within the iOS environment for third-party applications. The CNIL had also observed this issue in a 2022 opinion. #consent 
- **Breach of Device Neutrality**: The rules governing the interaction between different displayed windows compromise the neutrality of the ATT device. For instance, refusing an advertising tracking operation only needs to be done once, but accepting such an operation always requires a second user confirmation. This **asymmetry** prevents informed consent, which ATT is supposed to promote. The Adlc noted that marginal modifications to the device, as recommended by the CNIL, could have avoided these issues. #consent #change 
- **Asymmetry of Treatment between Apple and Publishers**: The Adlc identified an asymmetry in how Apple treated its own data collection compared to that of third-party publishers. Previously, Apple did not require user consent for its own applications until iOS 15, while demanding double consent for third-party tracking. Even now, Apple uses a single "Personalized Advertising" window for its own data collection, but continues to require double consent for third-party data collection. This disparity had previously led the CNIL to sanction Apple for violating Article 82 of the French Data Protection Act. #consent 
- **Disproportionate Harm to Smaller Publishers**: The ATT device was found to be particularly detrimental to **smaller app publishers**. Unlike major integrated platforms like Meta and Google, which possess their own extensive data ecosystems and alternative targeting capabilities, smaller operators largely depend on third-party data collection to finance their activities. The loss of activity for smaller players contrasts with the ability of "actors with their own 'data ecosystem'" to thrive and strengthen their offerings without relying on IDFA or cookies.

The case further confirmed that **competition law and personal data protection law are not opposed but converge** towards a common goal of a responsible digital environment. The close cooperation between the CNIL and the Adlc, highlighted by their joint declaration in December 2023, aimed to ensure innovation while guaranteeing fundamental citizen rights and market competitive balance. The CNIL's advice showed that making ATT compliant with competition law would not have degraded its privacy protection effectiveness.

## Sanction

On March 28, 2025, the Adlc sanctioned Apple with a **fine of €150,000,000**. This penalty was imposed on Apple Distribution International Limited (ADI) and Apple Inc. as perpetrators, and Apple Operations International Limited and Apple Inc. as parent companies. The amount of the fine was determined considering the **gravity of the facts**, the **duration of the infringement** (between April 26, 2021, and July 25, 2023), and **Apple's economic power**. In addition to the monetary sanction, Apple is obligated to publish a summary of the decision on its website for seven consecutive days. The full decision is referenced as Decision No. 25-D-02 of March 31, 2025.
