# Summary

The President of the Personal Data Protection Office (Prezes UODO) in Poland has imposed administrative monetary penalties totaling **27,124,816 PLN on Poczta Polska (Polish Mail)** and **100,000 PLN on the Minister of Digital Affairs**. These fines were issued for the unlawful processing of personal data of approximately **30 million Polish citizens from the PESEL registry** in April and May 2020, during the attempted organization of so-called "envelope elections" for the President of Poland. The UODO determined that the data was shared and processed without a proper legal basis, violating fundamental principles of data processing under the GDPR, and threatening citizens' constitutional rights to privacy and personal data protection. The decision to impose these fines was strongly influenced by prior administrative court rulings that declared the Prime Minister's directive for the elections invalid and the Minister of Digital Affairs' data transfer action ineffective.

# Details

## Background

In the spring of 2020, amidst the COVID-19 pandemic, Polish authorities attempted to organize presidential elections exclusively by mail, referred to as "envelope elections". On **April 16, 2020**, the Prime Minister issued an administrative decision instructing Poczta Polska to prepare for these elections. Following this directive, on **April 20, 2020**, Poczta Polska requested the transfer of personal data for approximately **30 million Polish citizens** from the PESEL registry who would be of legal age and residing in Poland on the planned election day. The Minister of Digital Affairs approved this request and transferred the data, stored on a DVD, to Poczta Polska on **April 22, 2020**, where it was subsequently processed.

The data transferred included **PESEL numbers, first names, last names, current and last known permanent residence addresses, temporary residence addresses (with declared end dates), and information about current temporary departures from Poland**. These details pertained to all adult Polish citizens whose country of residence on May 10, 2020, was Poland. The elections ultimately did not take place on May 10, 2020, and the personal data was destroyed between **May 15 and 22, 2020**.

The disclosure of PESEL data without a legal basis quickly led to numerous citizen complaints to the UODO, though the previous UODO President initially deemed them unfounded. However, the Commissioner for Human Rights (Rzecznik Praw Obywatelskich, RPO) took action, filing complaints with the Provincial Administrative Court (WSA) in Warsaw. On **September 15, 2020**, the WSA ruled that the Prime Minister's decision was not only a **gross violation of law but also issued without legal basis**. This ruling was upheld by the Supreme Administrative Court (NSA) on **June 28, 2024**. Additionally, on **February 26, 2021**, the WSA found the Minister of Digital Affairs' action of transferring data to be **ineffective**, a judgment upheld by the NSA on **March 13, 2024**. These final court judgments in March and June 2024 definitively established that the sharing and processing of personal data for the "envelope elections" were unlawful. Given the systemic nature and large number of affected individuals, the UODO then initiated administrative proceedings ex officio to assess violations of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

## Legal Analysis

The UODO, as the competent supervisory authority for data protection in Poland, is tasked with monitoring and enforcing the application of the GDPR. The core of the violation was the **processing of personal data without any legal basis**, directly infringing Article 6(1) of the GDPR, and consequently violating the fundamental principle of **lawfulness, fairness, and transparency** as outlined in Article 5(1)(a) of the GDPR. The GDPR specifies a **closed list of conditions** under which data processing is lawful, and at least one must be met.

Both the Minister of Digital Affairs and Poczta Polska were determined to be **data administrators**. The UODO's analysis, crucially **bound by the administrative court rulings**, concluded that neither entity possessed a valid legal basis for the data processing. The courts found that:

- The Prime Minister's decision on April 16, 2020, was **invalid** because no statutory provision allowed for the conduct of presidential elections solely by mail at that time, nor did it grant Poczta Polska the competence to prepare such elections. The COVID-19 related legislation cited did not authorize such an alteration of election law.
- The Minister of Digital Affairs' action of transferring data on April 22, 2020, was **ineffective**. The Minister had a duty to verify if Poczta Polska had the legal competence to organize elections, which it did not. The special law (u.s.i.w.) cited by the Minister (Art. 99) did not grant such powers to Poczta Polska, and general provisions on PESEL data access were not applicable.

The UODO emphasized that the **unlawful sharing and processing of PESEL data threatened fundamental constitutional rights**. Article 47 of the Polish Constitution guarantees the right to privacy and personal life, while Article 51 ensures the protection of personal data. The lack of control over one's data due to unlawful processing caused legitimate fear and uncertainty among citizens. The PESEL number, being a unique identifier containing sensitive information, requires exceptional protection under Article 87 of the GDPR, and its unauthorized disclosure in conjunction with other data posed a high risk of negative consequences for individuals.

## Sanction

The UODO imposed administrative monetary penalties on both the Minister of Digital Affairs and Poczta Polska, exercising its power under Article 58(2)(i) of the GDPR.

- The **Minister of Digital Affairs was fined 100,000 PLN**. This amount represents the **maximum statutory penalty** for public sector entities in Poland. The UODO considered the violation to be of **very high gravity** and **exceptionally serious**, affecting fundamental data processing principles. Factors contributing to this severity include:
    
    - The **Minister's high responsibility** as custodian of the central PESEL register.
    - The **intentional nature of the violation**, as the Minister was aware of the legal ambiguities and the lack of proper legal basis at the time of data transfer.
    - The **large scale of the breach**, affecting nearly **30 million Polish citizens** (approximately 80% of the population).
    - The potential for **non-material harm** such as fear and uncertainty among affected individuals.
    - The **scope of data involved**, including PESEL numbers and addresses, which allow for immediate identification and carry risks when unlawfully disclosed.
    - The **negative societal impact**, leading to public concern and erosion of trust in state authorities.
    - Previous GDPR violations by the Ministry were also considered as an aggravating factor.
    - A **mitigating factor** was the Minister's request to Poczta Polska on May 12, 2020, to delete the data, which might have prompted the deletion process.
- **Poczta Polska was fined 27,124,816 PLN**. This fine was determined based on Article 83(5)(a) of the GDPR, which allows for fines up to 20,000,000 EUR or 4% of the enterprise's total annual worldwide turnover, whichever is higher. For Poczta Polska, 4% of its 2024 turnover (6,444,174 EUR equivalent) was chosen as the dynamic maximum. The UODO deemed Poczta Polska's violations as having **very high gravity and an exceptionally serious nature**, affecting fundamental data processing principles. Aggravating factors included:
    
    - Poczta Polska's status as a **state-owned company and universal postal service operator**, requiring the highest diligence in adhering to legal provisions, especially data protection laws.
    - The company's **awareness of the legal risks** associated with processing the data, as evidenced by its internal legal opinions recommending an analysis of the Prime Minister's decision's legality.
    - The **massive scale of the breach**, affecting approximately **30 million citizens**.
    - The potential for **non-material harm** to individuals.
    - A history of **previous GDPR violations** by Poczta Polska, indicating systemic issues.
    - The **scope of data involved**, including PESEL numbers and addresses.
    - **Mitigating factors** included the **relatively short duration** of data processing (April 22 - May 22, 2020) and the **prompt deletion of the data** by Poczta Polska without direct UODO intervention, though following the Minister's request.
    - The fine was **further adjusted downwards** (from an initially calculated higher amount) to ensure proportionality, considering Poczta Polska's **poor financial situation** (significant net losses in 2022 and 2023) and its role in providing essential public services. The UODO recognized that an excessively high fine could jeopardize the company's ongoing recovery efforts and impact public service delivery.

The UODO concluded that the imposed fines are **effective, proportionate, and dissuasive**, aiming to penalize the unlawful conduct and deter future similar violations.
