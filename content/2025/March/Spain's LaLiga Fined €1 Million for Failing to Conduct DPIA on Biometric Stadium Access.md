# Summary

The Spanish Agency for Data Protection (AEPD) imposed a €1 million fine on the Liga Nacional de Fútbol Profesional (LNFP), commonly known as LaLiga, for a serious infringement of Article 35 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). The AEPD found that LaLiga, as the data controller, failed to conduct a mandatory Data Protection Impact Assessment (EIPD) for its system requiring or facilitating the use of biometric data (primarily fingerprint scans) for access to "animation stands" (gradas de animación) in top-tier football stadiums. Despite LaLiga's arguments that the clubs were the true data controllers or that its actions were mandated public functions, the AEPD concluded that LaLiga's approval of the internal regulations establishing the biometric system, and its provision of the technology through its subsidiary SEFPSA, amounted to a determination of the purposes and means of processing special category data, thus incurring the obligation to perform a prior EIPD. In addition to the fine, the AEPD ordered a definitive suspension of the biometric access system until a valid EIPD is carried out.

# Details

## Background

The AEPD initiated an investigation following **complaints received on November 4, 2022, and March 22, 2023**. These complaints alleged that the **Liga Nacional de Fútbol Profesional (LNFP)**, through its General Regulations (RGLNFP), had implemented **biometric control mechanisms for access to football stadiums**, particularly for "animation stands". The complainants argued that this practice was an "excess" of data processing as defined by Article 5.1(c) of the GDPR and that simpler methods like nominal season tickets or DNI checks would suffice. They also highlighted that processing biometric data involves "special categories of personal data," which requires compliance with Article 9 of the GDPR.

As part of the investigation, the AEPD requested information from several entities:

- **LNFP (LaLiga):** The AEPD asked for all relevant rules, instructions, and recommendations concerning the processing of personal data for stadium access, especially special categories of data, and inquired about the justification for such processing under GDPR Article 9, as well as any proportionality assessments. LaLiga responded on March 10, 2023, stating that its **RGLNFP, specifically Article XII.2 ("conditions for the sale of season tickets in animation stands"), approved by the Superior Sports Council (CSD)**, mandated biometric recognition for these areas, but also indicated that alternative procedures were possible. LaLiga **denied being the data controller or processor** for this purpose, claiming it was not responsible for conducting impact assessments.
- **C.S.D. (Superior Sports Council):** The AEPD sought information from the CSD regarding any dispositions that mandated or recommended biometric systems for stadium access and any prior data protection impact assessments. The CSD's **State Commission against Violence, Racism, Xenophobia, and Intolerance in Sport (CEVRXID)** confirmed on March 28, 2023, that it had **adopted an agreement on March 3, 2022, establishing additional security measures, including biometric systems for unique identification at animation stands**, citing relevant anti-violence laws. However, the AEPD's Legal Cabinet, in its **Report 98/2022, advised that while identity verification was allowed, the law did not specifically cover biometric data processing or adequate guarantees, and a Data Protection Impact Assessment (EIPD) and proportionality judgment would be required**. Following this report, the **CEVRXID on March 13, 2023, agreed that biometric access would require explicit consent, or clubs must provide alternative identification procedures**, which then needed to be submitted to LaLiga for validation. The CSD indicated it had no knowledge of specific alternative procedures implemented by LaLiga's member clubs.
- **SOCIEDAD ESPAÑOLA DE FÚTBOL PROFESIONAL SAU (SEFPSA):** This **subsidiary of LaLiga** confirmed it supplied the software and hardware (turnstiles, servers) for biometric identification but **denied accessing personal data or acting as a data controller or processor**. SEFPSA stated that biometric patterns were stored on the clubs' servers, and clubs were responsible for their deletion.

LaLiga's main contention throughout the process was that it was not the data controller responsible for the biometric processing, as the RGLNFP was an administrative act approved by the CSD, and the actual processing was carried out by the individual clubs. LaLiga also argued that its non-profit status and the nature of its revenue (derived from managing audiovisual rights for clubs under a legal mandate) should mitigate any potential fine.

## Legal Analysis

The AEPD's legal analysis focused on two key aspects: **identifying the data controller** and **the obligation to conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (EIPD)**.

Regarding the **definition of personal and biometric data**, the AEPD explained that biometric data, such as fingerprints, are "special categories" of personal data and their processing is generally prohibited under Article 9.1 of the GDPR due to the significant risks they pose to fundamental rights and freedoms. The AEPD emphasized that any processing of such data requires a clear definition of purpose, justification of necessity, and adherence to the principle of proportionality, with less intrusive alternatives being preferred.

For the **identification of the data controller**, the AEPD adopted a **broad, functional interpretation** of the term, asserting that an entity is a data controller (Article 4.7 GDPR) if it **determines the "why" (purposes) and the "how" (means) of the data processing**, even if it does not have direct access to the data itself. The AEPD concluded that **LaLiga was indeed a data controller** based on the following:

- **LaLiga's RGLNFP established the regime:** LaLiga, through its RGLNFP (specifically Article XII.2), approved a singularized and exceptional regime for access to "animation stands," requiring the use of biometric data based on consent. This regulatory act **defined the purpose of the processing** (combating violence, racism, xenophobia, and intolerance in professional football) and **mandated the use of biometric systems**.
- **Influence over means:** LaLiga also influenced the "means" of processing by **developing and putting at the disposal of clubs a specific technological solution** (software and hardware for fingerprint recognition) through its subsidiary, SEFPSA. This, according to the AEPD, constitutes "designing" a treatment and is considered an operation of processing under a broad interpretation, even if the material collection of data occurs at the club level.
- **Rejection of LaLiga's arguments:** The AEPD rejected LaLiga's claim that the RGLNFP was a mere administrative act imposed by the CSD, clarifying that the CSD's approval was a "legality check" of an internal federative norm originating from LaLiga's own initiative. The AEPD also stated that even if clubs performed the "material" processing, this did not absolve LaLiga of its responsibility for defining the overall system and its parameters, suggesting a concept of "successive independent responsibilities" in a chain of processing operations.

Consequently, the **AEPD found that LaLiga, by determining these essential aspects, was obligated to conduct a Data Protection Impact Assessment (EIPD)** as per Article 35 of the GDPR. The AEPD reiterated that EIPDs are mandatory for processing involving new technologies, special categories of data, or large-scale systematic monitoring, all of which apply in this case. The AEPD noted that LaLiga failed to conduct such an assessment and that its system, initially approved in 2015, had not been updated to comply with the GDPR which became applicable in 2018. #dpia 

## Sanction

Initially, the AEPD proposed a fine of **€10,000,000** for the infringement of Article 35 of the GDPR. This figure was based on the maximum allowed under Article 83.4(a) GDPR, which can be up to €10,000,000 or 2% of the total global annual turnover of an "enterprise".

LaLiga lodged significant **allegations against the proposed fine**:

- **Disproportionate Calculation:** LaLiga argued that its non-profit status and the nature of its revenue should be considered. It asserted that a substantial portion of its turnover (over €1.8 billion in 2022/2023) came from managing audiovisual rights for its member clubs under a legal mandate (Real Decreto-Ley 5/2015), not from its own commercial activity, and therefore should be excluded from the fine calculation base. LaLiga suggested that its relevant operational income was closer to €50.9 million or €169.9 million (if considering the consolidated group's relevant activities). #fine 
- **Interpretation of "Enterprise" and Turnover:** LaLiga contested the AEPD's broad interpretation of "enterprise" from EU competition law (Articles 101 and 102 TFEU) for GDPR fine calculation, especially given a pending preliminary ruling from the CJEU (Case C-383/23) on this specific issue. #fine 
- **Aggravating Factors:** LaLiga disputed the AEPD's use of "lack of diligence" as an aggravating factor, arguing that the high risk associated with biometric data is inherent to the violation itself and that there was sufficient prior information (guidelines) which suggested consent was a valid basis for biometric processing.

The AEPD, while upholding its interpretation of "enterprise" and the applicability of the broad turnover concept for determining the _maximum_ fine, did **consider LaLiga's specific financial structure** and the nature of its audiovisual rights management. #change 

Ultimately, the AEPD's final resolution imposed a fine of **€1,000,000** on LaLiga. This reduced amount, from the initial €10,000,000 proposal, indicates that LaLiga's financial arguments regarding the calculation base were partially taken into account.

In addition to the fine, the AEPD issued a **definitive corrective measure**:

- It **ordered LaLiga to suspend definitively the processing of biometric data** for access to the "animation stands" of its affiliated clubs and SADs.
- This suspension will remain in place **until LaLiga conducts and successfully completes a valid Data Protection Impact Assessment (EIPD)**. This EIPD must specifically examine the necessity, suitability, and proportionality of the processing, considering the risks to the rights and freedoms of affected individuals, and propose appropriate technical and organizational measures and safeguards.
- LaLiga is required to **communicate this definitive suspension** to its member clubs.

The AEPD had previously issued a provisional suspension order on December 27, 2023, which was challenged by LaLiga but upheld by the National Court on April 2, 2024. LaLiga subsequently communicated this provisional suspension to its clubs in April 2024. The definitive order now formalizes this requirement.
