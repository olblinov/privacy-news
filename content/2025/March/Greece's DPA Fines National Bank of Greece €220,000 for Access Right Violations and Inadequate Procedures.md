# Summary

The Greek Data Protection Authority (DPA) investigated a significant number of complaints against the National Bank of Greece concerning violations of data subject access rights and found multiple instances of delayed or inadequate responses. The bank attributed delays to teleworking conditions, an increase in electronic fraud cases, and internal misdirection of requests, but the DPA concluded these were not valid excuses for non-compliance. The DPA determined that the bank's procedures for handling access requests were ineffective and that its internal policies were not properly applied in practice, leading to the imposition of a total fine of **€220,000** and a reprimand for various GDPR infringements.

# Details

## Background

Since early 2022, the Greek Data Protection Authority (DPA) received a significant number of complaints against the National Bank of Greece (Εθνική Τράπεζα) concerning violations of data subject access rights as per Article 15 of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR). These complaints primarily related to the bank's failure or substantial delay in satisfying these access requests.

The DPA specifically examined seven aggregated cases, mostly stemming from incidents of electronic fraud where customers requested personal data such as recorded phone conversations, i-bank activity logs, or transaction details.

Examples of the issues include:

- In one case (A), recorded telephone conversations were provided after a 7-month delay, and only after the data subject initiated a lawsuit. The bank used these personal data in court proceedings against the data subject before providing them access, leading to a court award of €1,000 for moral damages due to the violation of access rights.
- Another case (B) involved a 4.5-month delay in providing recorded conversations, despite repeated requests.
- A request for transaction information (C) took 74 days to be satisfied and was initially misclassified as a "complaint" instead of an access request.
- Further cases (D, E, ΣT) showed delays of 5 months, 4 months, and 6 months respectively for providing requested data, again with the bank citing teleworking arrangements and the volume of requests. In case D, the bank also failed to provide clear information about the recipients of the data.
- In case Z, the bank partially satisfied the request after 4 months and, notably, sent responses to the email address of the unknown perpetrator who had fraudulently altered the data subject's e-banking settings. When the data subject requested a specific call recording again, the bank failed to locate it but re-sent an old recording without informing the data subject that the requested data was not included.

The National Bank of Greece provided several explanations for the delays, including:

- Misdirection of requests internally.
- The large volume of requests due to a surge in electronic fraud incidents, which require extensive investigation.
- Difficulties in accessing specific infrastructure for creating copies of recorded conversations, especially due to teleworking arrangements for authorized personnel during the pandemic.
- The need for cooperation across multiple internal units and with other banks for fraud investigations.

The bank stated that it had adopted a Data Protection Policy in May 2018 (updated in May 2022) and issued an internal circular for GDPR compliance and staff guidance. It also claimed to have developed an internal procedure for managing GDPR requests in December 2023 and designed mandatory training seminars for its staff, although the DPA noted that the bank did not clarify if all staff had completed these trainings or at what point.

The DPA had previously imposed a fine of €20,000 on the bank in 2021 for a similar violation of Article 15 GDPR. In February 2023, the DPA also sent a letter to the bank, reminding it of its obligations to comply with Article 12(3) and 15 GDPR and to implement appropriate organizational and technical measures.

## Legal Analysis

The DPA, as the supervisory authority, is competent to oversee the application of the GDPR and investigate complaints regarding data access rights violations, as well as to conduct ex officio investigations into compliance with Articles 12, 15, and 24 of the GDPR.

Key GDPR principles and provisions relevant to the DPA's analysis include:

- **Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency (Article 5(1)(a) GDPR)** and **accountability (Article 5(2) GDPR)**, which require controllers to demonstrate compliance with these principles.
- **Right of Access (Article 15 GDPR):** Data subjects have the right to obtain confirmation of whether their personal data is being processed and to access that data. The controller must provide a copy of the data, and the purpose for which the data subject exercises this right is irrelevant; no preconditions can be imposed for its exercise. Access is crucial for processing transparency and is closely linked to other GDPR principles and rights. When data subjects request information about data recipients, especially after data transfer has occurred, the controller must name the specific recipients. #access 
- **Facilitating Rights and Response Time (Article 12 GDPR):** Controllers must facilitate the exercise of data subject rights without undue delay and, in any event, within one month of receiving the request. This period can be extended by two additional months for complex or numerous requests, but the data subject must be informed of the extension and the reasons for it within the initial one-month period. If a controller frequently needs to extend this deadline, it may indicate a need to improve its general procedures. #rights 
- **Controller's Responsibility (Article 24 GDPR) and Data Protection by Design (Article 25(1) GDPR):** Controllers must implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure and demonstrate compliance with the GDPR, considering the nature, scope, context, and purposes of processing, as well as the risks to data subjects' rights and freedoms. These measures must be reviewed and updated as necessary. It is particularly important for large-scale data processors to have effective procedures for handling data subject requests, especially when delays could cause harm. Internal policies, such as teleworking arrangements, should not undermine data subjects' rights.

The DPA found that the National Bank of Greece had violated Article 12(3) and (4) GDPR in all seven examined cases, and additionally Article 15 GDPR in cases 1, 4, and 7. The DPA concluded that the bank's procedures for handling access requests were ineffective. The bank's justifications, such as teleworking and the increase in fraud cases, were deemed insufficient to exempt it from its GDPR obligations.

Furthermore, the DPA noted that the bank's specific procedure for managing GDPR requests ("Διαχείριση Αιτημάτων Άσκησης Δικαιωμάτων GDPR") was only developed in December 2023, after the DPA initiated its ex officio investigation. The DPA also criticized the apparent lack of specific proposals or contributions from the bank's Data Protection Officer (DPO) in addressing the observed issues or updating policies. The DPA concluded that despite the theoretical existence of policies, the bank had not complied with Article 12(2) and 24(1) and (2) GDPR in practice since the GDPR's implementation in May 2018, especially given the volume and nature of personal data it processes and the potential harm from delayed access responses. The bank failed to identify specific causes for its non-compliance and only vaguely referred to future reviews of its procedures.

## Sanction

Based on the findings, the DPA decided to exercise its corrective powers under Article 58(2) of the GDPR.

The sanctions imposed were:

1. For the specific violation concerning data subject A (Case 1), a **fine of €20,000** was imposed for violating Article 15(1) and (3) GDPR. This fine was particularly effective, proportionate, and dissuasive because the bank had used the data subject's requested personal data (recorded telephone calls) in court proceedings against them before providing it, placing the data subject at a procedural disadvantage.
2. For the violations identified in the other six cases (B, C, D, E, ΣT, Z), the DPA issued a **reprimand** to the National Bank of Greece, pursuant to Article 58(2)(b) GDPR, for infringements of Article 12(3) and (4) GDPR (and Article 15 GDPR in cases D and Z). These cases were considered in the broader context of the bank's overall compliance.
3. For the overall systemic violation of Article 12(2) and Article 25(1) GDPR, relating to the lack of appropriate and effective procedures for handling data access requests, a **fine of €200,000** was imposed.

In determining the amount of the €200,000 fine, the DPA considered several criteria under Article 83(2) GDPR, including:

- The **large number of affected data subjects**, potentially including all of the bank's customers and third parties.
- The **long duration of the violation**, which spans from the GDPR's entry into force on May 25, 2018.
- The fact that the violation concerned the **core activities** of the data controller.
- The **bank's prior sanction** for a similar violation of access rights.
- The bank's apparent **indifference** to the timely satisfaction of access rights for a significant period, attributing delays to pandemic conditions, and only initiating the development of relevant policies after the DPA's intervention.
- The bank's turnover (gross operating income) for the fiscal year 2023, which amounted to approximately €2.6 billion.
