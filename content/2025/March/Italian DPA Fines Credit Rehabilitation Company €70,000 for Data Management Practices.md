# Summary

Italy's Garante per la protezione dei dati personali (Data Protection Authority) has **issued a €70,000 fine and corrective measures** against Studio Riabilitazione Creditizia s.r.l.s., a credit rehabilitation company, following an inspection that revealed multiple data protection violations. The violations include breaches of principles related to **lawfulness, fairness, transparency, and storage limitation** regarding client data. The company also failed to adequately **define roles and contracts with data processors** and improperly designated its legal representative as the Data Protection Officer (DPO) #change #dpo . Furthermore, Studio Riabilitazione Creditizia s.r.l.s. was found to have generally inadequate technical and organizational measures, demonstrating a **lack of accountability**, and failed to respond to requests for information from the authority.

# Details

## Background

The inspection into Studio Riabilitazione Creditizia s.r.l.s. was initiated on June 13, 2023, following a **report from the Bank of Italy**. The Bank of Italy noted that the company's legal representative, Mr. XX, had made numerous access requests to the Central Credit Register on behalf of individuals without proper legitimacy, raising concerns about the **improper acquisition and use of financial personal data**.

Studio Riabilitazione Creditizia s.r.l.s. primarily **focuses on removing negative credit reports** from credit bureaus. Clients typically find the company through its website, www.studioucp.it, which provides information on services offered. Upon initial phone contact, clients receive a pre-recorded data processing notice. If they accept, the operator collects personal data (name, surname, address, email) into a Customer Relationship Management (CRM) database. This CRM also stores further data provided by the client, including signed forms. Once delegated by clients, the company requests access to public and private credit bureaus (such as Banca d'Italia and the Chamber of Commerce) to assess the client's debt situation and feasibility of data deletion or limitation. If no grounds for deletion are found, a questionnaire is sent for more details. If feasible, a quote is sent, and upon acceptance, contractual documentation is dispatched for signature, initiating the credit rehabilitation process.

During the inspection, it was found that the company's CRM contained data for **74,214 clients**, including personal and contact details, an identifying number, status, and payment information. It also included reports from banks in response to client access requests. Access to the CRM was restricted to the company's premises via username and password, with maintenance performed by an external system administrator. Critically, the CRM had also **accumulated data from several predecessor companies** (Ufficio Cattivi Pagatori srl, UCP srl, Centro Realizzazioni Informatiche e Finanziarie, Insurance Global Service srl), all managed by Mr. XX. Despite Mr. XX being the sole administrator for all these companies and having obtained consent for each, the CRM lacked a feature to trace which specific company collected a client's data. It was also discovered that the CRM contained data of individuals who had only inquired about services but did not become clients. The company confirmed it had not systematically deleted old data, retaining full paper and digital records even for those with whom no current contractual relationship existed.

## Legal Analysis

The Garante Privacy found Studio Riabilitazione Creditizia s.r.l.s. in violation of several articles of the GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) and Article 157 of the Italian Data Protection Code.

Specifically, the violations included:

- **Article 5, paragraph 1, letter a) (Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency) and Article 14 (Information where personal data have not been obtained from the data subject) of the Regulation**: The company failed to inform individuals about the transfer of their personal data from previous entities to Studio Riabilitazione Creditizia s.r.l.s. when the data was not directly collected from the data subject. The CRM did not allow identification of which entity initially collected a client's data, violating the principle of transparency. 
- **Article 5, paragraph 1, letter e) (Storage limitation) of the Regulation**: Despite stating in its privacy policy that data would be kept no longer than necessary, the company did not have clear data retention periods and had not deleted personal data after contract cessation, particularly for individuals who only inquired about services without becoming clients. This violates the principle that data should be kept only for the time necessary to achieve the processing purposes.
- **Article 28 (Processor) of the Regulation**: The company engaged other entities, both individuals and legal persons, to process data on its behalf (e.g., "Centro Realizzazioni informatiche e finanziarie S.r.l.s.," "Ufficio Cattivi Pagatori S.r.l.," and external legal/fiscal consultants). However, these relationships were not properly regulated by a contract or other binding legal act as required by Article 28, paragraph 3, which specifies the need to define the subject matter, duration, nature, purpose, data types, categories of data subjects, and the obligations and rights of the data controller. #vendor_management 
- **Articles 37 and 38 (Designation and position of the Data Protection Officer) of the Regulation**: Studio Riabilitazione Creditizia s.r.l.s. designated Mr. XX, its legal representative, as the DPO. The Garante found this role **incompatible with that of the legal representative**, as the DPO must operate independently and without receiving instructions regarding their tasks. The company also failed to **communicate the DPO's contact details to the Authority** as mandated by Article 37, paragraph 7. #dpo #change 
- **Articles 5, paragraph 2 and 24 (Accountability) of the Regulation**: The combined violations indicated that the technical and organizational measures adopted by the company were inadequate, demonstrating a **lack of overall responsibility and compliance** with the accountability principle.
- **Article 157 of the Code (Failure to respond to requests)**: The company failed to respond to a request for information from the Authority, necessitating the involvement of the financial police and causing procedural delays and costs.

The Authority deemed the violations to be **serious**, considering their nature and gravity, as they impacted general principles of data processing, the data controller's responsibility, and the proper designation of the DPO. The **large number of affected data subjects (approximately 74,000)** also contributed to the high severity assessment.

## Sanction

The Garante per la protezione dei dati personali determined that the treatment of personal data by Studio Riabilitazione Creditizia s.r.l.s. was unlawful. The Authority **ordered the company to pay an administrative fine of €70,000**. This amount was determined considering factors such as the nature, gravity, and duration of the violations (concerning general principles like lawfulness, transparency, and storage limitation, as well as accountability), the culpable conduct of the company, the significant number of affected individuals, and the company's limited cooperation with the Authority. The absence of specific prior offenses was considered a mitigating factor.

In addition to the fine, the Garante prescribed the following **corrective measures** to be implemented within 90 days of notification:

- **Establish a data retention procedure** that defines terms for data conservation in relation to processing purposes and criteria for deletion.
- **Delete personal data of clients whose retention is no longer necessary**, particularly for those who only contacted the company but did not use its services.
- **Delete all personal data for which retention terms have expired**.
- **Properly regulate relationships with data processors** by adopting suitable contracts or other binding legal acts in compliance with Article 28, paragraph 3 of the Regulation.
- If the company intends to designate a Data Protection Officer, it must **appoint a suitable individual** who meets the requirements of Article 37, paragraph 5, and respects Articles 38 and 39 of the Regulation.

The Authority also mandated the **publication of the enforcement order** on its website due to the nature and numerous violations affecting thousands of individuals. The company has 30 days to pay the fine or appeal the decision.
