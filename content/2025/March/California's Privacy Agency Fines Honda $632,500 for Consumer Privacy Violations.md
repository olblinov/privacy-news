# Summary

The California Privacy Protection Agency (CPPA) has mandated that American Honda Motor Co. alter its business practices and pay a **$632,500 fine** to resolve claims that the company violated the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA). The investigation revealed that Honda unlawfully required consumers to provide excessive personal information for certain privacy rights, failed to offer privacy choices symmetrically in its online tools, made it difficult for authorized agents to exercise privacy rights, and shared consumers’ personal information with ad tech companies without the necessary protective contracts. To resolve these issues, Honda has agreed to implement a simpler process for privacy requests, consult a user experience (UX) designer, train employees, and modify its contracting process. The resolution reflects Honda's cooperation in making changes.

# Details

## Background

The California Privacy Protection Agency's (CPPA) Enforcement Division initiated an investigation into American Honda Motor Co., Inc. on July 31, 2023, as part of its ongoing review of data privacy practices by connected vehicle manufacturers and related technologies. American Honda Motor Co., Inc., the North American subsidiary of Japan-incorporated Honda Motor Co., Ltd., has a significant presence in California, distributing and selling Honda and Acura brand automobiles, motorcycles, and scooters since 1959. Honda is subject to the CCPA because it is a for-profit corporation that collects, uses, sells, and shares consumers' personal information, with annual gross revenue exceeding $25 million and annually selling or sharing the personal information of 100,000 or more consumers or households. The relevant period for the investigation was January 1, 2023, through November 20, 2024.

## Legal Analysis

The CPPA’s Enforcement Division alleged that Honda violated Californians’ privacy rights in several key areas under the CCPA:

- **Excessive Information for Privacy Rights:** Honda unlawfully required consumers to provide more personal information than necessary, and often to **"Verify" themselves**, when exercising certain privacy rights, such as the **right to opt-out of sale or sharing** and the **right to limit** the use of their sensitive personal information. Unlike "Requests to Delete," "Requests to Correct," or "Requests to Know," these specific requests do not require verification under the CCPA, as the potential harm from an imposter is minimal. Honda used a single webform for all request types, mandating at least eight data fields (name, full address, email, phone number) regardless of whether verification was required. From July 1, 2023, to September 23, 2023, **at least 119 consumers** were required to provide excessive information, and **at least 20 requests were denied** due to this unlawful verification requirement. #rights #change 
- **Non-Symmetrical Online Privacy Choices:** Honda's online privacy management tool, which allowed consumers to opt-out of cross-context behavioral advertising through cookie management, **failed to offer privacy choices in a symmetrical or equal way**. Advertising cookies were "allowed" or "active" by default . While consumers could "Accept All" cookies with a single click, opting out required a minimum of two clicks (toggling off and then confirming choices). Additionally, after opting out, an "Allow All" button appeared, making it easier to opt back in with one click . The CCPA mandates that methods for submitting requests must provide symmetry in choice, meaning the path to a privacy-protective option should not be longer or more difficult than a less privacy-protective option. #cookies #consent #change 
	- **Cookie Management Tool:** Honda utilized a cookie management tool provided by OneTrust, a third-party compliance vendor, on its Honda and Acura websites. This tool allowed consumers to opt-out of advertising and other tracking technologies used for **Cross-context Behavioral Advertising**.
	- **Default Settings:** Within this tool, various types of cookies, including performance, functional, and advertising cookies, were **"allowed" or "active" by default**. Honda informed consumers that Advertising Cookies "may be used to build a profile of your interests and show you advertisements on other sites".
	- **Opt-Out Process:** To disable or "turn off" Advertising Cookies, consumers were required to take **two steps**: first, click the toggle button next to Advertising Cookies, and second, click the "Confirm My Choices" button.
	- **Opt-In Process:** Conversely, if consumers returned to the cookie management tool after opting out, an **"Allow All" button** appeared. To opt back into Advertising Cookies, consumers only needed to take **one step** by clicking this "Allow All" button, which would then close the form.
	- **Violation of Symmetry Requirement:** The CCPA regulations mandate that methods for submitting privacy requests must be easy to understand, **provide symmetry in choice**, avoid confusing language or interactive elements, and avoid choice architecture that impairs or interferes with a consumer's ability to make a choice. **Symmetry in choice specifically means that the path to exercise a more privacy-protective option cannot be longer or more difficult or time-consuming than the path to exercise a less privacy-protective option**. A choice is not symmetrical if, for example, a business's opt-out process requires more steps than its process for a consumer to opt-in after having opted out. An example of a symmetrical choice would be "Accept All" and "Decline All" options on a website banner, ensuring equal steps for both privacy-protective and less privacy-protective choices.
	- **Honda's Failure:** Honda's cookie management tool was deemed a method through which consumers could submit their Request to Opt-Out of Sale/Sharing. The tool failed to provide symmetrical choice because consumers could "Accept All" cookies with a single click, but **opting out required at least two clicks**.
	- As a remedy, Honda is required to **add a "Reject All" button** to its cookie management tool to ensure symmetrical choice with its "Allow All" button. Additionally, the link to manage cookie preferences must be included in its Privacy Center, Privacy Policy, and privacy policy webpage footers.
- **Difficulty for Authorized Agents:** Honda made it difficult for Californians to authorize other individuals or organizations (known as "authorized agents") to exercise their privacy rights. Specifically, for **"Requests to Opt-Out of Sale/Sharing" and "Requests to Limit"** submitted by an authorized agent, Honda unlawfully required the consumer to **directly confirm** with Honda that they had given the agent permission. The CCPA prohibits businesses from requiring direct confirmation from the consumer for these non-verifiable requests when submitted by an authorized agent. From July 1, 2023, to September 23, 2023, **at least 14 consumers** were subjected to this unlawful requirement.
- **Sharing Without Proper Contracts:** Honda was found to be sharing consumers' personal information with advertising technology companies for cross-context behavioral advertising purposes but could not produce contracts with these companies that contained the necessary terms to protect privacy, as required by the CCPA. The CCPA mandates agreements that specify limited purposes for data use, restrict the recipient's use, and require compliance with the same level of privacy protection as mandated for businesses. This failure unnecessarily placed consumers' personal information at risk. #vendor_management 

## Sanction

To resolve the allegations, American Honda Motor Co. has agreed to pay an administrative **fine of $632,500**. This fine includes **$382,500** specifically allocated for the violations affecting consumers: $382,500 for the 119 consumers who were required to provide excessive information for opt-out/limit requests, the 20 consumers whose opt-out/limit requests were denied due to unlawful verification, and the 14 consumers who were required to directly confirm authorized agent permission. The CCPA allows the agency to impose fines of up to $2,500 for each violation, or $7,500 for each intentional violation, with an inflation adjustment, on a per-violation basis.

In addition to the monetary penalty, Honda is required to implement several significant changes to its business practices to ensure CCPA compliance:

- **Streamlined Privacy Request Process:** Honda must establish a new, simpler process for Californians to assert their privacy rights. This includes:
    - Only requiring information **necessary** to process "Requests to Opt-Out of Sale/Sharing" and "Requests to Limit" [39, 77a].
    - No longer requiring consumers to directly confirm an authorized agent's permission for "Requests to Opt-Out of Sale/Sharing" or "Requests to Limit" [39, 77b].
    - Separating the methods for submitting "Requests to Opt-Out of Sale/Sharing" and "Requests to Limit" from "Verifiable Consumer Requests" on its online platforms [39, 77c1].
    - Modifying the authorized agent process so agents can provide their own contact information alongside the consumer's [40, 77c2].
- **Symmetrical Cookie Management:** Within its cookie management tool, Honda must add a **"Reject All" button** to ensure symmetrical choice with its "Allow All" button [40, 77c4]. The link to manage cookie preferences must also be included in its Privacy Center, Privacy Policy, and privacy policy webpage footers [40, 77c3].
- **Global Privacy Control:** Honda is required to apply the Global Privacy Control to known consumers [41, 77c5].
- **User Experience (UX) Consultation:** Honda must consult a UX designer to evaluate its methods for submitting CCPA requests, ensuring they are easy to use and avoid confusing elements. Honda must certify that it received recommendations and inform the CPPA of its implementation timeline.
- **Employee Training:** Honda must train all personnel handling CCPA Requests on relevant CCPA requirements and confirm this training in writing within 90 days.
- **Contract Management:** Honda must modify its contract management and tracking processes to ensure that all required contractual terms are in place with all external recipients of personal information, confirming this in writing within 180 days.
- **Annual Metrics Posting:** For five years, and as required by CCPA thereafter, Honda must annually post specific CCPA metrics on its website.

Honda has agreed to complete the implementation of changes related to privacy request processes within 90 days, and changes to cookie management, Global Privacy Control, UX consultation, training confirmation, and contract modifications within 180 days of the order's effective date . Honda admits the truth of specific factual findings but neither admits nor denies other factual findings or liability for CCPA violations. Honda has waived its right to any hearings or appeals regarding this decision. The Agency noted Honda's early cooperation and commitment to rectify the issues.
